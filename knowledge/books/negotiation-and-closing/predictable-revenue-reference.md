# Predictable Revenue — Framework Reference

*Pure framework. No company-specific application. Pull from here when building skills, designing outbound/prospecting systems, or advising clients on pipeline generation.*
*Book: "Predictable Revenue: Turn Your Business Into a Sales Machine" — Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler*

---

## 1. Cold Calling 2.0

The core method Ross built at Salesforce: prospecting into cold accounts to generate new business without making cold calls.

Two parts to the definition:
- **No cold calls.** Outreach happens through targeted, short emails instead of dialing.
- **A system, not a one-off push.** A repeatable process that generates pipeline predictably, so the organization knows how X effort produces Y results.

---

## 2. Why short emails beat long ones

Response rates for short, mobile-readable emails consistently beat long sales pitches.

- The email doesn't sell. It asks for a referral to the right person.
- Sent to high-level executives, asking them to point you to the best person in their org for a first conversation.
- Short + specific ask outperforms a long pitch nobody reads.

Rule of thumb for any outbound email: readable on a phone, honest, to the point, one clear ask.

---

## 3. Results over activity

Pipeline engines get managed on outcomes, not busywork.

Don't build the system around tracking:
- Calls per day
- Calls per week
- Qualified opportunities per month, treated as an activity quota rather than a result

Calls/dials only get tracked during initial training, for coaching purposes, not as an ongoing performance metric. Once a rep is ramped, everything (management, coaching, hiring) is driven by proven results, not logged activity.

---

## 4. Cold Calling 2.0 tenets

- Dedicated prospecting team, separate from closing reps
- Determine mutual fit before pushing for a meeting
- Measure results, not activity
- Qualify leads before they enter the pipeline
- Research calls, not pitch calls, when opening a new account
- Authentic technique: integrity over scripts and pressure
- Prospecting treated as a skill being built, not a grind
- Short, sweet emails
- The system serves rep productivity, not just management visibility

---

## 5. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

Spend real time defining who to target before building the list.

- Study the top 5-10% of existing customers.
- Define what makes those companies similar (size, industry, use case, etc.).
- Identify which profile is likely to generate the most revenue.
- Build a tight, focused target list from that criteria, not a broad net.

---

## 6. Research calls

A research call has a different goal than a sales call. It's not to get the decision-maker on the phone, it's to learn about the company and whether there's a potential fit at all.

- Not a pitch. Not a close attempt.
- Objective: understand the account, confirm or rule out fit.
- Feeds the referral-request email process, doesn't replace it.

---

## 7. Measurement

- Avoid long sales emails. Nobody reads them.
- Keep outreach short, mobile-friendly, honest, specific about the ask.
- Use dashboards to track what actually matters (results, not activity) so the team can see what's working and adjust.

---

## 8. Prerequisites before building the system

Cold Calling 2.0 works best when these are already true:

- At least one person 100% dedicated to prospecting (the SDR, sales development rep).
- A CRM where the team shares client and deal info.
- Prospects reachable by email.
- A proven product or service.
- Customer lifetime value above $10,000. Works below that, but harder to make profitable. The higher the LTV, the better the fit.

Treat all of this as guidance, not a rigid template. Experiment and adapt to your own setup.

---

## 9. Core roles: SDR vs. account executive

The system runs on specialization, not generalist reps.

- **Sales development rep (SDR):** generates new opportunities. Prospects into cold or inactive accounts.
- **Account executive (AE):** closes business. Re-qualifies and works opportunities the SDR passes over.

One rep switching between prospecting and closing mindsets in the same day loses efficiency. Splitting the roles is what makes both faster.

Ratio: one SDR can typically support 2 to 5 AEs. The larger the deal size, the smaller the ratio.

---

## 10. Specialization at higher volume

Only relevant once lead volume justifies it (bigger companies), but the full model has four roles:

- **Outbound SDR:** prospects into cold/inactive accounts, sources net-new opportunities, passes to AEs.
- **Market response rep (inbound):** qualifies inbound leads (website, phone, marketing programs, paid ads).
- **Account executive:** closes.
- Rule of thumb: one market response rep per 400 inbound leads/month needing human attention.

Adding a dedicated SDR function measurably improves outbound results and frees AEs to focus on higher-potential accounts and relationship-building instead of qualification grind.

---

## 11. The CRM as the source of predictability

Tool choice matters less than consistent use. Everyone in the company needs to use it the same way.

Predictable revenue comes from predictable lead generation, which comes from a consistent, trackable process, not from any specific software.

---

## 12. Revenue predictability formula

**Revenue predictability = funnel + average deal size + time (rep ramp-up)**

Ramp time varies by company, driven by:
- Lead flow
- Who you hire
- How well you train them
- Whether they inherit an established territory or start cold

Advice: before active sales duty, run new reps through a stint elsewhere in the company talking to customers first. Ramps them faster and makes them more effective long-term.

**Two cycle lengths to track separately:**
- **Prospecting cycle length:** from first prospect response to a quality opportunity being created (i.e. the AE has re-qualified and accepted what the SDR passed over).
- **Sales cycle length:** from opportunity created/qualified to closed.

**Worked example:**
- New prospector ramps to full quota in 2 months.
- Each rep produces 10 new qualified opportunities/month.
- Deal size: $100,000.
- Result: after 2 months, one outbound rep produces $1M in new qualified pipeline/month.
- Win rate: 20%. Sales cycle: ~6 months average.

Once you have these numbers, use them as the baseline to forecast future performance.

---

## 13. The Cold Calling 2.0 process (5 steps)

1. **Get clear on your ICP.** Most companies fail here first: wrong prospects, wrong seniority, too many company types, wrong language.
2. **Build your list.** Resist dumping in random prospects just because you have them. Bad-fit responses waste time and clutter the database. There's a real opportunity cost to targeting poor fits.
3. **Run outbound email campaigns.** Mass emails/voicemails to ICP-fit prospects, written to look like one person wrote it. Goal: 5-10 new responses per rep per day.
4. **Sell the dream.** SDRs aren't appointment-setting machines. Train them to pitch the vision, build trust, credibility, and rapport, not just book meetings nobody shows up to.
5. **Pass the baton.** A clean, simple handoff process from SDR to AE. This has to be smooth or opportunities leak.

---

## 14. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) — SMART targeting

The single highest-leverage exercise for sales and marketing productivity: getting ICP definition right. It lets you disqualify bad fits fast and find good fits faster.

**SMART targeting sequence:**
1. Ideal industry
2. Ideal company
3. Research functional teams
4. Find influencers
5. Business issues
6. Fit

**Defining core challenges** — ask these of the prospect (or research to answer them):
- What are your greatest challenges?
- What keeps you up at night?
- What are your main frustrations?
- What are you afraid of?
- What do they really want?
- What's important to them?
- What do they spend money on?

Cap at 1-5 ICP profiles. More than that means the strategy needs tightening, not broadening.

---

## 15. Building the target list

- Use existing CRM-integrated data services or research your own sources.
- List-building tools/data are the fastest-changing part of outbound. Don't assume last year's source is still the best one, check.
- Know where your specific leads actually live before you commit to a data source.

---

## 16. Running outbound email campaigns

**The two-step motion:**
1. Email to generate an internal referral to the right person at the target company.
2. Follow up on responses/referrals by phone.

**Volume:**
- 50-100 targeted mass emails per rep per day.
- Target: 5-10 responses/day (roughly a 10% response rate).
- Filter the list tightly: vertical, industry type, revenue, geography/territory, employee count, business model, last contact/account activity date, contact title, or any other tracked field.

**Email guidelines:**
- Looks like a single email from one salesperson, not a mail-merge blast.
- Text-based.
- States plainly why you're reaching out.
- Mobile-readable, easy to respond to on a phone.
- Offers credibility.
- Asks exactly one simple question.

**Sending cadence:**
- 150-250 outbound emails/week, spread across 3-4 days.
- Send before 9am or after 5pm.
- Avoid Mondays. Fridays and Sundays are fine.
- Goal stays constant: 5-10 responses/day.

**The single next-step ask** — every mass email should drive toward exactly one of these two, never both:
1. Who's the best point of contact for a referral?
2. What's the best day/time for a quick discussion about setting up a conversation with the prospect?

**No response isn't a dead end:**
- Check email opens. Opened more than once with no reply → call them.
- Old, dead opportunities (6+ months no activity) are a good training ground for new SDRs and a real source of reopened pipeline.

---

## 17. Response handling and qualification

- Log every response in the CRM. Nothing falls through the cracks.
- Don't ignore bounces, clean bad emails out as they surface or they'll fog up the whole database.
- SDRs should pass fewer, better opportunities rather than flooding AEs with weak ones.

**First-call objective:** get the prospect talking about their business, then listen. Once they've talked, dig into their challenge/pain. Keep digging until there's nothing left. Build trust, be respectful, be persistent.

**Qualification checklist before an SDR passes an opportunity to an AE:**
- Does the company fit the ICP?
- Are we talking to someone with influence or power?
- Is there clear interest in a next step?

**Handoff mechanics** — pick one:
- Hot transfer (live, in the moment)
- Scheduled time on the AE's calendar
- Email introduction with the SDR CC'd (so they get credit)

**Quality control:** an opportunity only becomes "qualified" after the AE has personally re-asked the key questions and upgraded the stage. The SDR's pass is provisional, not final. Run an audit process on this.

---

## 18. Call effectiveness without scripts

Before any sales call, define:
- What answers do you want to learn?
- What attitude do you want the prospect to feel?
- What actions should happen after the call?

**Call flow (order matters for ease and productivity):**
1. Opening: "Did I catch you at a bad time?" plus a short intro.
2. Discuss the prospect's current business situation.
3. Curiosity.
4. Probe for needs, confirm you understood them.
5. Position the solution against those specific needs.
6. Handle objections.
7. Give next steps.

**Voicemail:** speak slowly and clearly. State name and number twice, once near the start, once at the end.

---

## 19. Account/lead status stages

- **Cold lead:** no meaningful activity, unresponsive, untouched.
- **Working lead:** conversations and qualification activity underway.
- **Nurturing lead:** active opportunity, or a check-back-quarterly follow-up.
- **Dead opportunity:** was active, now stalled/lost.
- **Bad fit:** disqualified.

Customize the labels to your own language, but keep the stages distinct and consistently applied.

---

## 20. SDR compensation

- Base salary + commission.
- Commission targeted at ~50% of base (roughly one-third of total comp).
- Paid monthly, in two parts:
  - 50% tied to a monthly qualified-opportunity quota.
  - 50% tied to closed deals (percentage of revenue).
- Mindset: the SDR's customers are the AEs they support. Make the AE successful and the SDR succeeds.

---

## 21. SDR training and ramp plan

**Weeks 1-2:** general company training, product training, services, before any SDR-specific training starts.

**Daily during ramp:**
- Configure CRM.
- Sit with an SDR and salesperson daily.
- Add an account and contact from source data.
- Learn to dedupe accounts.
- Learn to check new leads against what's already in the system.
- Send a mass outbound email to 20-50 contacts.
- Transition the prior SDR's territory.

**Weeks 4-5:**
- Send 100 outbound emails.
- Practice logging and responding to emails correctly.
- Work up to 5 call conversations/day.
- Sit with a veteran SDR daily.
- Draft a personal dashboard.
- Cover a new section of training material with the team each day.

---

## 22. Structuring the SDR's day

Design the day on purpose, don't let it default to whatever's loudest.

- **Morning:** respond to new and past leads.
- **Afternoon:** calls, and prep for what's next.
- Before either: prioritize the day's key goals first, three goals, set deliberately, not reactive.
- Take a break every 90 minutes with coworkers. Sustains energy and focus over the full day, not just the first two hours.

---

## 23. Six prospecting mistakes reps make

1. Expecting instant results. A new qualified opportunity can take 2-4+ weeks to develop.
2. Writing long emails.
3. Going wide instead of deep (spreading thin across too many accounts instead of working good-fit ones properly).
4. Giving up too quickly on ideal targets.
5. Not giving up quickly enough on non-ideal targets.
6. Depending on activity metrics instead of a proven process.

---

## 24. Best questions and quick prospecting tricks

**Opening/discovery questions:**
- "Did I catch you at a bad time? May I ask how your sales team, marketing organization, and research efforts are set up?"
- "If you were me, how would you approach your organization?"
- "Do you have your calendar handy?"

**Tricks:**
- **Call low, email high.** Call a lower-level contact to learn about the org, or email a high-level contact to get referred down to the right person. Don't go straight at the target.
- **Attitude:** position yourself as a non-threatening researcher, not a pushy salesperson.
- **Bite-sized emails:** short and sweet, one question per email, never more.
- **If they're not interested, find out why.** The reason is more valuable than the no.
- **Don't give up easily.** Persist until you get a clear, explicit no.
- **Always leave with a next step set.**

---

## 25. Selling to success

The core sales philosophy: hire and train salespeople who are genuinely committed to the company's vision and values, then help prospects connect to that vision, whether or not they end up a customer.

- Applies even to prospects who aren't a good long-term fit, tell them straight and point them elsewhere if needed.
- The rep's job is to connect the prospect's goals to what the company can actually deliver, not to manufacture urgency.

---

## 26. Why closing feels artificial

Closing gets a bad reputation because of how it's typically managed, not because closing itself is wrong.

- Reps get paid to close, so they get pressured, and fear is the default management lever.
- Pressure tactics generate short-term results at the cost of long-term ones. The math rarely works in the seller's favor once you account for churn, referrals lost, and reputation.
- Ask directly: is today's pressure-driven win worth what it costs you down the line?

---

## 27. What customers actually care about

Customers care about one of two things: improving their business, or improving themselves. Every pitch, objection-handle, and close should map back to one of these two.

---

## 28. The customer success plan

Sell past the close. Success isn't "service launched," it's "service produced the outcome the customer wanted."

- A success plan pulls the prospect through the buying cycle instead of the rep pushing them through the sales cycle.
- It ties the customer's own goals to the company's ability to deliver them, so the close becomes a logical next step, not a separate battle.
- **The trick: don't care too much about the close itself.** Over-caring leaks through as "I care about getting paid," not "I care about your outcome," and prospects feel that. Build the joint vision first, let the close fall out of it naturally.

**Building it in practice:**
1. Include a simple success plan step before the close: a definition of what success means to the client, a few key milestones, and named responsibilities on both sides. A half-dozen bullet points in an email, mutually agreed, is enough. The clearer the client's own picture of success, the more they pull the deal forward themselves.
2. Have an explicit answer, before you ever pitch, for "what is our plan for this client's ongoing success after the sale." Happy customers are what keep the business fed.

---

## 29. Nine ways to shorten the sales cycle

1. Wrong prospects, poor messaging. Sharpen the ICP before blaming the pitch.
2. No sales process. Or a good process that nobody actually follows.
3. Selling selfishly (pushing your product) instead of solving the prospect's actual problem.
4. Selling too low in the org.
5. Not identifying who holds real influence and approval power, early.
6. Poor understanding of the prospect's internal buying process.
7. Not genuinely caring about the prospect's outcome.
8. Telling instead of showing. Give something valuable away for free to demonstrate rather than describe.
9. Dragging your feet on disqualifying bad fits. Comes back to having (and using) a real ICP.

---

## 30. Mapping the buyer's decision-making process

The decision-making process matters more than the decision-maker. Map it before you push for a meeting with whoever's at the top.

**Questions to ask:**
- How have you evaluated similar products or services before?
- What is the decision-making process here?
- How are you personally involved in it?

**Sequencing the relationship:**
- Don't avoid the decision-maker, but don't need to reach them on day one either.
- Build credibility with influencers first. Win them over before you try to sell the actual decision-maker.
- Goal in early stages: map the internal buying process, get access to decision-makers, and start building a shared vision, in that order.

---

## 31. The three-step qualification sequence

Three steps, ~3 hours 15 minutes of total invested time, to reach mutual clarity on fit and timing:

1. **First contact (~15 min): is this a waste of time?** Set expectations immediately. Lay out your process for the prospect in a way that's framed as a benefit to them, not an interrogation.
2. **Qualification / discovery: is there a fit?** If yes, build a plan with the prospect to bring in the right people and decision-makers on both sides.
3. **Should we work together? Build a joint vision together**, the two sides now co-authoring the plan rather than one pitching the other.

---

## 32. Uncovering the real problem

Killer salespeople dig past the requested solution to find the actual problem. Prospects usually arrive already attached to a solution, and often haven't traced their own problem to its root.

- Always ask why. The stated ask is rarely the real issue.
- Treat the prospect's proposed "solution" as a data point, not the brief.

---

## 33. Earning the right to send a proposal

Don't hand out proposals or pricing on request. There's a real cost to giving one out too early:

- The prospect won't value it, or your time, if it came too easily.
- You lose the leverage of a next step they'd have to earn it through.

**Instead:** when a prospect casually asks for pricing or a proposal, tell them you'd be glad to, and that doing it right means a scoping call with them and the other key people first, so the proposal is accurate and actually fits. Confirm they want what you have before you produce anything.

---

## 34. Best call-opening question

Start every call with: **"Did I catch you at a bad time?"**

Gives the other person explicit permission to either engage or push back on timing, either way the call starts on honest footing.
