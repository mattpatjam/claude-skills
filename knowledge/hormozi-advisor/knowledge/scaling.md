# Scaling Framework
## Source: acquisition.com Scaling Course + $100M Scaling Roadmap

---

## Core Scaling Principle
You don't have a growth problem. You have a **constraint** problem.
One thing is limiting your growth right now. Fix that one thing before everything else.

Throwing resources at a business with an unidentified constraint = wasted money.

---

## The Scaling Roadmap Stages

### Stage 1: $0 → $1M (Nail the Offer)
Primary constraint: **Offer clarity + personal sales ability**
- You are the product. You sell it yourself.
- No systems needed yet — you need reps and proof
- Focus: get 10 paying clients, deliver results, get testimonials
- Don't hire until YOU can close consistently
- Key metric: close rate on qualified conversations

### Stage 2: $1M → $3M (Nail Fulfillment)
Primary constraint: **Delivery quality + capacity**
- You can't scale a leaky bucket
- Every client that churns is killing LTV and word-of-mouth
- Focus: document your delivery process so someone else can do it
- Hire your first fulfillment person (VA, operator, specialist)
- Key metric: client retention / NPS / results delivered

### Stage 3: $3M → $10M (Nail Sales)
Primary constraint: **Sales machine without you**
- Build a sales team that closes at 70%+ of your personal rate
- Script, train, track, comp correctly
- KPI dashboards per rep, weekly pipeline reviews
- Key metric: sales team close rate vs. your close rate

### Stage 4: $10M → $30M (Nail Operations)
Primary constraint: **Systems and management layer**
- You need people managing people
- Department heads, not just doers
- OKRs, quarterly reviews, accountability structure
- Key metric: % of decisions made without your involvement

### Stage 5: $30M+ (Nail Capital Allocation)
Primary constraint: **Where to invest profit for maximum ROI**
- Build vs. buy vs. partner decisions
- Portfolio thinking
- Exit readiness / optionality

## The Constraint Identification Framework
Ask these in order until you find the bottleneck:

1. **Lead flow:** Are you getting enough qualified leads per week?
   - If no → lead gen is the constraint
2. **Conversion:** Are leads showing up but not buying?
   - If yes → offer or sales process is the constraint
3. **Fulfillment:** Are buyers churning early or getting poor results?
   - If yes → delivery is the constraint
4. **Operations:** Are you working IN the business on things others could do?
   - If yes → hiring/systems is the constraint
5. **Capital:** Is money the limiting factor for growth?
   - If yes → monetization or reinvestment strategy is the constraint

**Rule:** Only work on the first constraint you identify. Don't skip ahead.

## Hiring Sequence (By Revenue Level)
- **$0-500K:** Do everything yourself. Learn every role.
- **$500K-$1M:** Hire admin/VA to free your time for selling
- **$1M-$3M:** Hire a fulfillment lead (someone to deliver)
- **$3M-$5M:** Hire a sales person (to replicate your sales)
- **$5M-$10M:** Hire an operator/COO (to manage the managers)
- **$10M+:** Build department heads (marketing, sales, ops, finance)

**Hormozi Rule:** Never hire for a role you haven't done yourself. You can't manage what you don't understand.

## Employee Training Framework (Document → Duplicate → Certify)

*Source: Hormozi — $100M Leads, employees chapter.*

Three steps, in order. Don't skip to step 2 without step 1 done properly.

**Step 1: Document.** You already know how to do the thing, so write the checklist down exactly as you do it, step by step. Have a trusted observer watch you work and take notes if that's easier than writing it cold. Then sit the new hire down and walk them through the checklist yourself, demonstrating each step in front of them, the same way a parent shows a kid how to tie their shoes. If they stop you to ask questions or slow you down anywhere, that's a gap in the checklist, not a gap in them. Fix the checklist on the spot. What comes out the other end is draft two, ready for someone else to run.

**Step 2: Duplicate.** Now they do it while you watch. Same checklist, roles reversed: they execute, you observe. The goal is exact duplication, not improvisation. If the checklist is right, they get the same outcome you got. If it's off, you'll find out immediately, because they'll stall or fail at the exact step the checklist got wrong. Fix the checklist, have them run it again, repeat until they get it right.

**Step 3: Certify.** Once they can run the checklist correctly on their own, they're a legitimate producer, not a trainee.

**Operating rules:**
- Most entry-level roles aren't complex. They take grit, not skill. If someone is trained properly and still underperforming after three weeks, cut them. Don't keep coaching past that point.
- Grade people on whether they followed directions, not on whether they got the result. This is the whole point: if they follow the checklist and the result is wrong, the checklist is broken, not the person. Fix the system, not the individual. If you grade on results instead, you can't tell whether a bad outcome means "didn't follow directions" or "checklist was wrong" — and you'll misdiagnose which one it is.

**How to apply for Matt:** any repeatable task Matt currently does himself (new listing intake with Ervien, CRM data entry, viewing coordination, follow-up cadence) should get a written checklist before it gets handed to anyone else — VA, junior agent, or future SC hire. Ties into the Hiring Sequence above: the checklist is what makes a hire at any revenue stage actually transferable.

## The Acquisition.com Growth Levers
In order of ease and ROI:
1. **Raise prices** — easiest lever, immediate impact, underused by 90% of businesses
2. **Reduce churn** — LTV goes up, CAC stays same, profit multiplies
3. **Increase purchase frequency** — sell more to existing buyers
4. **Expand to adjacent offers** — new product to same buyer
5. **New acquisition channel** — add a new traffic source
6. **New market** — new buyer segment or geography

**Do them in this order.** Most people jump to #5 and #6 when #1 and #2 would 3x the business.
