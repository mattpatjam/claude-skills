# Lead Generation Framework
## Source: acquisition.com Leads Course + $100M Leads

---

## The Business Growth Model

Only two ways to grow a business:
1. Get more customers
2. Make them worth more

Getting more customers = getting more leads (better, cheaper, more reliable).
**Double your leads, double your business.** That's the whole book.

---

## The Core Model
```
Leads → Engaged Leads → Buyers → Repeat Buyers
```
The only goal of lead gen is to get strangers to raise their hand.
Everything before a sale is about getting attention and converting it to engagement.

**What is a lead:** Any person you can contact. The numbers in your phone, emails on a list, people on the street. If you can contact them, they're a lead.

**What is an engaged lead:** A lead who shows interest in what you sell. This is the true output of advertising — not impressions, not followers, not reach.

---

## The Core Four Traffic Channels

**What defines the Core Four (from the book):** every way to advertise trades either time or money. You reach people who know you (warm) or strangers (cold). You do it publicly (content, ads) or privately (outreach). Four combinations, four methods — nothing else is available to a single person.

**The confirmed order to bring them online:**
1. Warm Outreach — start day one, zero cost, fastest proof
2. Content — start in parallel from day one, slow to pay off so the cost of delay is permanent
3. Cold Outreach — add once warm outreach is proven
4. Paid Ads — add last, only once the offer and message are proven

### 1. Warm Outreach (Best ROI, hardest to scale)
- Contact people you already know
- The fastest path to first revenue
- LinkedIn DMs, WhatsApp, email to existing network
- Script: "Hey [name], I'm working on [thing]. Do you know anyone who might want [result]?" — NOT "do you want to buy"
- Use network to get referrals, not direct sales

**The math (benchmark):**
- 100 warm reachouts → ~20 replies (1 in 5)
- 20 replies → ~4 take free offer (1 in 5)
- 4 free → ~1 paying customer
- 1 customer per 100 warm reachouts is the floor

---

### Warm Outreach — 10-Step Process

1. **Get your list** — every contact across every platform
2. **Pick a platform** — the one with the most contacts, start there
3. **Personalize your message** — use something real about them as the opener (2–3 sentences)
4. **Reach out** — 100 attempts per day
5. **Warm them up** — use the ACA framework (see below); don't pitch, ask questions
6. **Invite their friends** — "Do you know anyone who...?" not "Do you want to...?"
7. **Make it easy** — your offer should be the easiest thing in the world to say yes to (first five free)
8. **Start at the top** — best contacts first
9. **Start charging** — raise price 20% every 5 clients once you're getting referrals
10. **Keep your list warm** — regular value via email/content; then send the 9-word probe

**Follow-up rule:** Reach out up to 3 times (once per day) until they respond. Don't stop until you hit 3 attempts or a reply.

**9-word probe email:** "Are you still looking to [result]?" — sends to your warm list to surface who's ready now. The replies = your warm outreach priority list.

---

### The ACA Framework (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask)

Use this to navigate every reply toward your offer — without ever feeling pushy.

1. **Acknowledge** — restate what they said in your own words (active listening signal)
2. **Compliment** — tie it to a positive character trait if possible
3. **Ask** — ask another question; guide the conversation toward the offer

People love talking about themselves and love being complimented. ACA builds trust fast and gives you full control of where the conversation goes.

---

### The Value Formula

Every offer (lead magnet or core) is evaluated by the same 4 levers:

| Lever | What it means | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | What they want, the way they want it | Maximize |
| Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | How likely they think they are to get it | Maximize |
| Time Delay | How long before they get results | Minimize |
| Effort and Sacrifice | What they'll have to do or give up | Minimize |

**The perfect offer pitch:** "I help [ideal customer] get [dream outcome] in [time period] without [effort/sacrifice]" — then add proof to increase perceived likelihood.

**Ways to increase perceived likelihood:**
- Your own transformation story
- Proof from people just like them (same situation, same fear)
- Volume of happy outcomes (reviews, numbers, testimonials)
- Certifications, credentials, third-party validation
- Stats and research supporting the outcome
- Expert endorsements
- Guarantees or live demonstrations

**Hidden costs** — when someone says no, the real blocker is usually a hidden cost (time, effort, risk — not money). Ask: "What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?" Their answer shows you exactly what to solve. Solve it → they buy. Don't solve it → use the answer to get the next person.

### Free Content — Audience Building (Full Framework)

**The core insight:** The content isn't the compounding asset. The audience is.
Content disappears. The audience keeps growing. Warm reach-outs reach 10 people with 10 messages. One post reaches all 10. The sharing mechanism is infinite — if someone finds it valuable, they share it, and those people join your warm audience too.

**Why it matters for lead gen:** Free content makes all other advertising more effective.
If someone gets a DM from you and finds no content on your profile, they're less likely to respond. If they find a library of valuable content, they're primed. Content warms the list you're reaching out to.

**Trade-offs of posting content:**
1. Harder to personalize your message
2. You compete with everyone else posting — standing out is harder
3. If you do stand out, people will copy you — you must keep innovating

**The math:** When volume of content is 10x'd, audience grows 10x as fast. Volume works.

### The Content Unit

The smallest amount of material that hooks, retains, and rewards attention. Can be an image, a sentence, a clip — as long as it does all three.

**1. Hook — get their attention**

Goal: give them a reason to redirect attention toward you. Effectiveness = % of people who start consuming.

**Topics** — what to make your content about. Use personal experience first. No one has lived your life. Five categories:

| Category | What it is | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Far Past | Important life lessons. Connect wisdom to your product/service. Give them the story without the scar. | Relatable, differentiating — only you can tell your story |
| Recent Past | What happened in the last week. Meetings, conversations, deals, observations. Look at your calendar — there's gold there. | Fresh, real, easy to produce |
| Present | Ideas that come to you right now. Always have a way to capture them. Turn ideas into reality. | Timely, authentic |
| Trending | What's being talked about right now. Apply your expertise to the trend. | Broadest reach — attracts new eyes |
| Manufactured | Pick a topic, learn about it, make it or do it, show the world. Highest effort, highest payout. Top content on any platform is manufactured. | Highest ceiling for audience growth and monetization |

Action: Life happens. Profit by sharing yours.

**Headlines** — the short phrase that gives them a reason to consume. They weigh the likelihood of reward vs. every other option they have.

Seven components that drive click-through (use one or more):
1. **Recency** — as recent as possible
2. **Relevancy** — personally meaningful to the reader
3. **Celebrity** — includes prominent people
4. **Proximity** — close to home geographically
5. **Conflict** — opposing ideas, opposing people, nature
6. **Unusual** — odd, unique, rare, bizarre
7. **Ongoing** — story still in progress, plot twist possible

**Format** — match the best content on the platform. People consume what looks like what's rewarded them before. No matter how good the content is, better-looking content will hook them before yours has a chance.

Action: Format for the platform first. Use top-performing content in your market as your guide.

---

**2. Retain — keep their attention**

The favorite driver: **curiosity**. Unresolved questions keep people reading. Can be explicit (ask the question) or implicit (imply it).

Three retention tools:

| Tool | What it is | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **Lists** | Things, facts, tips, opinions, ideas — one after another under a theme | Flexible order. Less explicit reward. More versatile. |
| **Steps** | Actions in a specific sequence that accomplish a goal | Must be done in order. More explicit reward. Less flexible. |
| **Stories** | Events (real or imagined) with a lesson. People want to know what happens next. | Highest emotional pull. Works across all platforms. |

Mix and layer them. Stories that reinforce a step. Lists of stories. Steps inside a story. The more you layer, the more curious people stay.

Action: Use Lists, Steps, and Stories to keep questions unresolved until you're ready to close the loop.

---

**3. Reward — satisfy the reason they clicked**

Good content = content that makes people want to share it. It satisfies the hook. It delivers what the headline promised.

How to increase reward probability:
1. Hook the right audience (proper topic, headline, format)
2. Retain them (lists, steps, stories — keep questions open)
3. Clearly satisfy the reason the hook created

The audience decides if content is good. Rewarding them = matching or exceeding expectations.

**How you know it worked:** Your audience grows.

Action: Provide more value than anyone else. Make good on the promise. Completely answer the unresolved question.

---

**Shorter vs. longer content:** Shorter hooks and rewards fewer times. Longer does it more. More cycles = more skill required. String good content units in a row.

**The stranger test:** If you make it for strangers, strangers will like it and share it — and your audience grows faster. Consider the alternative.

### Free Content — Monetizing Your Audience (Part 2)

**The core principle:** The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow down your growth. The more patient you are, the more you will get.

---

#### The Give-Ask Ratio

Mature platforms (TV, big media, Facebook) have a studied give-ask ratio: **3–5 gives per 1 ask**. Facebook's feed runs roughly 4 content posts per 1 ad. That's the minimum to monetize without shrinking your audience.

But most of us want to grow, not just maintain. **Model growing platforms, not mature ones.** The more you reward your audience, the bigger it gets.

**Strategy: Give until they ask.**
People are always waiting for you to ask for money. When you don't, they trust you more, share your content more, and you grow faster. If you give enough, people start asking you to take their money. It makes people uncomfortable to keep receiving without giving back.

**Give in public. Ask in private.** Let the audience self-select when they're ready. Your growth never slows.

---

#### Two Ways to Weave Promotions into Content

**1. Integrated**
Advertise in every piece of content as long as your give/ask ratio stays high. On long-form content, this is your best bet.

Example: 1-hour podcast with three 30-second ads = 58 minutes of value, 1.5 minutes of ask. Well above 3:1.

- Place CTAs after a valuable moment or at the end
- Add a PS statement — one of the most-read parts of any piece of content. One of the best places for an ask.
- Test one CTA placement first. Watch audience growth. Then add a second.

**2. Intermittent**
Make many pure-value pieces, then occasionally make an ask piece. On short-form platforms, this dominates.

Example: 10 give posts → 1 promotional post.

- After the ask, go right back to providing value.
- Pick: advertise your **core offer** or your **lead magnet**. If unsure, lead magnet — it's lower risk.

**Platform rule:** Short platforms (TikTok, Twitter/X, Instagram) → intermittent. Long platforms (YouTube, podcast, email) → integrated.

---

#### One CTA at a Time

Never ask people to share, like, subscribe, and comment simultaneously. They'll do none. Make up your mind so they don't have to.

- Want shares? Only ask for shares.
- Want buyers? Only ask them to buy.

---

#### How to Scale Your Warm Audience

**Strategy 1 — Depth then Width**
Maximize one platform fully before adding the next.

1. Post on a relevant platform
2. Post regularly
3. Maximize quality and quantity (short-form: up to 10x/day; long-form: up to 5x/week)
4. Add a second platform while maintaining the first
5. Repeat until all relevant platforms are maximized

Advantage: compounding kicks in faster when you go deep. Fewer resources required.
Disadvantage: single-channel dependency risk.

**Strategy 2 — Width then Depth**
Get on every platform early, then maximize them together.

1. Post on all relevant platforms
2. Post regularly on each
3. Continue adding platforms before maximizing any one
4. Once on all platforms, maximize content creation across all simultaneously

Advantage: broader reach faster, content repurposing efficiency (same content, different formats = free reach).
Disadvantage: higher labor, attention, and time cost. Risk of mediocre content everywhere.

**How Hormozi executes it:**
1. Finds topics using the 5 categories (far past, recent past, present, trending, manufactured) — takes ~1 hour
2. Sits down twice a month, records 30+ short clips from those topics
3. Same day: records 2–4 longer videos unpacking high-performing short posts with more stories or examples

---

#### 7 Lessons from Making Content

1. **Switch from "how to" to "how I."** From "the best way is" to "my favorite ways are." Talk about what you've done, not what others should do. When you share experience, no one can question you — you're bulletproof. Telling a stranger what to do reads as preachy. Sharing your experience reads as generous.

2. **We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught.** Your audience doesn't listen 100% of the time. Repeat yourself. You'll get bored with your content long before your whole audience has even seen it.

3. **Puddles, ponds, lakes, oceans — narrow your focus.** Start with the most specific version of your topic. Local business? Make local business content, not general. Your audience will follow people with bigger track records on general topics. Narrow to what you do and where. Expand the pond as you grow.

4. **Content creates tools for salespeople.** High-performing content becomes a sales asset. Label each hit by the problem it solves and the benefit it provides. Sales team sends it before/after calls to handle objections the prospect hasn't even voiced yet.

5. **Free content retains paying customers.** Where someone gets value matters less than that they get it. A paying customer who also consumes your free content likes you more and stays longer. Your free content is a retention tool.

6. **No such thing as too long — only too boring.** Attention spans haven't shortened for good content. Streaming platforms prove people will binge hours of content they love. Make it good enough.

7. **Avoid prescheduling posts.** (Post in real-time where possible — current, fresh content performs better.)

---

#### Benchmarks — How to Know If It's Working

Measure monthly:
- **Total followers and reach** — how big
- **Rate of follower and reach growth** — how fast

You can only control inputs. Measuring outputs only tells you something if your inputs are consistent. Pick a posting cadence you can maintain. Pick your ask cadence. Start. Don't stop.

## Lead Magnets — Getting the Raise

**Definition:** A complete solution to a narrow problem. Free or low-cost. Once solved, reveals another problem your core offer solves.

**Why they work:** People give time before money. A person who pays with time now is more likely to pay with money later. If the lead magnet isn't worth their time, it's overpriced even at free.

**The economics:** A good lead magnet lowers your cost per customer because more engaged leads = more bites at the apple. Even if the lead magnet costs you money to deliver, extra customers cover the cost.

---

### The Problem-Solution Cycle

Every problem has a solution. Every solution reveals more problems. The cycle never ends.

**How to pick the right problem to solve:**
- Pick a narrow, meaningful problem
- Solve it completely with your lead magnet
- Make sure the NEXT problem that reveals itself is one your core offer solves
- That's the only criteria that matters

---

### 7-Step Lead Magnet Creation Framework

**Step 1 — Pick the problem and who to solve it for**
Narrow and meaningful. The next problem it reveals must be solved by your core offer.

**Step 2 — Choose how to solve it**
Three types of lead magnets:
1. **Reveal their problem** — works best when the problem gets worse the longer you wait
2. **Sample or trial** — give full but brief access to your core offer (limit uses, time, or both). Works when you have a recurring solution to a recurring problem. A good sample is so good they get hooked and come back. A bad sample is sucky fluff that destroys trust.
3. **One step of a multi-step process** — give one valuable step for free, sell the rest. Works when your core offer solves a complex problem with steps.

**The dressing room principle:** The best lead magnets do all three simultaneously — it's a trial (try it on), it reveals a problem (that shirt needs different pants), and it's step one of a larger process (building a complete outfit). If you can engineer all three into one lead magnet, you've built a dressing room.

**Step 3 — Choose how to deliver it**
Four delivery methods, each compatible with all three types above:
1. **Software** — a tool that does the job for them (spreadsheet, calculator, dashboard)
2. **Information** — teaches something (course, guide, interview, checklist, live event)
3. **Service** — you do work for free (website audit, 30-day ad management, consult)
4. **Physical product** — something they can hold (book, supplement, sample, branded item)

3 types × 4 delivery methods = 12 possible lead magnets for a single problem. Make as many versions as you can. Rotate them. Test which converts best.

**Step 4 — Test the name**
Leads must notice it before they can consume it. The headline/name matters more than almost anything else.

Test in this order:
1. Headline (most important)
2. Images
3. Sub-headline

Use polls, posts, or pre-launch DMs. If people respond asking when they can get it — you've got a winner.

**Step 5 — Make it easy to consume**
People do things that take less effort. Package in every format you can:
- Software: accessible on phone + desktop, multiple formats
- Information: video + audio + text versions. People consume differently — give them all three.
- Services: more times of day, more days of the week, phone + video + in-person
- Physical: fast shipping, simple to open, clear directions

**Step 6 — Make it genuinely good**
> Give away the secret. Sell the implementation.

The marketplace judges everything — free or not. If your lead magnet is fluff, people conclude you're fluff. They buy from someone else and tell others not to buy from you.

People buy based on the value they *expect* after buying. The easiest way to set that expectation high is to deliver value before they pay.

Your lead magnet should be valuable enough that you could charge for it. If you're scared to give it away, you've probably got a winner.

**Step 7 — Make it easy to say they want more (CTA)**
Once they consume the lead magnet, some are ready to buy. Tell them exactly what to do.

**Good CTAs have two things:**
1. What to do (clear, simple, direct — call, click, book, reply)
2. A reason why — any reason works better than no reason

**Best reasons to act now:**
- **Scarcity** — limited supply, real or natural. Use the natural limits of your business ("I can only take 5 new clients this month"). Don't fake it.
- **Urgency** — limited time. Offer closes at X. Bonus disappears in 24 hours. The shorter the window, the faster people move.
- **Any reason at all** — Harvard study: people are more likely to act if you give a reason, even a weak one. "Because your business needs it" is better than nothing. Use "because" as a sentence trigger.

**Pro tip on bonuses:** Add valuable rotating bonuses instead of changing prices. Bonus scarcity is easier to manage than pricing scarcity and creates natural urgency without touching your core offer.

### Lead Magnet Rules (Summary):
- Solve ONE specific problem completely
- Deliver value BEFORE asking for anything
- The lead magnet should make them want the paid offer (logical next step)
- Name it like an offer — specific, outcome-focused
- Make it worth their time or it's overpriced at free
- Give away the secret, sell the implementation

## The Engage-to-Nurture Sequence
After someone opts in or engages:
1. Deliver the promised value immediately
2. Follow with 1-2 more value hits (no pitch)
3. Introduce the problem you solve better than anyone
4. Make the offer

Timing: most sequences are too short. Nurture over days/weeks, not hours.

## Real-World Examples & Case Studies

### The Accidental Lead Machine — Webinar vs. Case Study
Spent 8 Sundays building a webinar funnel. Ran $150/day in ads. Got 80 leads, zero sales. Shut it off.

Same night, made a 13-minute screen recording of walking through a real gym ad account (results, spend, what they did, how many sold). Called it a case study. New headline: "How we added 213 members and $112,000 in revenue to a small gym in San Diego."

Result: calendar stacked with gym owner appointments within 24 hours.

**The lesson:** Give them the proof they want, not the presentation you want to make. People don't want the webinar — they want to see it work for someone like them.

### The 300 Flyers Lesson — Volume Is Not Optional
Mentor ran a tanning salon chain. Said flyers crushed for him. Hormozi printed 300, put them on cars, got one call — from an angry Mercedes owner whose car got scratched.

Called the mentor to report failure. Response: *"How many did you put out?"* 300. *"Shoot, I test with 5,000. When we find a winner, we do 5,000 per day, every day for a month."*

Hormozi had done 1/1,000th of the required volume and concluded the method didn't work.

**The rule he extracted:** The right action in the wrong amount always fails. Most people do 1-10% of the inputs required and declare the method broken.

## Lead Gen Audit Checklist
Before choosing a channel, answer:
- [ ] Do I know exactly who my avatar is?
- [ ] Do I know where they spend time online?
- [ ] Is my offer clear enough that a stranger gets it in 10 seconds?
- [ ] Do I have a lead magnet that delivers real value?
- [ ] Do I have a follow-up sequence after the lead comes in?
- [ ] Am I tracking CPL and conversion from lead to sale?
