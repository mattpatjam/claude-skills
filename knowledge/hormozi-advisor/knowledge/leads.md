# Lead Generation Framework
## Source: acquisition.com Leads Course + $100M Leads

---

## The Business Growth Model

Only two ways to grow a business:
1. Get more customers
2. Make them worth more

Getting more customers = getting more leads (better, cheaper, more reliable).
**Double your leads, double your business.** That's the whole book.

---

## The Core Model
```
Leads → Engaged Leads → Buyers → Repeat Buyers
```
The only goal of lead gen is to get strangers to raise their hand.
Everything before a sale is about getting attention and converting it to engagement.

**What is a lead:** Any person you can contact. The numbers in your phone, emails on a list, people on the street. If you can contact them, they're a lead.

**What is an engaged lead:** A lead who shows interest in what you sell. This is the true output of advertising — not impressions, not followers, not reach.

## The Core Four Traffic Channels

### 1. Warm Outreach (Best ROI, hardest to scale)
- Contact people you already know
- The fastest path to first revenue
- LinkedIn DMs, WhatsApp, email to existing network
- Script: "Hey [name], I'm working on [thing]. Do you know anyone who might want [result]?" — NOT "do you want to buy"
- Use network to get referrals, not direct sales

### 2. Cold Outreach (Scalable, lower conversion)
- Contact strangers who match your avatar
- Email, LinkedIn, DMs
- Volume game — expect low % response
- Key: personalization + clear value prop + low-friction CTA
- CTA should be a question, not a pitch: "Would this be useful to you?"
- Follow-up is everything — most replies come on message 3-5

### 3. Content (Compound, slow to build, high leverage)
- Post regularly on one platform where your buyer lives
- Content formula: Problem → Insight → Solution → CTA
- Goal: make people feel you understand their problem better than they do
- Consistency > quality in early stages
- Pick ONE platform and go deep before adding more
- Types: educational, opinion, behind-the-scenes, case studies, mistakes

### 4. Paid Ads (Fast, costs money, requires offer clarity first)
- Only run ads once you have a proven offer
- Ads amplify what's already working — they don't fix a broken offer
- Start with retargeting (warm audience) before cold
- Test creative (the hook) more than the landing page
- Metrics to watch: CPL (cost per lead), show rate, close rate

## Lead Magnets — Getting the Raise
A lead magnet is something valuable given free in exchange for contact info or attention.

### Types (ranked by conversion power):
1. **Free trial / sample** — let them experience the product
2. **Done-for-you asset** — template, swipe file, calculator they can use now
3. **Mini-course or workshop** — short, teaches one thing, demonstrates expertise
4. **Checklist / playbook** — fast to consume, actionable
5. **Quiz or assessment** — high engagement, personalized output
6. **Webinar / live event** — high intent, but harder to get attendance

### Lead Magnet Rules:
- Solve ONE specific problem completely
- Deliver value BEFORE asking for anything
- The lead magnet should make them want the paid offer (logical next step)
- Name it like an offer — specific, outcome-focused

---

## The Engage-to-Nurture Sequence
After someone opts in or engages:
1. Deliver the promised value immediately
2. Follow with 1-2 more value hits (no pitch)
3. Introduce the problem you solve better than anyone
4. Make the offer

Timing: most sequences are too short. Nurture over days/weeks, not hours.

## Referral Systems
- Every happy client/customer is a referral machine — if you ask
- Timing: ask right after a win ("You just got [result] — who else do you know who needs this?")
- Make it easy: give them exact language to use
- Incentivize where appropriate (commission, credit, gift)

- Demonstrate the output, not the process
- Show newsletters/posts you've created — let the work sell
- Case study format: before/after of content output

## Real-World Examples & Case Studies

### The Accidental Lead Machine — Webinar vs. Case Study
Spent 8 Sundays building a webinar funnel. Ran $150/day in ads. Got 80 leads, zero sales. Shut it off.

Same night, made a 13-minute screen recording of walking through a real gym ad account (results, spend, what they did, how many sold). Called it a case study. New headline: "How we added 213 members and $112,000 in revenue to a small gym in San Diego."

Result: calendar stacked with gym owner appointments within 24 hours.

**The lesson:** Give them the proof they want, not the presentation you want to make. People don't want the webinar — they want to see it work for someone like them.

### Warm Outreach — Building the First Business From Zero
Before ads, before content, before anything: 100% warm outreach.

**The approach:**
- Reached out to everyone he knew: "Do you know anyone trying to get in shape? I'm training people for free for 12 weeks. All they have to do is donate to a charity and let me use their before/after pictures."
- Only 6 people said yes: 2 high school friends, 1 college friend, 3 referrals
- After 12 weeks of results, asked them to pay him instead of the charity
- 5-6 more clients from referrals, all paying directly

**Result:** $4,000/month. Replaced his full-time job income.

**The math benchmarks (warm outreach):**
- 100 warm reachouts → ~20 replies (1 in 5)
- 20 replies → ~4 take the free offer (1 in 5)
- 4 free → ~1 converts to paying customer
- **1 customer per 100 warm reachouts**
- 500 reachouts/week at 1% conversion × $1,400 offer = $2,000/week = **$104,000/year from warm outreach alone**

### The 300 Flyers Lesson — Volume Is Not Optional
Mentor ran a tanning salon chain. Said flyers crushed for him. Hormozi printed 300, put them on cars, got one call — from an angry Mercedes owner whose car got scratched.

Called the mentor to report failure. Response: *"How many did you put out?"* 300. *"Shoot, I test with 5,000. When we find a winner, we do 5,000 per day, every day for a month."*

Hormozi had done 1/1,000th of the required volume and concluded the method didn't work.

**The rule he extracted:** The right action in the wrong amount always fails. Most people do 1-10% of the inputs required and declare the method broken.

### Content — The 78% Stat That Changed Everything
At Gym Launch, paid ads started declining. Team debated why: the creative? the copy? the offer?

Ila asked a different question: *"What did we stop doing in the months before the decline?"*

Answer: Alex stopped making gym-specific content and switched to general business content.

**Sent a survey to all gym owners.** Found that **78% of clients had consumed at least 2 long-form content pieces before booking a call.**

Content was nurturing demand the whole time. Paid ads got the credit but free content was doing the warming.

**Content compound math:**
- Posted 2 podcasts/week for 4 years before hitting top 10 in US for business
- When 10x'd content volume, audience grew 10x as fast
- $100M Offers book: 1/4 split between ebook, physical, audiobook, free video (4x reach from same content, different formats)

**Give until they ask:** Once you give enough, people start asking to buy. The best customers are those who feel they get the better end of the deal even as paying customers.

**Hormozi's own referral math:**

- At $200 cost per customer, a referral that costs $0 improves LTGP:CAC from 3:1 to 12:1 (3 customers for the price of 1)

### Allen Software — The Affiliate Pyramid
Allen (software company) was built entirely through a 3-tier affiliate structure with no paid ads:

**The pyramid:**
- 1 super affiliate → recruited 10 agencies/month
- Those 10 agencies → each brought 5 local businesses/month = 50 businesses
- Those 50 businesses → each brought 50 leads/month = 2,500 leads
- Allen worked those leads at ~$15 each = **$37,500/month from one super affiliate**

**Month-by-month compound effect:**
- Month 1: $12,500
- Month 2: $25,000
- Month 3: $37,500
- (continues growing as each super affiliate adds layers)

**Result:** $1.7 million/month within 6 months of launch from just a few agency super affiliates.

Over a year of preparation. $1M+ in custom affiliate software. $3M in inventory ordered.

**Integration model:** Taught gym owners to do free nutrition consults with every new member → upsell Prestige Labs products at the consult → gym kept the consult fee, Prestige split the supplement revenue.

## Lead Gen Audit Checklist
Before choosing a channel, answer:
- [ ] Do I know exactly who my avatar is?
- [ ] Do I know where they spend time online?
- [ ] Is my offer clear enough that a stranger gets it in 10 seconds?
- [ ] Do I have a lead magnet that delivers real value?
- [ ] Do I have a follow-up sequence after the lead comes in?
- [ ] Am I tracking CPL and conversion from lead to sale?
