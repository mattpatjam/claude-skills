# Execution Playbook

*Source: Hormozi — $100M Leads, final section. Applies across all methods: cold outreach, content, paid ads, referrals.*

---

## The Core Problem Most People Have

Most people aren't doing the wrong thing. They're doing the right thing at the wrong volume.

Hormozi put out 300 flyers and got nothing. His mentor was putting out 5,000 a day (150,000 a month). Same tactic. 500x the volume. One of them thought flyers didn't work.

You might be running decent ads, doing outreach the right way, making content consistently. The question is whether you're doing enough of it. Most of the time the answer is no, and the gap is bigger than you think.

---

## The Learning Budget

At a $30M/year business, someone Hormozi met told him: "I allocate a fixed percentage to crazy ideas I give myself permission to fail on."

Most ideas flop. That's expected. Occasionally one takes off and rewrites how you compete in the market. The budget covers both outcomes.

How to apply it:

- Pick a number, not a percentage. Hormozi used $10,000/month when he started this.
- Treat it as a cost of staying competitive, not as waste.
- Apply it to everything: one day a month for content experiments, one rep per week with a different outreach script, one ad test per cycle that has nothing to do with what's working.

His media team runs "hackathon days" -- no meetings, everyone produces one or two things that are completely outside what they normally do. Some of the best content ideas have come from those days.

This works for the same reason anything else does: volume and variance. If 90% of your effort is on what's already working and 10% is on things that might work, you stay ahead without gambling everything.

## Rule of 100

The baseline. Do 100 primary actions per day, every day, toward getting leads. That's the floor.

What counts as a primary action depends on the channel:
- Cold outreach: 100 messages sent
- Content: 100 minutes producing
- Paid ads: $100 in spend (or your defined daily budget)

The rule matters because it creates a non-negotiable daily commitment. You do it regardless of motivation, regardless of whether yesterday worked, regardless of results so far. The results show up over time; the actions show up today.

---

## Open to Goal (Rule of 100 on steroids)

Instead of committing to a number of actions, commit to a number of outcomes. Don't stop until you hit it.

The door knocker example: he wouldn't stop until he had 20 appointments set. He counted out loud as he went. Hit one, say "one" at the next door. Hit two, say "two." Didn't matter if it took until lunchtime or 9pm. He hit 20 every single day and was the top producer at that company.

The difference from Rule of 100:

- Rule of 100 says do 100 reach outs. Maybe you get 2 replies, maybe 10.
- Open to goal says get 5 replies. Maybe that takes 80 reach outs, maybe 200.

Open to goal pulls you forward on bad days. When the 1% rate becomes 0.5%, you just do twice as much. The goal stays fixed; the effort adjusts. That builds a different kind of muscle.

## Hormozi's Personal Schedule

He shares this not as a prescription but as a way to show how he makes the volume possible:

1. **Wakes up early.** Not because mornings are magical. Because 4-6 hours of uninterrupted time before the day starts is valuable and he has to get it somewhere.

2. **No morning routine.** Coffee, then work. A two-hour morning ritual would need to produce more output per hour than just working for two hours. He doesn't think that math works.

3. **No meetings before noon.** 5am to noon = 7 uninterrupted hours before the "work day" starts.

4. **Schedule back to front.** Meetings go at the end of the day. The calendar is built backwards from the last meeting so the front of the day stays clear. Working 5am to 3pm with meetings starting at 3 gives 10 straight hours first. Most people do it the other way around.

## The 5-Step Execution Checklist

This is the full loop. Start here, run it, repeat.

**Step 1: Pick your lead type**

Who are you trying to get? Customers, employees, affiliates, or agencies? Each one is a different target, but the same four methods apply to all of them.

**Step 2: Commit to your daily volume**

Choose Rule of 100 or Open to Goal. Define your number before you start the day. For paid ads, define your learning budget as a separate line from your core spend.

Optional: add 100 actions per day on a second channel on top of your primary one.

**Step 3: Fill out your advertising brief**

Before you start, answer these:
- Who are you targeting?
- What's the offer or lead magnet?
- What platform or channel?
- What audience or list?
- When in your day does this happen?
- What result are you driving toward (leads, booked calls, applications)?
- Which method from the core four?
- How many actions or dollars per day?
- How many follow-ups and over what timeframe?

These don't change often, but writing them out eliminates the daily decision tax of figuring out what to do.

**Step 4: Do the daily action until you can afford to hand it off**

You do this until the revenue from it covers hiring someone to do it for you. That's the whole scaling path: do it yourself until it pays for help, then hire, then free yourself up to do the next thing.

**Step 5: When you hire, go back to step 1**

Make your new hire (the employee) your new lead type. Use the core four to find and recruit them the same way you used it to find customers. Then repeat steps 1-4.

Each loop adds someone doing the work on your behalf. That's how the leverage from the referrals playbook applies to the whole operation, not just customer referrals.

## Conclusion

If leads are slow, it's almost always one of two things:

1. Not enough volume.
2. Not doing it consistently enough.

The four methods -- warm outreach, cold outreach, content, paid ads -- work. The question is whether you're doing 100 primary actions a day toward one of them, and whether you're doing it every day without exception.

That's it. Everything else in this system supports these four things or multiplies them.
