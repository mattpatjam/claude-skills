# DotCom Secrets — Framework Reference

*Pure framework. No company-specific application. Pull from here when building skills, designing funnels, or advising clients.*

---

## What is a Sales Funnel

A structured journey that moves prospects from awareness to purchase. Traffic → lead capture → sales process → upsell → ascend to client.

Two outcomes when done right:
- Better customer experience: they find exactly what they need
- More money: you upsell them on the right next thing

Each step has one job. One hook, one story, one offer, one call to action.

---

## 1. The Secret Formula

Four questions that sit behind every profitable funnel. Answer these before touching a landing page.

1. Who is your dream customer?
2. Where are they right now?
3. What bait can you use to attract them?
4. What result do you want to give them?

Everything builds on these four answers. Skip this and your funnel is a solution looking for a problem.

---

## 2. Value Ladder Mission Statement (VLMS)

Before building the ladder, write the mission statement.

**Formula:** We help (who) to (what — the result they want) through (how — your unique vehicle).

- Who: your dream customer
- What: the unsolved problem, stated as the result they want
- How: your offer that is different from what they've tried before

**Examples:**
- Dentist: "We help families in our community to increase their confidence through creating a beautiful smile they are proud to share."
- ClickFunnels: "We help visionary entrepreneurs to grow their companies through sales funnels."

---

## 3. The Value Ladder

You can't sell a $50K product to someone who doesn't know you yet. The ladder moves people from free/low-cost entry up through higher-value offers over time.

Each rung offers more value at a higher price. Most businesses only have one rung — that's the problem. You lose money at the bottom. You make it at the top. The goal is to ascend customers, not just convert at entry.

**Value Ladder vs. Sales Funnel:** The ladder is the destination map — all your offers and their prices. The funnel is the mechanism that moves someone up one rung at a time. As customers ascend, their numbers shrink until you're left with your true fans. The deeper your funnel, the more each customer is worth, the more you can spend to acquire them. The business that can spend the most to acquire a customer wins.

## 4. The Attractive Character

Your online persona. What makes people follow you before they buy from you.

**Three types:**
- Leader: guides people from one place to another ("I did this, follow me")
- Adventurer/Reporter: figures it out in public ("I'm learning this alongside you")
- Reluctant Hero: didn't set out to be the expert but ended up here anyway

**Four elements:**
1. Backstory — the origin story that explains why you do this
2. Character flaws — you're not perfect; that's what makes you believable
3. Polarity — you have actual opinions, you're not neutral
4. A cause — something bigger than the product

**Story arcs to use:**
- Loss and redemption: had success, lost it, rebuilt
- Us vs them: polarizes your audience, defines who "us" is
- Before and after: transformation stories, work in any market
- Amazing discovery: something you found that changes the game
- Secret telling: a secret that pulls people in better than almost anything else
- Third-person testimonial: other people's results delivered through story

**The binding principle:** The attractive character is the thread running through every funnel page, every email, every offer. Customers come in looking for a result, but they stay because of the relationship they've built with you. Without this, a funnel is just a series of pages.

## 5. Hook, Story, Offer

The three-part structure behind every piece of sales communication that actually works.

- **Hook** — stops the scroll, earns the next sentence
- **Story** — builds belief, handles objections before they're asked
- **Offer** — what you're selling, structured so the value is obvious before the price

The order matters. Most businesses lead with the offer. That's why people scroll past.

**Hook formats:**
- Question: something the dream customer would ask themselves
- Statement: a counter-intuitive truth about their market
- Visual + curiosity: a result image + "here's how this happened"

## 6. Traffic Strategy

Three types of traffic. One goal.

**Traffic you control** — paid ads. You send it wherever you want.
**Traffic you earn** — organic, referrals, PR, word of mouth.
**Traffic you own** — your email list, your audience. No algorithm between you and them.

The rule: always convert traffic you control and earn into traffic you own. Ads rent attention. Lists own it.

**The three temperatures:**
- Cold: never heard of you
- Warm: knows who you are, not ready yet
- Hot: ready to buy, needs the right offer

## 7. Dream 100

There are 100 people or companies who already have your dream customers. Get in front of their audience instead of building yours from zero.

**How it works:**
1. List 100 dream partners — influencers, publications, agencies, other businesses, communities
2. Start digging: follow, comment, engage, provide value
3. Move to buying in: ads to their audience, joint ventures, guest appearances
4. Eventually earn your way in: become known in their world

**Two phases:**
- Digging in: relationship building, showing up consistently
- Buying in: ads targeting their audience, sponsored appearances

## 8. Follow-up Funnels

Two types of messages that run after someone enters your world.

### 8a. Soap Opera Sequence (SOAP)

**Superseded — see `expert-secrets-reference.md` § SOAP Opera Email Sequences for the current 9-email version** (emotion → logic → fear arc, emails 1-5/6-7/8-9). This project's current-priorities.md confirms the 9-email version is the intended one to use.

Historical note, kept for reference only: this original 5-email version framed the turning point as a single "Epiphany" beat rather than splitting it into separate emotion/logic/fear stages. The goal (build a bond before pitching, each email hooking the next) still holds — just use the more granular 9-email structure to execute it.

### 8b. Daily Sign-in Emails (Seinfeld Emails)

After the SOAP sequence ends, subscribers move to a broadcast list and receive daily emails. These are entertainment with a redirect — not nurture sequences.

**The formula:**
- 90% entertainment, 10% content — always
- Subject line: the hook that gets the open
- Body: a story, idea, or list that entertains
- End: a tie-back to whichever offer or funnel you're currently pushing

**Three formats:**
- Episode: something that happened in your life or business
- Epiphany: an insight, idea, or perspective shift
- Checklist/Q&A/FAQ: practical, punchy, useful

**How they work:** After the SOAP sequence, subscribers land on your broadcast list. Each day you send one sign-in email. If you're launching something new, it directs them to that funnel. If nothing's happening, pick your best-converting offer and point there daily until the next event.

**Difference from SOAP:** SOAP builds the bond during onboarding. Sign-ins maintain the relationship long-term and keep redirecting traffic back into the value ladder.

## 9. Funnel Types

### Brunson's Core Funnel Types

| Funnel Type | Best for |
|---|---|
| Book funnel | Low-ticket physical or digital product; builds list + qualifies buyers |
| Cart funnel | E-commerce or multi-product offers with order bumps and upsells |
| Challenge funnel | Community-building; 5-day or 30-day challenge with daily engagement |
| Video Sales Letter (VSL) | Mid-ticket offers where the story needs room to breathe before the pitch |
| Webinar funnel | High-ticket offers requiring education + trust before purchase |
| Product launch funnel | New offer releases; builds anticipation across multiple touchpoints before cart opens |
| Application funnel | High-ticket/custom engagements; filters unqualified leads before any sales call |
| Back-end funnel | Existing customers only; ascends them to the next rung of the value ladder |

### Lead Funnels

Lead funnels sit at the front of the value ladder. Goal: generate a lead and get them into a follow-up funnel. Nothing is sold here — only email addresses are captured.

**Before picking a funnel type, answer these 4 questions:**
1. What is the goal for this step in the value ladder?
2. Which funnel type best achieves that goal?
3. What offer plugs into this funnel?
4. What copy or script should each page use?

**Type 1: Lead Squeeze Funnel**

Simplest funnel. One page, one action.

- Page 1 (Squeeze): Lead magnet offer + curiosity-based headline (the hook) + short sub-headline (the story) + email capture. The stronger the offer, the less copy you need.
- Lead magnet options: ebook, coupon, video, membership, contest, free report — anything the dream customer genuinely wants
- Page 2 (Thank You): Deliver what you promised. Introduce the next step.

The headline is everything. If it creates curiosity, conversion is high. If it explains the product, it doesn't work.

**Type 2: Survey Funnel**

Offer: "Take this quiz and I'll tell you X." The quiz engages the visitor before asking for anything. The key mechanic is the bucket question — one question whose answer determines which landing page they end up on.

Quiz structure (6 questions):
1. Self-identifying question (what type of person are you?)
2. Subject-specific self-ID (what kind of project/situation do you have?)
3. Skill level declaration
4. Biggest challenge declaration
5. Clarifying/educational question
6. Surprise curiosity-based question (pattern interrupt)

After the quiz:
- Calculating popup (increases perceived value of the result)
- Squeeze page: email address in exchange for the custom result
- Results page: video + custom PDF + your solution framed for their specific situation

Each results page and follow-up SOAP sequence is customized per bucket. The more you personalize per bucket, the higher conversions will be.

**Type 3: Summit Funnel**

A virtual summit where you interview 5-10 experts in your space. Subscribers get free access; you build your list by leveraging your speakers' audiences.

- Page 1 (Registration): Email in exchange for free access. Simple, single action.
- Page 2 (Special Offer): All-access pass upsell (recordings, transcripts, bonuses) + order form. Shown immediately after registration.
- Page 3 (Broadcast): Where registrants watch the content.

Strategic value:
- Partners and speakers promote to their own lists, driving new subscribers at no ad cost
- Interviewing speakers builds a direct network of influencers in your space
- The all-access upsell turns it profitable

## 10. Funnel Hacking

Before building any funnel, find people already succeeding with one and reverse-engineer it. If you can't find other businesses selling to your target market — don't move forward.

**Three ways to find funnels to model:**
1. Direct competitors — same market, same offer type
2. Indirect competitors — different market, same buyer
3. Same funnel type — someone running the funnel you want to build, in any market

**The process:**
1. Search the keyword your dream customer would use
2. Look for paid ads — those are funnels in action (organic doesn't tell you what converts)
3. Do the same on Facebook/Instagram
4. Click every ad. Act like a customer. Go through every step.
5. At each step: what's the hook? What's the story? What's the offer?
6. Buy the front-end product so you see the full sequence (upsells, thank-you pages, follow-up emails)
7. Do this across at least 6 funnels to establish a baseline

Once you know the baseline, you know what you have to beat.

## 11. The Seven Phases of a Funnel

Every funnel moves visitors through the same seven phases. Understanding what the customer feels at each step is how you remove friction.

**Pre-frame principle:** Each step is a pre-frame for the next. Every page, email, and ad sets up the next one. Optimize the transition between steps, not just the steps themselves.

| Phase | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Determine traffic temperature | Before anyone hits your page, know if they're cold, warm, or hot. Each needs different treatment. |
| 2 | Frame bridge | Ad, video, email, or article that pre-frames them before the landing page. Bridges where they are to where you want them. |
| 3 | Qualify subscribers | Capture email in exchange for value. Separates curious from interested. |
| 4 | Qualify buyers | Immediately after opt-in, show a sales page. A buyer is a buyer — if someone buys once, they'll keep buying. |
| 5 | Identify hyperactive buyers | Find who buys multiple things quickly. Put them on a separate sublist. Spend more to market to them. |
| 6 | Age and ascend the relationship | Follow-up funnels take over. The attractive character builds the relationship and moves them up the value ladder over time. |
| 7 | Change the selling environment | High-ticket offers close better over the phone, at a live event, or via direct mail. Change the environment to match the price point. |

**Lead awareness ladder (for traffic temperature):**

| Stage | Where they are |
|---|---|
| Unaware | Doesn't know they have the problem |
| Problem aware | Knows the problem, doesn't know solutions exist |
| Solution aware | Knows solutions exist, doesn't know yours |
| Product aware | Knows your product, hasn't bought |
| Most aware | Ready to buy, just needs the right offer |

## 12. Core Metrics

Two numbers. If you don't know these, you can't run a funnel profitably.

**CPA (Cost Per Acquisition):** average ad spend to get one new customer.
**ACV (Average Cart Value):** average total revenue per customer across the entire funnel — not just the front-end.

**The rule:**
- CPA < ACV → funnel is working. Scale spend.
- CPA > ACV → funnel is broken. Fix before scaling.

Example: $20 CPA, $100 ACV = $80 profit per customer. If consistent, spend as much as you can.

Why this matters: the deeper your funnel (more upsells, more ascension points), the higher your ACV. The higher your ACV, the more you can afford to spend on acquisition. This is how bigger businesses outbid everyone else for the same traffic.

## 13. Funnel Scripts

A funnel is just a sales presentation captured once and made to work forever. You already know how to sell your product. Record the pitch, put it in the funnel, and you've removed yourself from the selling equation.

The structure of the funnel is the frame. The script is what actually converts. Always weave your attractive character, your stories, and your message into every script.

### 13a. Curiosity-Based Headlines

The headline's job is not to sell. Its job: hook the right person, stop them, arouse curiosity, and move them to the next step. Curiosity — not information — is what keeps people reading.

A headline appears everywhere: ads, landing pages, email subject lines, the first sentence of a video.

**Question headline templates (desire or pain):**
- What if you could (desire) without (pain)?
- Are you worried about (pain)?
- Have you stopped struggling with (pain)?
- Are you sick and tired of not having (desire)?
- Are you getting enough (desire)?
- Do you want to be a (target audience identity)?
- Is it time you got the (desire) you really want?

**Headlines by traffic temperature:**

| Temperature | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hot | Mention your name and product | "Finally, discover how [product] gives you the secret to [big result] without [pain]. Today." |
| Warm | Lead with the payoff/benefit | "See how easily you can [big result] without [pain]." |
| Cold | Lead with their problem | "Don't let [pain] stop you." |

Never send cold, warm, and hot traffic to the same landing page. Each needs a headline calibrated to where they are in the relationship.

### 13b. Who, What, Why, How Script

Used at the front of the value ladder for cold or warm traffic. Answers 4 questions fast, then pushes for a CTA.

**The 4 questions:**

1. **Who are you?** Introduce yourself briefly.
2. **What do you have?** Hook them and introduce the offer immediately.
3. **Why do they need it?** Use PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solve.
   - Problem: Name it using their exact words.
   - Agitate: Twist the knife. Why has it stayed unsolved?
   - Solve: Your offer as the logical resolution.
4. **How do they get it?** Tell them exactly what to do next. One action.

**4 amplifiers to add after the CTA:**

| Amplifier | Purpose |
|---|---|
| The catch | Preempt skepticism — explain why the deal is so good. If there's no catch, say that directly. |
| Urgency and scarcity | Add a real time or quantity constraint to make acting now matter. |
| Guarantee | Remove purchase risk. |
| Recap | Summarize offer + deadline for skimmers. |

### 13c. Star, Story, Solution Script

Longer format — typically 20-30 minutes in video, or a long-form sales page. Used for mid-ticket offers that need to break false beliefs before the pitch lands.

#### Part 1: The Star

1. **Pattern interrupt / hook** — first thing they see. Big promise, open a loop.
2. **Core desire questions** — ask 2-3 questions about what they most want.
3. **Agitate past failures** — acknowledge they've tried before and it hasn't worked.
4. **Big promise (the one thing)** — the single thing that, if solved, changes everything.
5. **Introduce the star** — transition to the story.

#### Part 2: The Story

Start at the peak of drama. Fill in backstory after you have their attention.

| Story element | What it does |
|---|---|
| High drama (hook) | Starts mid-crisis — pulls them in immediately |
| Backstory (the wall) | How the character got to this point |
| The problem | What they're stuck on — mirrors the reader's problem |
| Epiphany / declaration | The moment of realization and decision to change |
| Path to solution | All the things tried before the breakthrough |
| First sign of success | A glimpse of what's possible |
| Conspiracy | The cards were stacked against you from the start |
| The big lie | It's not their fault they haven't succeeded before now |
| Common enemy | Who or what is really responsible |
| Rapid growth | How fast things moved once the truth was clear |
| Case studies | Third-party proof — others got the same result |
| Hidden benefits | Unexpected wins that came along with the main result |

#### Part 3: The Solution

| Element | What to say |
|---|---|
| Formal intro | "That's why I created [offer name]" — name the pain it addresses |
| Pain and cost | What it took to create this — time, money, failures |
| Ease | How much easier it is for them now that the trail has been blazed |
| Speed | How much time this saves them |
| Features → benefits | For each feature: "[Feature], so you can [benefit]." Never sell features alone. |
| Social proof | Others who got the same results |
| Offer stack | Everything included — present it like a stack slide |
| Price anchor | Total value should be 10x the asking price. If it's not, add more. |
| Emotional close | "If all this did was X, would it be worth the full price?" Get the internal yes before revealing the price. |
| Real price reveal | Drop the price and explain why. |
| Guarantee | Remove all risk. |
| Urgency and scarcity | Legitimate reason to act now. |
| Future pacing | Paint the picture of what life looks like after they buy. |
| CTA | Tell them exactly what to do. One action. |
| Post-selling | Make them feel like they'd be left behind if they wait. |
| Takeaway selling | "It doesn't matter to me either way — but this closes soon." |
| Recap (close) | Summary for skimmers. Offer + deadline + what they get. |

### 13d. One-Time Offer (OTO) Script

Used on upsell pages immediately after a purchase. 3-5 minutes. Goal: confirm they made the right call and get the second yes fast.

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Confirm the decision | "Congratulations on [purchase]. Don't close your browser — your order isn't finished yet." Reassure, kill buyer's remorse, keep the loop open. |
| Why it was smart | Reinforce the value of what they just bought. |
| Question about more | Ask if they want to go further or get better results faster. |
| Exclusivity | This offer is only here, right now. |
| Speed / better results | The OTO complements the initial purchase and delivers the result faster or deeper. |
| The one thing | The single most important element of the OTO that determines success. Don't explain everything — just the one thing. |
| CTA | One action. Clear and direct. |
