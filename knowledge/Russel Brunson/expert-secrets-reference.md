# Expert Secrets — Framework Reference

*Pure framework. No company-specific application. Pull from here when building skills, designing offers, or advising clients.*

*Source: Expert Secrets by Russell Brunson. Companion to `dotcom-secrets-reference.md` — funnels tell you HOW to move people, Expert Secrets tells you WHY they move at all.*

---

## The Core Insight

People don't buy products or services. They buy new opportunities. Your job is not to improve on what already exists — it's to create an entirely new vehicle that takes them where they want to go.

The difference between an improvement offer and a new opportunity:
- **Improvement offer:** "Better than what you're using now." — forces the buyer to admit their past decisions were wrong
- **New opportunity:** "Something you haven't tried yet." — no pain of disconnect, no admission of failure, only hope

When someone chooses a new opportunity, they're not saying their old way was stupid. They're saying there's a better path they hadn't found yet. That psychological distinction is everything.

---

## 1. Status — The Only Thing That Makes People Move

Status is the mechanism. Everything else is packaging.

When someone is presented with an opportunity, their subconscious runs one question before anything else:

> "Will this increase my status or decrease it?"

If the answer is increase → they move toward it.
If the answer is decrease → they stop, make excuses, and don't buy.

**Critical distinction:** The status we're talking about is not about what others perceive of you. It's about how you perceive yourself as a human being. The internal story someone tells about who they are.

### The 4 Universal Status Levers

These apply across all markets and buyer types:

1. **Appearance of intelligence** — "This was the smart, non-obvious move"
2. **Appearance of wealth, power, or happiness** — "I'm the kind of person who operates at this level"
3. **Physical appearance** — "This makes me look and feel better"
4. **Style** — "This signals something about my taste and identity" (think MacBook vs. PC)

An offer activates at least one of these. The best offers activate two or three simultaneously.

### Status Works in Both Directions

Status is also what keeps some people from wanting material things at all. Not everyone craves wealth or style. The question is always the same — which status levers matter to this specific buyer? Find those and load your offer with them.

### How to Load the Status-Increase Side

Two tools that always work:
- **Done-for-you delivery** — reduces the effort and risk of looking incompetent ("they handle it; I just benefit")
- **Money-back guarantees** — removes the fear of looking foolish for buying something that doesn't work

Every risk-removal element in an offer is a status protector. Remove the downside and you leave only the upside status gain.

---

## 2. Why People Crave New Opportunities

Three reasons people respond to new opportunities that they don't respond to improvements:

1. **It's a new discovery** — novelty itself has value. New feels like possibility. Familiar feels like more of the same.

2. **No pain of disconnect** — switching from an old vehicle to a new one doesn't require admitting the old vehicle was wrong. There's no psychological cost of switching. Improvement offers force people to say "I was doing it wrong" — new opportunities don't.

3. **Dream replacement (the grass is always greener)** — a new opportunity invokes the possibility of a better future. It's not about the product. It's about the feeling that this time, things could actually be different.

---

## 3. How to Create a New Opportunity (5-Step Process)

### Step 1: Identify the Desired Result

What core market desire is this buyer trying to fulfill?

The three fundamental motivators:
- **Wealth** — ROI, investment returns, capital growth, financial security, income
- **Health** — wellness, vitality, physical environment, energy, longevity
- **Relationships** — belonging, status among peers, family, love, community

Most offers address primarily one. Some blend two. Getting this right determines every word of the marketing.

### Step 2: The 3-Year Question

Use this to surface what the buyer actually wants (not what they say they want):

> "If we were having this conversation three years from today, and you were looking back over those three years — what has to have happened, both personally and professionally, for you to feel happy with your progress?"

This question bypasses surface-level answers and reveals the real dream outcome. Use it in discovery calls, intake forms, and offer design.

### Step 3: Identify the Current Vehicle

Inside each of the three core markets (wealth, health, relationships) are sub-markets, and inside sub-markets are niches. The niche is the vehicle.

What vehicle is the buyer currently using — or has used in the past — to try to get their desired result? What's their history with it? How has it failed them, and what did that failure feel like?

This is the key research question. The current vehicle's failures are the fuel for the new opportunity.

### Step 4: The Opportunity Switch

Don't offer a better version of the vehicle they're already using. Offer an entirely new vehicle.

If they've tried gym memberships and failed, don't sell them a better gym. Sell them a different physical practice entirely.

If they've tried local agencies and gotten burned, don't sell them a better agency. Sell them an outsourced sales partner model — fundamentally different category.

**The switch takes the buyer from pain to hope.** Not "try harder with the same thing" but "here's a way you haven't seen yet."

Rule: if your offer can be described as "a better version of [what they already tried]," it is not an opportunity switch. Reframe or rebuild until it can't.

### Step 5: The Opportunity Stack

Every business has one and only one opportunity switch — the core new vehicle. Everything else builds on top of it.

The opportunity stack is not multiple switches. It's multiple ways to help the buyer go deeper, faster, or further with the same new vehicle. Each offer on the value ladder stacks on top of the same single opportunity.

**Wrong:** Offer 1 is one new vehicle. Offer 2 is a different new vehicle. (Two switches = confused market)

**Right:** Offer 1 is the new vehicle. Offer 2 is a deeper engagement with the same vehicle. Offer 3 is the premium version. One switch, stacked.

## 4. The New Opportunity Applied to Your Own Offer

Before building any client's offer, run these questions on your own business:

1. What vehicle was your dream customer using before they found you?
2. Why was it failing them? (Emotionally, not logically — what did failure feel like?)
3. What is the new vehicle you're offering? Can you state it in one sentence without referencing the old vehicle?
4. Which status levers does your offer activate? (See section 1)
5. What done-for-you elements and guarantees can you add to protect their status?
6. Is your value ladder a stack (one switch, multiple levels) or a mess (multiple switches)?

## 5. More Money from the Same Farm — Frameworks as Multipliers

The core idea: your intellectual framework is not a single product. It's a raw asset that can be packaged, repackaged, and turned into tools that each generate separate revenue.

You either sell information products or you use them as fuel to create desire for your core offer. Either way, one framework monetizes multiple times.

### Why This Works
- Information products indoctrinate buyers about WHY they need your product before they see your pitch
- Giving the framework away at the top of funnel acquires customers for free
- Once you're seen as the creator of the framework, you're an expert, not a commodity

### Three Forms of Any Framework

Every framework can be delivered at three levels. Same intellectual content, different experience, different price:

| Level | Model | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Do It Yourself (DIY)** | Course, guide, templates |
| 2 | **Do It With You (DWY)** | Coaching, cohort, group program |
| 3 | **Do It For You (DIFY)** | Full service, outsourced execution |

Price scales with each level. The framework doesn't change — the experience of consuming it does.

### How to Increase Perceived Value Without Changing Content

Change the medium:
- Written word → audio
- Audio → video
- Video → live experience

Each step up dramatically increases perceived value at minimal marginal cost.

### Framework as Pre-Funnel Content

The framework itself is free marketing:
- **Ad hook** — one step from the framework as the hook pulls people into the funnel
- **Lead magnet** — 2 to 20 minute video teaching one framework step = high-converting free opt-in
- **Indoctrination content** — primes buyers to understand WHY they need your offer before they see the price

### Turning Frameworks into Tools

The more you simplify the execution for the client, the more you get paid. The ladder:

1. Framework → simplifies thinking
2. Tool → simplifies execution (software, physical product, service, supplement)

Your earning ceiling rises with how simple you make the process.

**Brainstorm questions for expanding any offer:**
- Is there software that could automate part of this framework?
- Could we bundle a physical product into this offer?
- What byproducts have we created while implementing this framework that clients would value?
- Are there other info products or services we could bundle?

### The Stack Slide

Goal: show 10x perceived value vs. the price being charged.

Structure:
1. **Core product** — the main thing you sell
2. **Opportunity stack** — everything bundled on top of it

Every item gets a standalone dollar value assigned. The buyer sees: "I'm getting $X,000 in value for $X."

Rules:
- Every item must have a value attached
- Every new offer requires rebuilding the stack slide from scratch
- Items can be: tools, info products, done-for-you components, physical products, bonuses, guarantees, software

## 6. Creating Your Movement — Future-Based Cause

*"Where there is no vision, the people perish."*

The expert's job is to cast a vision of a better future and bring people from where they are to where they want to be. After creating the new opportunity, you must give followers hope — something to believe in — so they become receptive to the change you're offering.

Note: every winning presidential campaign ran on a new opportunity or future-based cause. Every losing campaign ran on improvement slogans.

### Step 1: Run for President of Your Movement

Find one message that works across all communication with your audience. A consistent, constant call to your tribe that reminds them of the vision they've put their faith in.

Ask:
- What do my people really want?
- Where do they want to go?
- How do I capture that in a simple campaign slogan?

Your slogan is not about you. It's a rallying point for them.

### Step 2: Give Them an Identity Shift

After someone joins your movement and takes you up on your new opportunity, the most important thing you can do is shift their identity. Success is more tied to identity than to tactics, skills, or information.

Key rule: don't make the brand name about you. If it's about you, others can't identify with it.

Three tools for the identity shift:

1. **"I am" statement** — a name for your tribe that makes them want to belong. Customers self-identify with your movement. ("I am a funnel hacker." "I am a closer." "I am an OLC partner.")

2. **Title of Liberty / Manifesto** — a rallying declaration that:
   - Reminds them who you are
   - Reminds them who they are
   - Refocuses them on where you're going together
   Use it when they're doubtful. It's the text they can return to.

3. **Consistent re-enrollment** — your job doesn't stop at the first sale. You sell them on the identity over and over. People are real; they drift. Pull them back.

### Step 3: Create Milestone Awards

Set goals your tribe can chase. Create milestone awards they can achieve as they grow inside your new opportunity.

Two journeys happen simultaneously for every member:

1. **Journey of Achievement** — the external path toward the promised result. You guide them through this.

2. **Journey of Transformation** — who they become while on the journey of achievement. Even if they hit the promised result, without this transformation they'll drift away looking for fulfillment elsewhere.

**Liberate and educate.** As people progress on both journeys, give them the opportunity to help others who can't yet help themselves. This is the social mission layer — it locks people into the movement because their identity is now tied not just to their own progress but to the progress of others.

## 7. The Epiphany Bridge

People don't buy logically. They buy based on emotion, then use logic to justify the decision they've already made.

The epiphany bridge is a story that takes people through the same emotional experience that made you believe in the new opportunity. It bridges the gap between your emotional conviction and their current skepticism — without relying on data, logic, or techno-babble.

### How It Works

When you discovered your new opportunity, something happened: a guide gave you an insight, an idea arrived, a conversation changed your thinking. That moment of realization — your epiphany — is what made you move. You didn't need a spreadsheet. You felt it.

The problem: after months or years of living inside the new opportunity, you've accumulated so much knowledge that when you explain it to others, you instinctively reach for the logic. The features, the mechanism, the data. That's techno-babble. And it doesn't sell.

Your audience hasn't had the epiphany yet. You're talking to the version of yourself that existed before the moment of realization. Logic can't get them there. Only story can.

### The Core Mechanism

Status is in play on both sides:
- The buyer is emotionally attracted to the status the new opportunity would give them
- But they need to justify it logically to the people in their life, so they don't lose status with them

Your job: tell the story that creates the emotional state first. Let them find the logic themselves.

If you structure the story correctly, they will have the same epiphany you had — and then sell themselves.

### Three Keys to Effective Epiphany Bridge Storytelling

**1. Oversimplify**
Speak at a third-grade level. Complex language signals status to you; it loses audiences. If the room has to work hard to follow you, you've already lost the emotional thread.

**2. Kinda Like Bridge**
When you need to explain something complex, translate it to something they already know:

> "What are ketones? They're kind of like millions of little motivational speakers running through your body that give you energy and make you feel awesome."

Take the abstract concept → find an analogy they already understand → bridge it.

Rule: every time you hit a word or concept past a third-grade level, stop and run the Kinda Like Bridge.

**3. Make them feel**
Don't describe the scene — put them inside it. Explain how the character felt. When the audience knows what the character is feeling, they start to feel it too.

To land the epiphany, the audience must be in the same emotional state you were in when you received yours. If they're not feeling it, they'll miss the point entirely.

### How to Build Your Epiphany Bridge Story

Start by going back:
- What was happening around you when you had the epiphany?
- Who was the guide — the person, the book, the moment that delivered the insight?
- What did you feel in the seconds before the realization? After?
- What changed forever in that moment?

Those sensory details are the story. Build outward from them.

Then test: if someone who has never heard of your opportunity listens to this story, do they feel what you felt? If not, you're still in techno-babble territory. Strip it back further until the feeling is the only thing left.

---

### The Hero's Journey Framework

Every good story has three elements: **character, desire, conflict.** Little Red Riding Hood wants to take cookies to her grandma — the wolf is the conflict. That's it. Every movie, book, play, and pitch runs on this structure.

Stories actually tell two journeys simultaneously:
- **First journey:** The journey of achievement (external — what the hero does)
- **Second journey:** The journey of transformation (internal — who the hero becomes)

The first is visible. The second is what makes people feel something.

#### Phase 1 — Separation from the Ordinary World (first 25%)

The hero leaves where they started. Two things happen here:

**Build rapport with the hero — make them:**
- A victim of outside forces (we root for them)
- In a difficult situation (we worry about them)
- Likable (we want to be with them)
- Funny (we connect with them)
- Powerful (we want to be like them)

**Introduce the desire — always one of two directions:**
- Toward pleasure: to win or retrieve something
- Away from pain: to escape or stop something

#### Phase 2 — The Journey: Conflict and Villain

The hero has left the ordinary world and is pursuing their desire. Along the way: conflict. The villain's role is to make us root harder for the hero.

Rule: the worse the villain, the stronger the bond between the audience and the hero.

The villain doesn't have to be a person. False beliefs, broken systems, and failing market vehicles all work as villains.

#### Phase 3 — The Mentor, Expert, or Guide

The hero can't reach their desire alone. They meet a guide who gives them the epiphany and the framework.

For Expert Secrets purposes: the guide is the person who delivers the new opportunity. Sometimes it's a person — sometimes it's an impression, a book, a moment of inspiration. The framework received from the guide becomes the hero's plan for defeating the villain.

#### Phase 4 — Achievement and Transformation

The hero finishes the journey. External: they achieved the desire. Internal: they've become a different person. The old identity is dead. The new one has arrived.

This invisible second journey — transformation — is the one audiences feel most deeply, even if they can't articulate why.

---

### The Epiphany Bridge Script

An adaptation of the Hero's Journey. Use it for podcasts, Facebook Live, ads, emails, sales videos, webinars, speaking — any time you tell a story. Seven phases answer 14 questions. Scales from two minutes to two hours depending on how deep you go on each phase.

**Phase 1 — The Back Story**
What is your back story that gives us a vested interest in your journey? Return to the time and place before the epiphany. This is where your audience currently is — anchor them there first.

**Phase 2 — The Desire**
- What do you want to achieve? (Usually: to win, retrieve, escape, or stop something)
- What are your **external** desires? (The journey of achievement — visible, concrete)
- What are your **internal** desires? (The journey of transformation — the fear, the doubt, the shame)
  Note: internal desires are harder to share but build rapport faster than anything else when you do.

**Phase 3 — The Old Vehicles**
What did you try before that didn't work? This is the moment of frustration that made the hero willing to try something new. The old vehicle's failure is what makes the opportunity switch credible.

**Phase 4 — The Journey**
- What was the call or event that started the journey? (Impression, feeling, conversation, event)
- Who or what is the villain? (Often: false beliefs, broken systems, market structures)
- What is the worst-case scenario if the journey fails?

**Phase 5 — The New Opportunity**
- Who was the guide who gave you the epiphany?
- What was the epiphany itself?
- What new opportunity did you create or discover from it? (This is the opportunity switch)

**Phase 6 — The Framework**
- What strategy or framework did you develop?
- What was your plan for applying the new opportunity?
- What results did you get following the framework?
- What results did others get? (Case studies go here)

**Phase 7 — Achievement and Transformation**
- What external result did you accomplish? (The achievement)
- How did you transform internally during the journey? (Who you became)
- What happened after? Show the aftermath — let people see the real outcome.

---

### The 32-Second Epiphany Bridge Script

When time is short, skim the surface of five core steps:
1. The back story
2. The journey
3. The new opportunity
4. The framework
5. The achievement

Same arc, compressed. Use for ad hooks, email openers, Instagram Reels, quick cold outreach.

## 8. False Beliefs and Story Inventory

The only real goal in marketing is to identify the false beliefs customers tell themselves that keep them from success — and rewrite those stories.

If a buyer believes it will work for them, they will do the work. If they don't, they'll start the journey but won't finish it. The number one job of any salesperson or marketer is to get the buyer to believe the new opportunity is the key to their desired result.

### Why False Beliefs Form

When someone has a bad experience, they create a story they tell themselves. That story holds them back. When they encounter your new opportunity, their subconscious immediately scans their existing stories to see if what you're saying fits. If it does, they believe you. If not, they're skeptical — and you need to rewrite the story.

The mechanism: identify the experience that created the false belief → share a story that breaks it → give them an epiphany → new belief forms.

### The 4 Types of False Beliefs (and the Stories That Break Them)

Every buyer has false beliefs in four areas. Each requires a specific type of story:

**1. False belief about the new opportunity itself**
The buyer has tried something before that didn't work and has concluded this category is broken. Your origin story (epiphany bridge) breaks this — it shows how you discovered a different path that the old vehicle couldn't offer. Goal: break the belief that what you're offering is just another version of what failed them.

**2. False belief about the vehicle framework**
Once they understand the opportunity, their next objection is the mechanism. "That might work for some people, but I don't understand how it actually works." Share a story about how you learned the framework, how you earned it, why it works in the real world. Goal: make the mechanism credible, not abstract.

**3. Internal false belief — "I can't do it"**
Even if they believe in the opportunity and the framework, they don't believe they personally can execute. "Others can do this, but not me." Share a story of how you discovered you could do it — and examples of people just like them who did. Goal: break the belief that it requires skills or circumstances they don't have.

**4. External false belief — "Something outside me will stop it"**
The last layer: they believe an outside force (the market, the timing, their partner, some systemic factor) will prevent success even if everything else is right. Share an epiphany bridge story that shows how someone in the same external situation succeeded despite it. Goal: remove the escape hatch of external blame.

### How to Rewrite a False Belief

1. Identify the experience they went through that created the story.
2. Tell a better story that gives them an epiphany and collapses the old belief.

The story must match the belief category exactly. A story about why the vehicle works (category 2) does nothing to break "I personally can't do this" (category 3). Match story to belief type.

### Building the Story Inventory

Work through four columns for each category of false belief:

| Column | What to write |
|---|---|
| **False belief** | The exact story they tell themselves. Write it in their words, not yours. |
| **Experience** | The likely event that created this belief. What happened to them? |
| **Story they tell** | How that experience became a permanent rule in their mind. |
| **Your epiphany bridge** | The story you tell that breaks the rule and installs a new belief. |

Run three rows per category (new opportunity, vehicle, internal, external) = 12 story inventory slots total.

**If you don't have a personal story for a false belief:** use someone else's. A client story, a case study, a third-party example works just as well — as long as the epiphany moment is clear and the listener can see themselves in it.

### The Payoff

A fully built story inventory means no objection surprises you. Every objection a buyer raises maps to one of the four belief categories. You already have the story. You tell it. The objection dissolves not because you argued against it — but because the buyer's internal story just changed.

The better you are at telling the stories, the more effective you become at persuasion — and the less you need to rely on logic, pressure, or discounts.

### Cross-Reference: Hormozi Confirms This Framework

Alex Hormozi's closing framework (see `advertising/sales-closing.md`) maps to the same three axes independently:

| Brunson false belief | Hormozi layer | How it shows up on a sales call |
|---|---|---|
| **External** | Circumstances + Other people | "I can't afford it / no time / I need to ask my spouse" |
| **Internal** | Self | "I'm afraid to make a mistake / not sure it's for me" |
| **Vehicle** | Pre-close / proof phase (opticals) | "I don't know if this works / I've been burned before" |

**Key synthesis:** Brunson's story inventory and Hormozi's call framework are the same tool for two contexts. Build the stories once (Brunson's 12-slot grid). Deploy them in presentations and VSLs (Brunson's sequence: Vehicle → Internal → External) AND in live sales calls (Hormozi's onion: destroy Vehicle belief pre-offer, then handle Circumstances, then Authority, then Self on the call).

## 9. Plugging In Your Offer and Stories — The One-to-Many Presentation Framework

The framework for one-to-many selling takes the Story Inventory (Section 8) and the Stack Slide (Section 5) and loads them into a proven presentation structure. The output is a presentation that can run in a webinar, a live room, a pitch meeting, or a video funnel — same bones, same sequence.

### The Core Rule

Teaching the best content hurts sales. Identifying, breaking, and rebuilding false belief patterns gets people to take action. The presentation is not a class. It is a belief-change sequence that ends with an offer.

### The Three Parts

1. **The Big Domino** — get the audience to believe one thing
2. **The Three Secrets** — break the three false belief categories through story, strategy, and social proof
3. **The Stack and Closes** — present the offer in the precise format proven to convert

### Timing (30-minute version)

| Minutes | Content |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Intro, Big Domino statement, Origin Story (new opportunity belief) |
| 5–10 | Vehicle Framework Story (how the mechanism works) |
| 10–15 | Internal Belief Story (they can execute it) |
| 15–20 | External Belief Stories (nothing outside will stop them) |
| 20–30 | The Stack and Closes |

---

### Part 1 — The Big Domino

The presentation is designed to knock down exactly one domino. Not five. One.

**The domino:** your audience needs to believe that your new opportunity is the key to the result they desire most, and that it is only attainable through your specific vehicle frameworks.

**The domino statement formula:**
> "If I can make people believe that [new opportunity] is the key to [the result they desire most] and is only attainable through [my specific vehicle frameworks], then all other objections and concerns become irrelevant, and they have to give me money."

If the audience truly believes the new opportunity is the only path to what they want, every other objection dissolves. Status, fear, past failures — all secondary once the domino falls.

---

### Part 2 — Slide Sequence

**Slide 1 — The Hook:** "How to [result they desire most] without [the thing they fear most]." No preamble. This is the title. It answers: why should I keep watching?

**Slides 2+ — Intro / Rapport:** Build trust before teaching anything. Blair Warren's five-step persuasion sequence:
1. **Justify their failures** — "If you've failed before, it's not your fault. The information landscape is confusing."
2. **Allay their fears** — "If you've worried you can't do this, I want to put that to rest. You can. You just need the right guide."
3. **Throw rocks at their enemies** — "The [industry incumbent / status quo] wants you to believe you need [barrier]. They're wrong, and they have reasons for wanting you to think that."
4. **Confirm their suspicions** — "If you've suspected that [frustrating system] is rigged against you, you're probably right."
5. **Encourage their dreams** — "That's why we're here. I want to show you how to make it happen."

**Slide 3+ — The Ruler Slide:** Define who this is for. Be inclusive. Name the two types of person in the room (beginner / experienced) and tell each what they'll get. No one should wonder if they're in the right place.

**Slide 4+ — The Big Domino Slide:** State the one belief you need them to walk away with. Make it explicit. "In the next [X] minutes, my goal is to get you to believe that [new opportunity] is the key to [desired result]. I'll show you the frameworks that make it simple to get there."

**Slide 5 — Qualify Yourself:** One or two lines. Where you were (same place they are now). Where you are now (the result). Then move immediately into the Epiphany Bridge origin story — no long credential list, no resume. The story is the qualification.

**Slide 6+ — Epiphany Bridge Origin Story:** Tell the full origin story (Section 7 script). This knocks down the first false belief category: that the new opportunity doesn't work. The story must end with the epiphany, not the outcome. The outcome comes later in the social proof section.

---

### Building the Three Secrets — Step by Step

Before the presentation, you need to identify and prepare the material for all three secrets.

**Step 1 — Identify the three core false beliefs**

Prospects hold false beliefs in three categories. Find the one dominant belief in each:

| Category | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle | "This new opportunity doesn't work / isn't right for me" | "Sales funnels don't work in real estate" |
| Internal | "I don't have the ability to execute this" | "I'm not good at sales / tech / presenting" |
| External | "Outside forces will stop me regardless" | "The market / economy / competition is against me" |

Identify which belief in each category is the core chain holding the big domino up. That's your target.

**Step 2 — Write three epiphany bridge stories**

For each core false belief, find (or create) the epiphany bridge story that breaks it. The story must show the transformation from the false belief to the new belief in a visceral, personal way.

**Step 3 — Rewrite each as a secret**

Turn each false belief + story into a secret phrased as a how-to statement:
- "Secret 1: How to [vehicle outcome] even if [vehicle false belief]"
- "Secret 2: How to [internal outcome] even if [internal false belief]"
- "Secret 3: How to [external outcome] even if [external false belief]"

---

### Delivering the Three Secrets — The 6-Step Flow

Each secret follows the same sequence. Every time. No shortcuts.

1. **State the secret** — name it using the how-to framing
2. **Introduce the framework's name** — give it a proprietary label
3. **Share the epiphany bridge story** — the one you created or earned that breaks this belief
4. **Teach the strategy (the what, not the how)** — show them what to do, never how to do it; the how is what they pay for
5. **Case study slides** — people like them who got the result using this framework
6. **Other supporting epiphany bridge stories** — additional stories that reinforce the new belief

**Note on Secret 1 (Vehicle):** There are two related but distinct stories here. The origin story (told during the intro) is about how you discovered the new opportunity and built your framework. The vehicle story (Secret 1) is about how you developed the framework itself. Don't confuse them — they serve different purposes.

**The Rule on Teaching:** The content section answers "what" — never "how." Teaching tactics kills the close. The desire for the how is the conversion mechanism. Satisfy it prematurely and you lose the sale.

---

### Part 3 — The Stack and Closes

#### The Transition Slide

Before revealing the offer, use a permission-based transition. Ask a question that anchors everything they just learned to their desired outcome:

- "If you used what I just showed you, would that help your business?"
- "Who here wants me to spend 10 minutes showing you how to get all of this?"

This removes pressure, signals a shift, and gets them nodding yes before the offer appears.

#### The Question Slide

Ask for permission to share something that will help them. Then warm them up:
- "How many of you are excited about what we just covered?"
- "Who wants me to walk you through how to get this?"

This pre-frames the close as a favour, not a pitch.

#### The Offer Reveal Sequence

Present slides in this order:

1. **Core product slide** — show a visual of what they're buying
2. **What they'll gain / what they'll eliminate** — move them away from pain, toward pleasure. "When you have this, you'll be able to..." / "You'll finally be able to get rid of..."
3. **The problem this solved for you** — the roadblock you hit trying to get what they want. "When I was figuring this out, I ran into... so I had to create this for myself."
4. **What this saves them** — time and money they won't have to spend learning what you already learned

#### The Stack Slide — Core Mechanic

The stack slide is the most important close mechanic in the entire framework. The principle: **the only thing prospects remember is the last thing you show them.** If the last thing they see is the full offer stack, they anchor the price to total value — not the last item you mentioned.

How to build it:

- Introduce element 1 → tell the story behind how you created/learned/earned it → show element 1 on the stack slide with its standalone value
- Introduce element 2 → same story → add to the stack slide, update total value
- Repeat for every element
- Each time you add an element, the audience sees the cumulative value rise

Never present the price until the full stack slide is showing. The last thing they see before the price is every element, combined, with total value. Price is then revealed as a steep discount against that total.

#### Slide Sequence Within the Close

| Slide | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Core product | Visual of what they're buying |
| Ability / elimination | Desire and pain relief |
| Your roadblock | Empathy and credibility |
| Time/money saved | Justify cost |
| Stack slide (element 1) | Value builds |
| Framework elements | Each added one at a time |
| Case studies | Proof it works for others |
| Who this is for | Requalify the room |
| Destroy the #1 objection | Address the elephant directly — "You might be thinking you can't start because X. Here's why that's a mistake." |
| Full stack slide | Everything combined |
| "If all this did was..." | If-all-this-did statements for each secret. Anchor each outcome independently. |
| Price reveal | Discounted from total stack value |
| Price justification | Compare to retail price or cost of equivalent results |
| Guarantee | Risk reversal — state it clearly and simply |
| "Even if it only does half..." | Minimum-viable-ROI reframe |
| Full recap slide | Entire offer one more time |
| Urgency/scarcity bonus | Time-limited or quantity-limited push to act now |
| Closing CTA | Stays on screen during Q&A — price, countdown, next step |

#### Price Justification Framing

"If you went to my regular site right now you could get this for $X. But because you've invested this time with me and proven you want this result, I'm making you a special offer."

### The 16 Mini-Closes

Mini-closes are short epiphany bridge stories used to break the false belief "I shouldn't buy." Use them throughout the close sequence and during Q&A — not all at once. Pick the ones that match the objections surfacing in the room.

1. **Money is good** — Money is a tool for exchange. You spend it to get something greater in return. Get the prospect to reframe money as a vehicle, not a sacrifice.

2. **Disposable income** — They're already spending on things that don't serve them. Redirect that spend toward growth. Long-term fulfillment vs. short-term comfort.

3. **Money replenishes** — Money comes back every month. Time doesn't. If they don't act, time is what they lose — not money.

4. **Break old habits** — If they leave today without investing, nothing in their life changes. The only variable that produces a different outcome is a different decision.

5. **Information alone** — They've received real value from the content. But information without accountability and coaching produces nothing. That's the gap this fills.

6. **Money or excuses** — Call it gently: they have the money, or they have an excuse. Help them see which one is actually running the show.

7. **Your two choices** — Walk them through what both paths look like. Stay where you are, or move. Make the contrast vivid.

8. **Two choices (cost frame)** — Justify why the investment is priced where it is. Help them understand what it would cost to get these results any other way.

9. **Us vs. them (doers vs. dabblers)** — Some people are here to implement. Others will take notes and never move. Ask them which one they want to be.

10. **The hand hold** — Walk them through the signup process step by step, live. Remove any friction from the mechanics of buying.

11. **Say goodbye** — Show them all the pain that disappears the moment they invest. Make the relief concrete and specific.

12. **Now and later** — Paint their life now vs. what it becomes after they implement. Make the contrast visceral.

13. **Only excuses** — Surface every excuse you've heard and diffuse each one directly. "Some people say X. Here's the truth about that."

14. **Reluctant hero** — Help them believe they can actually do this. The obstacle isn't external — it's the story they're telling about themselves.

15. **If you only got...** — Reference the value they've already received for free in the presentation. "If that's what you got for free, imagine what you'll get when you're inside."

16. **Close close** — The final push. Used repeatedly during Q&A. A pure epiphany bridge story drawn from your story inventory — one that breaks the last remaining false belief about why they shouldn't buy right now.

**Build your own closes:** A close is just an epiphany bridge story targeted at a buying objection. Any story from your story inventory that you didn't use in the presentation can become a close. The best presenters develop a library of 20-30 closes drawn from real conversations with their audience.

### Blair Warren's One-Sentence Persuasion Strategy (Full)

> "People will do anything for those who encourage their dreams, justify their failures, allay their fears, confirm their suspicions, and help them throw rocks at their enemies."

This sentence contains the entire rapport-building sequence. Run it in the intro. Revisit it whenever the audience shows skepticism. It is not manipulation — it is meeting the audience where they actually are.

## 10. Becoming Your Dream Customer's Guide

Your dream customer is stuck in a red ocean — they've tried vehicles that haven't worked, they're frustrated, and they're waiting for a guide to lead them through the challenges ahead.

**Your role is the guide, not the hero.** The customer is the hero. Your brand exists to take them on their hero's journey.

### The Journey Structure

1. **Hook** — grab attention, become the guide who can lead them through their challenges
2. **The Calling (opt-in)** — your first real offer. The deliverable matters less than the commitment they make in deciding to start this journey with you. Give them a lead magnet; they give you their email. They've decided to go on the journey.
3. **The Presentation** — once they join your list, you have the ability to make them a presentation. It does three things:
   - Introduce yourself as their new guide
   - Tell your story to give them an epiphany
   - Present your new opportunity
4. **The Choice** — they accept the new opportunity or not. If yes, they become customers.
5. **The Framework** — deliver the framework and work to ascend them up your value ladder
6. **Transformation** — help them achieve what they desire and become the person they want to become

### The Core Principle

Focus on one funnel until it hits success. Do that by perfecting the presentation and overcoming your audience's objections. Every other move is a distraction until this one works.

## 11. The Live Presentation Funnel (8-Step Sequence)

The operational sequence for running a live webinar. Each step is distinct — don't collapse them.

**Step 1 — Send traffic to the webinar registration page**
- Headline format: "How to [desired result] without [feared obstacle]"
- Curiosity-based headlines throughout — build as much curiosity as possible
- Add a visual that creates curiosity (even one that makes no sense)
- Use urgency and scarcity — these are the real conversion weapons

**Step 2 — Send registrants to a thank-you page with a self-liquidating offer**
- The SLO offsets ad spend
- Presented immediately after registration, before the webinar

**Step 3 — Send a series of indoctrination emails**
- Prime registrants on your origin story, your framework, and the new opportunity
- Build belief before they show up live

**Step 4 — Send reminders**
- Multi-touch reminder sequence leading up to the live event
- Urgency and excitement in each reminder

**Step 5 — Present the webinar live**
- Follow the one-to-many presentation framework (Section 9)
- Big Domino → Three Secrets → Stack and Closes

**Step 6 — Send follow-ups and create last-minute urgency**
- For registrants who didn't attend and attendees who didn't buy
- Replay access + deadline framing

**Step 7 — Close the card**
- Hard deadline on the offer
- Remove access to the replay and special price simultaneously
- Urgency must be real, not manufactured

**Step 8 — Repeat**
- Improve the presentation, test new traffic sources, optimize the SLO
- The funnel is a system. Each run produces data. Use it.

## 12. OLC Method — Applied Framework

**OLC's core market desire:** Wealth (developer ROI, freed capital, next project funded)

**Secondary desire (for certain buyers):** Status — being the developer who had a real sales system, not just a listing on a portal

**Old vehicle (what developers were using):** Local real estate agencies, scattered brokers, ad-hoc commission structure

**Why it was failing them:**
- Agencies list the project, don't own the result
- Can't communicate concept value — treat every property the same
- No exclusivity, no accountability, often working competing listings
- Emotionally: feeling powerless, frustrated, like they've built something great that's being undersold

**OLC's opportunity switch:** Outsourced sales partnership — one partner who designs the offer, builds demand, and closes. Paid only on performance.

This is NOT "a better agency." It is a different category. The developer doesn't hire an agent — they gain a co-owner of the sales outcome.

**Status levers OLC activates for developers:**
- Intelligence: "I chose the smart, non-obvious partner — not just whoever showed up"
- Wealth/power: "I freed capital faster and moved to the next project"
- Style: "I operate at a level where I have a real sales system, not just listings on a portal"

**Status protectors built into the OLC offer:**
- Done-for-you execution (developer approves, OLC does)
- 90-day money-back guarantee
- Real-time campaign dashboard (never look uninformed)
- Exclusive lead clause (protect what you own)

**OLC's opportunity stack:**
1. Free audit (entry point — no risk)
2. Onboarding + offer design (foundation)
3. Full engagement: leads + closing (core)
4. Sales training for internal team (add-on)
5. Profit share on next project (partner tier)

One switch (outsourced sales partner model). Five levels. Each level deepens the same opportunity.

## 13. The Presentation Shortcut — 8 Questions

When you need to build a presentation fast (15 minutes minimum), answer these eight questions in order. The answers become your script.

1. **What's the new opportunity I'm offering?** — Not an improvement. A new vehicle.
2. **What special offer can I create for those who purchase?** — This is your offer stack.
3. **What is the one Big Domino for this offer?** — Frame it as: "How to make at least [result] with [framework]."
4. **What is my epiphany bridge origin story to knock down the Big Domino?** — Your story of how you discovered/proved this works.
5. **What framework am I teaching, and what false belief am I breaking about the vehicle?** — List: Framework name, false belief, the truth, the story that breaks it, the title.
6. **What framework am I teaching, and what false belief am I breaking about internal beliefs?** — Same structure: framework, false belief, truth, story, title.
7. **What framework am I teaching, and what false belief am I breaking about external beliefs?** — Same structure: framework, false belief, truth, story, title.
8. **How can I structure the stack and close to increase conversions?** — Work the stack slide sequence. Build value before revealing price.

**Note:** Titles are the most important element. Spend disproportionate time getting them right.

### When the Presentation Isn't Working

Three root causes only:

1. You picked a bad market — no one wants what you're offering.
2. You built an improvement offer, not a new opportunity.
3. You slipped into teaching tactics before selling your audience on the strategy (the vehicle).

Fix the market, the opportunity switch, or the belief-breaking sequence — in that order.

## 15. Hooks, Ads, and the Guide's Role in the Customer Journey

> **Format note:** The "Perfect Webinar" and "live presentation funnel" are Brunson's original containers. The frameworks inside them — hook sources, Epiphany Bridge Story structure, SOAP opera email arc, Three Secrets, Stack — apply equally to any format: short-form video (Reels, TikTok, Facebook Live), long-form video, email sequences, newsletters, LinkedIn posts, sales pages, DM follow-up cadences. When references to "webinar" appear in this document, read them as "presentation" and choose the format that fits the audience and platform.

### Where Hooks Come From

Every message you post and every ad you create draws from the same pool of source material. The personality and stories of the guide are woven into all of it.

Pull hook ideas from:

1. Epiphany bridge stories
2. Frameworks you teach
3. The red ocean you're throwing rocks into (the old, broken vehicle)
4. Your new opportunity
5. Elements of your future-based cause
6. Your platform (the movement you're building)
7. Identity shifts ("you are now someone who...")
8. Manifesto statements
9. Milestone awards (proof of transformation at each step)
10. Your offers
11. Your presentations

### The Three Most Profitable Ad Types

All other ad formats are worth testing. These three are where the money consistently comes from:

1. **Epiphany Bridge Story ads**
2. **Email epiphany follow-up funnels (SOAP opera sequences)**
3. **Live presentations repurposed as ads** (Facebook Lives, webinar replays)

Most ads are simply a story about how you created the lead magnet, webinar, book, or whatever the CTA points to. You tell the story — you don't explain the product.

### Epiphany Bridge Story Ads — The 5 Phases

Every story ad follows this sequence, in order:

1. **Backstory** — where the dream customer was before (the pain, the failed attempts, the frustration)
2. **Journey** — what they tried that didn't work; the villain is always a false belief or broken vehicle, never a person
3. **New opportunity** — the moment the old path was abandoned and something different appeared
4. **Framework** — the decision or mechanism that made the shift possible
5. **Achievement** — where they are now; stated plainly, no hype

End with a CTA: "Click to get access to the framework."

The story is about them, not you. You are the guide. They are the hero.

### SOAP Opera Email Sequences

The perfect webinar framework broken into an email sequence. Used when someone first joins your list — these are the emails that run immediately after the opt-in.

The name comes from the hook at the end of each episode: every email teases the next one so subscribers wait anxiously for it.

**Emotional structure across 9 emails:**

| Emails | Focus | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Emotion | Deliver the presentation — one core story per email, building toward the offer. Email 5 introduces the Stack. |
| 6–7 | Logic | Justify the decision with facts, data, results, proof. Why this is the rational choice. |
| 8–9 | Fear | Urgency and scarcity close the sequence. What they lose if they don't move. |

**Email-by-email breakdown:**

- **Email 1:** Open with the hook that got them on the list. Set the stage. Tease email 2 ("tomorrow I'm going to show you why everything you've been told about [topic] is wrong").
- **Email 2:** Origin story — your epiphany bridge. The moment you discovered the new opportunity. Ends with the vehicle secret teased.
- **Email 3:** Vehicle secret story — how the framework works in plain language. Breaks the false belief about the vehicle. Teases internal belief email.
- **Email 4:** Internal belief story — a person who thought "this isn't for someone like me" and did it anyway. Breaks self-doubt. Teases external belief email.
- **Email 5:** External belief story + **Stack introduction** — breaks the "the market / timing / circumstance won't allow it" belief. Then presents the full offer stack for the first time. This is the emotional close.
- **Email 6:** Logic — ROI breakdown, time saved, cost of inaction. Reference the stack value vs. the price.
- **Email 7:** More logic — case study, testimonial, or third-party proof. Make the rational case.
- **Email 8:** Fear — "Here's what happens if you don't do this." Not manipulation — the real cost of staying where they are.
- **Email 9:** Final urgency — deadline, price increase, or access closure. Last chance to act.

**The guide's role throughout:** You hook them in the ad. You give them the call to start the journey (opt-in). You present the new opportunity. You guide them through each belief shift. You help them achieve the transformation they want most.

You are never the hero of these emails. The reader is the hero. You are the guide who has walked this path before and knows the way through.
