# Paid Ads

*Sources: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads book, Part 4 — "Making an Ad"; acquisition.com Leads Course, Paid Ads Module)*

Platform-agnostic ad creation framework. For Meta-specific execution tactics (creative formats, headline hacks, scaling mechanics), see `facebook-ads.md`. For landing page specifics, see `landing-pages.md`.

---

## What It Is

> "Advertising is the only casino where, with enough skill, you become the house."

Paid ads = one-to-many communication to cold audiences. You pay a platform to put your offer in front of their audience. You rent eyeballs. Reach is guaranteed — unlike content, you pay for it upfront.

This is the 4th and final of the Core Four (see `hormozi-advisor/knowledge/leads.md`). Do warm outreach, content, and cold outreach first — each builds the data and cash flow you need to run ads efficiently.

The reach is guaranteed, getting your money back isn't — so it's a game of efficiency, not reach. If an ad is unprofitable, it's almost always because the right people never saw it. Fix who sees it before you fix the ad.

---

## The Core Equation

```
Output = Reach × Efficiency
```

All advertising works. The only question is how efficiently you extract value from eyeballs versus what you paid to reach them. Efficiency matters more than creativity.

## The 4 Problems Paid Ads Create (and how to solve each)

### Problem 1: Where to Advertise

Pick a platform where all 4 are true:
1. You've used it as a consumer — you know the format, your ads won't look alien
2. You can target your specific audience on it
3. You can format ads natively (ads that look like content outperform ads that look like ads)
4. You can afford the minimum spend

**How to pick placement:**
- Start where your competitors advertise — if they're running there profitably, so can you
- Most platforms have an ads library — find the longest-running, highest-engagement ads in your space
- Transcribe 50 of them. Study: how they hook, how they retain, what they offer, what language they use
- Don't copy one — absorb 50 and write your own version
- Replicate before you iterate. Once you match their performance, then get creative.

**Action:** Start with one platform. Watch and click every ad in your industry until your feed is full of them. That's your real-time school.

---

### Problem 2: Getting the Right Audience (Targeting)

Two targeting methods — use separately or combined:

**1. Lookalike audience**
Upload your existing lists (customers first, then schedule calls, then opted-in leads). Platform finds more people like them. Higher-quality list = higher-quality output.

**2. Factor targeting**
Age, gender, income, interest, location, time — layer filters on top of your lookalike audience to narrow further.

**Targeting rules:**
- Start narrow (puddle), then expand as ROI funds wider targeting
- Local businesses: geography alone is already narrow — add max 1-2 filters, no more
- Local ads benefit from inherent trust ("I know where that is") — leverage it
- If ads aren't working: the right people aren't seeing them. Fix targeting before fixing the ad.

**Meta-specific addendum:** on Meta, broad targeting with exceptional creative often beats narrow targeting with weak creative — the ad itself does the targeting once it's good enough. See `facebook-ads.md` Hack 5 (Broad Targeting + Super Specific Creative) and the Advantage+/Andromeda mechanism for why this works on that platform specifically.

**Action:** Consolidate all your lists (past customers, warm leads, cold outreach contacts). Upload the highest-quality segment first as a lookalike seed.

### Problem 3: What Should My Ad Say

Every ad has 3 parts: **Call-out → Value → Call to Action** (same as hook → retain → reward)

#### Part A: The Call-Out (most important)
Spend 80% of your effort on the first 5 seconds. If you nail the hook, almost every ad works.

The purpose of each second of an ad is to sell the next second. The headline is the first sale — test it the most.

**The cocktail party effect:** even in a room full of noise, one specific thing still cuts through and grabs attention. A call-out harnesses this to cut through the noise of the feed. Call-outs range from hyper-specific (grabs one person) to totally generic (grabs everyone) — pick based on how narrow your audience is.

4 call-out types that work:

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Labels** | "Clark County moms", "gym owners", "remote workers" — words they self-identify with |
| **Yes questions** | "Do you wake up sweating at night? Do you toss and turn?" — questions they nod to |
| **If-then statements** | "If you run over $10K/month in ads, I can save you 20%+" |
| **Ridiculous results** | "Gym opens first day, overfull, had to refund customers" — bizarre, specific, true |

Visual call-outs (beyond words):
- **Contrast** — bright colors, movement, anything that stands out in the feed
- **Likeness** — show people who look like your target customer, not just you (if your customers are diverse, your ads should be too)
- **Scene** — visually show the problem (person tossing in bed = sleep issues, room full of clutter = organizational problem)

**Production system that scales:**
Record 10 pieces of value content (the "meat"). Record 30 different hooks (5-second call-out clips). Combine = 300 ad variations to test in a matter of hours. Find the best hook first, then find which value content converts best off that hook.

**Action:** Stop skipping ads. Watch them for the call-out. Become a student of the game — the goal is that for the rest of your life, when you see an ad, you turn the volume up instead of scrolling past.

*Cross-reference: Sabri's High-Value Dobie framework (`facebook-ads.md`) and NEUE Wave / 9 Questions (`copywriting.md`) are complementary lenses on the same call-out/hook problem — no single winner, use whichever generates the angle you're missing.*

#### Part B: The Value Content (retain)

Use the **What-Who-When framework**:

**What — the 8 value drivers** (4 positive + 4 negative, from the value equation)

| Positive (carrot) | Negative (stick) |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Nightmare |
| Perceived likelihood of success | Risk of failure |
| Speed / fast results | Slow / never get there |
| Easy / no skill needed | Hard / requires sacrifice |

Show and tell both sides. The best ads magnify the benefits AND show the cost of not acting.

**Who — perspectives that give or take status**

Motivation comes from deprivation. You don't know which relationship your prospect is starving for status in — so hit as many as possible: spouse, kids, parents, boss, colleagues, rivals, competitors.

Same benefit, different perspective:
- "You fit into your suit again" (self)
- "Your wife sees the belt loop and says you look great" (spouse)
- "Your colleague comes back from a trip and says you look different" (work rival)
- "You can run with your kids instead of watching from the sideline" (parent)

**When — past, present, future timeline**

For each benefit and each relationship, show it across time:
- Past: "You remember when this was easy..."
- Present: "Right now, every morning you..."
- Future positive: "Six months from now, when you..."
- Future negative: "If you keep going as you are, five years from now..."

**Combining it:** 8 value drivers × multiple perspectives × 3 time frames = unlimited ad angles from a single product.

#### Part C: The Call to Action (reward)

Clear, not clever. They're interested for a short window — tell them exactly what to do next.

- "Click this button"
- "Reply YES"
- "Call this number"
- "Go to [URL]"
- "Scan this QR code"

Keep CTAs simple: easy numbers, obvious buttons, short memorable URLs.
(80% of acquisition.com traffic comes from people hand-typing the domain — short domains are underpriced assets.)

Layer urgency, scarcity, and bonuses into the CTA itself to push action now instead of later.

## Part 2: The Money

Efficiency matters more than creativity. All advertising works — the only real difference between ads is how efficiently each one converts spend into return. 4 big questions to answer: how much do I spend, how well am I doing, why aren't my ads profitable, and what do I do about it. The rest of this section answers each.

### The Metrics That Matter

**LTGP** = Lifetime Gross Profit (not LTV, not revenue — gross profit after cost of goods)
**CAC** = Cost to Acquire Customer

```
LTGP > CAC = making money
LTGP < CAC = losing money
LTGP ÷ CAC ≥ 3 = scalable
LTGP ÷ CAC ≥ 10 = home run (Hormozi's internal target)
```

Common mistake: tracking LTV instead of LTGP. A competitor with $500 LTV at 10% margin makes $50 in gross profit. If CAC is $100, they're losing money — even though the revenue "looks good." Gross profit is the number that matters because it's the actual money you have to acquire customers, pay rent, cover payroll, and run the business — revenue isn't.

⚠️ **Tension / correction:** `marketing-strategy.md` uses a straight LTV:CAC ratio (target 3:1). LTGP:CAC is more accurate because it's gross-profit-based, not revenue-based — two businesses with identical LTV can have wildly different LTGP depending on margin. Use LTGP:CAC when you have margin data; fall back to LTV:CAC only as a rough proxy.

**The real differentiator:** Within any industry, CAC only varies 1-3x between competitors. But LTGP can vary 10-50x. The winner isn't the best advertiser — it's the best business model. Fix the business model first.

**Two levers, and which one to pull:**
- Lower CAC — cheaper customers, more efficient ads
- Raise LTGP — make more per customer, via a better business model

For maximum money, work both. To know which one needs the work right now: research your industry's average CAC.
- Your CAC is below 3x the industry average → focus on the business model (LTGP side)
- Your CAC is above 3x the industry average → focus on advertising (CAC side)

Rule of thumb: lowering CAC by $100 eventually takes more work than making an extra $100 off the same customer. Once CAC is low enough, the business model is where the remaining money is.

### The 3 Phases of Spending

**Phase 1: Track money**
Set up tracking before spending a dollar. Without tracking, you can't see what's working. Running ads blind is the fastest way to get cleaned out.

**Phase 2: Lose money**
You will lose more times than you win. That's the game. Of 300 ads tested, maybe 5 are ROI-positive and 1 is a home run. The home run pays for everything. Stop stopping when you find a winner — that's where you double down.

**Test-spend budget rule:** cap each new ad's test spend at 2x the gross profit one customer generates in their first 30 days. Example: if a customer nets $100 in the first 30 days, let an ad run up to $200 in spend as long as it's still producing leads. If it hasn't produced a single lead before hitting that ceiling, shut it off.

**Phase 3: Print money**
Once LTGP > CAC, spend as much as you possibly can. If a machine gives you $10 for every $1 you put in, your budget is every dollar you can find. Stop asking "how much should I spend" — ask "how many customers do I want, or can I handle."

**Reverse-budgeting from the goal:** budget = customers wanted × CAC, padded ~20% since ads get less efficient as they scale. Example: want 100 customers next month, CAC is $100 → $10,000 base, padded to $12,000 over 30 days = $400/day. Set the daily budget from the goal, then commit to it — if the number feels scary, that's a sign you're doing it right.

### Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)

The goal beyond "3:1 LTGP:CAC" is breaking even within the first 30 days.

**Why:** If you break even in month 3, you're cash-flow negative for 3 months before you can reinvest. If you break even on day 1, you can recycle the cash immediately — and compound every cycle.

**How:** Add an upsell that covers your CAC upfront.

Example:
- $15/month membership, $5 COGS = $10 gross profit/month
- 10-month average = $100 LTGP
- CAC = $30 → 3.3:1 ratio (good), but 3-month payback (slow)
- Add: $100 one-time upsell, 20% take rate = $20 average uplift per customer
- $10 (month 1) + $20 (upsell) = $30 = break even day 1
- Now you recycle the cash immediately and compound monthly

At 2:1 in 30 days, reinvesting every month: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 32... month 12 = 4,000x customers.

**Upsell ideas:** More of what they bought (bigger, longer), better version, complementary product (burger → fries → Coke → supersize).

### Why Ads Hit a Ceiling (and how to fix it)

The platform didn't cap you — your ad did. Your ad works for a warm-ish audience and stops converting as it reaches colder people.

Fix: split-test more. Top 1% of advertisers split-test 11x more content than everyone else. Make more hooks. More value variations. More angles.

**The cap is your creative output, not the platform's limit.**

---

### How to Diagnose What's Broken

Work backward through the funnel. Each step breaks in sequence:

1. **No clicks** → ad problem (call-out, targeting)
2. **Clicks but no opt-ins** → landing page problem (mismatch with ad, too many steps)
3. **Opt-ins but no shows** → follow-up problem
4. **Shows but no closes** → sales problem, NOT an ad problem

If the right people are on the phone and not closing, fix your sales. Don't blame the ads. If engaged leads have the problem you solve and the money to spend, and still aren't buying, that's a sales problem wearing an ad-spend costume — don't confuse the two.

---

### Where to Find Your Next Winning Ad

- **Your best free content makes your best paid ads.** If a piece of organic content already generated sales or performed well, 9 times out of 10 it'll make a killer ad. Test proven organic before writing from scratch.
- **User-generated content compounds.** If customers create testimonials or reviews of their own accord, post them. If they perform as free content, they usually perform as ads too. Build a system that reliably prompts customers to post — it becomes a steady stream of ad material.

## Action Checklist

- [ ] Pick one platform (you use it, your audience is on it, competitors advertise there)
- [ ] Study 50 competitor ads: transcribe hook, value elements, CTA, language used
- [ ] Upload your lists to create a lookalike audience
- [ ] Add 1-2 targeting filters on top (more only if large national audience)
- [ ] Record 10 value content pieces + 30 hooks = 300 ad variations
- [ ] Build a simple landing page that matches your ad (see `landing-pages.md`)
- [ ] Set up tracking before spending anything
- [ ] Set an acceptable monthly loss budget — expect to lose it, you're learning not earning yet
- [ ] Calculate your LTGP (gross profit, not revenue) and your 30-day gross profit per customer
- [ ] Cap each new ad's test spend at 2x that 30-day gross profit figure
- [ ] Check your CAC against the industry average (3x rule) to know whether to fix ads or fix the business model
- [ ] Set a target: LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 to start, 10:1 to scale
- [ ] Design a day-1 upsell to achieve client-financed acquisition
- [ ] Pull your best-performing organic content and customer UGC as ad candidates
- [ ] When you find a winner: scale it hard, reverse-budget from your customer goal, don't stop
- [ ] If you haven't run a single ad yet: place one for $100 this week. Don't chicken out at the last step.
