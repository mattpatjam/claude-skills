# Sales & Closing Techniques

*Sources: Sabri Suby*

---

## Core Philosophy

Sales is not about convincing or pushing. It is about pulling. The prospect should arrive at the decision themselves — your job is to ask the right questions, remove resistance, and guide them to a conclusion they already sense is correct.

The higher the ticket price, the more you pull. The lower the ticket, the more push is required. High-value sales calls are about earning trust, uncovering the real problem, and making inaction feel more dangerous than the investment.

**Warning:** These tactics are powerful and can be misused. Only apply them when you genuinely believe the product or service will transform the prospect's life. Never use them to sell something that doesn't deliver.

---

## The 7 Tactics

### Tactic 1: Kill the Comfort Zone

Most salespeople try to keep prospects comfortable throughout the call. This is the wrong objective.

**The truth:** Transformations don't happen inside comfort zones. Every client who made a life-changing decision did so at a moment of discomfort — when they were pushed to confront the real issue.

- Ask the difficult questions. Go deep. Don't navigate around the painful truth.
- Ask for the sale multiple times. Don't stop at the first "no" or the first hesitation.
- 10 minutes of discomfort on a call can change 10 years of someone's life.
- Prospects will fight to stay in their comfort zone even when what they're fighting for is keeping them stuck.

**Only valid when:** You have done your qualification work and you genuinely, whole-heartedly believe this is the best solution for this person. Then you have no excuse NOT to push.

### Tactic 2: Radical Transparency

Never hide the negatives. Bring them up yourself, before the prospect can raise them as objections.

**Why it works:**
- Pre-handles objections: turns live resistance into resolved concerns
- Builds instant trust: "this person isn't commission-hungry, they're being honest"
- Stops comparison shopping: "this is the only person shooting me straight"
- Makes you the trusted advisor, not another salesperson

**How to apply:**
- Identify the 1–3 biggest drawbacks of your offer (long contract, requires client effort, time to results)
- Raise them early — within the first few minutes
- Frame them with reasoning: "This is a 12-month contract. SEO is a long game. If you're not ready to commit 12 months, I'd recommend Google Ads instead — it'll get you results faster."

Example from Sabri: When selling SEO, he would lead with the 12-month contract. Prospects who might have objected later accepted it on the front end because the reasoning was logical and honest. It disarmed the objection and built rapport simultaneously.

**The psychology:** When you're completely upfront about one drawback, the prospect's brain assumes there are no hidden surprises. Their defense system lowers.

### Tactic 3: Pull, Don't Push

Pushing creates resistance. Pulling creates desire.

- The harder you push, the less people want it. A voice in the back of their head says: "Why are they working so hard to sell this? It must not be good."
- The higher the price point, the more the pull dynamic applies.
- Ferrari doesn't sell to you — Ferrari qualifies you. You justify yourself to them.

**The pull method:**
1. Ask strategic questions to unearth the root problem
2. Let the prospect arrive at the decision themselves
3. You guide them to the "admission" — the moment they acknowledge their current approach isn't working

**Key questions to open with:**
- "What led you to book this call in the first place?"
- "How long have you been trying to solve this problem?"
- "What solutions have you already tried, and why do you think they didn't work?"

Once you understand all that, you can say: *"Based on everything you've told me, I think you'd be a good fit for what we do."* — not "This is perfect for you" before you even know their situation.

**The admission:** Get them to state out loud that their current situation is not working. Once they say it, they own it. The decision to change becomes their idea, not a sale you pushed on them.

### Tactic 4: Cost of Inaction

Prospects fear spending money. But the real danger is NOT taking action.

Reframe the entire conversation: the question is not "can I afford this?" but "what does staying where I am cost me?"

**The framework:**
- Assign a specific dollar cost to inaction over time
- "Every year you stay where you are, this problem costs you $X"
- "What's more expensive — our fee, or $2M in lost revenue over the next 2 years?"
- Time is the enemy: the longer they wait, the bigger the problem grows

**The psychology:** This shifts the risk in the prospect's mind. Your offer stops being the risky option — inaction becomes the risky option.

**How to apply on a call:**
- Once you understand their current situation and target, quantify the gap
- Assign a time dimension: what does staying stuck cost per month/year?
- Then position your offer against that cost, not against the absolute price

### Tactic 5: Tonality

People make subconscious judgments about you within 2–5 seconds of hearing your voice. The words you choose matter far less than how you say them.

**The authority tonality:**
- Slower pace
- Flatter delivery — minimal upward inflection
- Measured, calm, deliberate
- Like someone in a position of authority (customs officer, doctor, consultant) — not an enthusiastic salesperson

**What NOT to do:**
- Fast, upbeat, enthusiastic opening → immediately signals "salesperson" → defenses go up
- Upward inflection at the end of statements (sounds uncertain or eager)
- Talking too much too fast

**The frame shift:** You are not there to sell — you are there to assess whether this person is a good fit. That posture changes your entire tonality naturally.

Sabri's example: calling as a cold caller initially got resistance. When he called back as if he was checking on an existing account, the tonality changed and so did the outcome — same words, completely different response.

### Tactic 6: Vision Selling

Sell the destination, not the deliverables.

Nobody wants the 12-hour flight. They want the beach. Nobody wants "1 hour coaching + macro plan." They want to drop 3 dress sizes.

**The mistake:** Most salespeople justify the price by walking through every deliverable — modules, PDFs, hours of training. This is actually counterproductive. Deliverables signal work. People don't want work. They want the outcome.

**How to do it:**
- Paint a vivid picture of the desired end state
- Ask: "What would your life look like once you've solved this?"
- Ask: "What would [the outcome] unlock for you that isn't possible right now?"
- Make the outcome feel real — specific, sensory, tangible
- Everything else (deliverables, modules, process) becomes the vehicle. Mention it briefly, don't dwell on it.

**The psychology:** The prospect's brain cannot cleanly distinguish between vividly imagined futures and real ones. When you paint the picture in enough detail, they're already emotionally living in that future state — and the purchase becomes the bridge to get there.

## Quick Reference: The Sales Call Framework

```
1. Tonality from the first word — authority, not enthusiasm
      ↓
2. Strategic questions — unearth the root problem
   "What brought you here?" / "How long has this been a problem?" / "What have you tried?"
      ↓
3. The admission — get them to state that their current situation isn't working
      ↓
4. Radical transparency — raise the objections before they do
      ↓
5. Vision selling — paint the destination vividly
      ↓
6. Cost of inaction — assign a dollar cost to staying where they are
      ↓
7. Pull, don't push — "Based on what you've told me, I think you'd be a good fit"
      ↓
8. Kill the comfort zone — push past hesitation (only when you believe in the product)
      ↓
9. Follow-up system — the fortune is in the follow-up
```

## Applicability

- **Sales calls (OLC Close):** All 7 tactics apply directly
- **Sales training for clients (e.g. Nirvana Health Club):** Teach tactics 1–6 as core framework. Radical transparency and pull/push are the most counter-intuitive and highest value.
- **Written copy / VSLs:** Vision selling and cost of inaction transfer directly to written format
