# Offer Creation

*Sources: Sabri Suby*

---

## Core Principle

Nothing happens in a business until something is sold. Customer acquisition is the most important activity. Everything else — website, branding, delivery — is secondary to getting customers in the door.

The business that can spend the most to acquire a customer wins. That means: maximize LTV, minimize payback period. See `marketing-strategy.md` for the CAC/LTV/Payback framework.

---

## The Godfather Mindset

Before building the offer:

1. Sit down and write the most **ridiculous** offer you could ever go to market with — one so good that 9 out of 10 people in your audience would grab their credit card as you described it
2. Then tone it back to something you can actually deliver
3. This forces you to think about maximum value first, then constrain to reality — rather than starting conservative and never getting compelling

---

## The Godfather Offer (7 Parts)

### Part 1: Rationale
Why does this offer even exist? Why are you able to deliver it at this price or on these terms?

Without a rationale, an aggressive offer looks like a scam. Prospects will think "this is too good to be true" and dismiss it. The rationale makes it believable.

Examples:
- "We can offer this price because we cut out the middlemen and source direct from the manufacturer"
- "This is only available to 10 clients because it requires direct access to our team"
- "We can do this for free because we only charge on results — our model depends on you winning first"

### Part 2: Build Value
A transaction happens when perceived value exceeds price. Your job is to stack perceived value as high as possible before revealing the price.

Tactic: **everyday price anchoring**
- Show what this would normally cost (agency retainer, hiring in-house, standard market rate)
- Show what comparable solutions charge
- Build the value stack before the ask

### Part 3: Aggressive Price (Loss Leader)
Lead with the most aggressive price you can sustain. Make the bulk of the money on the backend — upsells, LTV, repeat purchases.

Why this works:
- One simple, compelling price removes decision friction
- Prospects value today's money infinitely more than tomorrow's money
- A low front-end price maximizes the number of people who enter your world
- Supermarkets sell nappies near break-even because once people are in the store, they buy everything else

The goal of the front-end offer is to bring people into your world. Profit comes from the lifetime relationship.

⚠️ **Tension — unresolved, flag for testing:** Hormozi (`hormozi-advisor/knowledge/offers.md` § "Charge What It's Worth") argues the opposite: price to the value delivered, not the cheapest entry point, because low price anchors low perceived quality. Neither source wins by default — Sabri's loss-leader model assumes strong backend upsell mechanics to recover margin; Hormozi's premium model assumes the offer stack alone justifies the price. Test both against the specific business's margin structure and backend before picking one. Don't silently default to either.

### Part 4: Payment Plans
Always offer payment plans. Split the total price into monthly terms (30 / 60 / 90 days or 12 months).

Why: People value today's money far more than future money. A $2,400 offer becomes "just $200/month" — the same outcome but the psychological cost of the decision drops dramatically. More yeses = more customers = lower effective CAC.

### Part 5: Premiums
Add-ons given for free alongside the main offer. Must be so valuable that someone could justify buying purely for the premium.

Key principle: **Give away information, sell the implementation.**
- Information is now free (ChatGPT, YouTube, Google)
- What people pay for is having someone do it for or with them
- Your free bonuses should be better than what competitors charge for

### Part 6: Power Guarantee
Every transaction involves risk — either the prospect carries it or you do. Most businesses make prospects carry all the risk. That inflates CAC.

**Guarantee types:**
- **Money-back** — full refund if not satisfied (standard)
- **Time-limited money-back** — 30 / 60 / 90 days to change mind
- **Conditional guarantee** — do everything we say; if it doesn't work, full refund
- **Done-for-you guarantee** — if you don't get the result, we keep working for free until you do

**Key insight:** In most countries, consumer law already gives customers a guarantee by default. If you're already honoring refund requests informally, you are already paying the cost of a guarantee — just without using it as a marketing tool on the front end.

**Benchmark:**
- Refund/claim rate under 3% = healthy. Offer is working, not over-promised.
- Under 1% = your guarantee isn't aggressive enough. Push harder.
- Over 3% = claims are over-promised or the offer needs tightening.

**If you don't have a guarantee:** Reduce your current CAC by ~60% — that's approximately what you're overpaying because risk-averse prospects don't convert.

### Part 7: Scarcity
Offers without scarcity don't convert. Without urgency, prospects delay — and delay is the death of the sale.

**Rules:**
- Scarcity must be genuine. People sniff test fake countdown timers immediately.
- Every business has natural scarcity: limited inventory, limited client slots, limited production capacity, time-bound pricing.
- Find what is naturally scarce in your business and advertise it explicitly.

## The Value Ladder Principle

The front-end offer (aggressive price / loss leader) is the bait. The money is in the backend:
- Upsells and cross-sells at point of purchase
- Setup fees and phase fees in service businesses
- Renewals and repeat purchases in product businesses
- Premium tiers and done-for-you offers

**One-click upsell** is the single fastest way to shorten payback period for e-commerce. Immediately after purchase, offer more of what they just bought (or a complementary product) at a discounted rate — one click, no re-entering payment details.

## Application: OLC Method — Godfather Offer (Session 3 Ready)

*Built for Nirvana Session 3 (~July 7). Performance-based outsourced sales model for Bali/Lombok developers.*

**1. Rationale**
"We only make money when you do. No retainer. No upfront risk. We built this model because the standard agency model is broken — most sales agencies get paid every month whether they close deals or not. That's not aligned with your interests. Our incentives are completely aligned: if you don't close, we don't get paid."

**2. Value Stack (anchor before revealing the model)**
Before mentioning the performance %, show what alternatives cost:
- In-house sales director: $5,000–8,000/month salary + benefits + management time + 3–6 month ramp
- Sales agency retainer: $3,000–5,000/month + success fee on top
- Doing it yourself: your time at senior-manager hourly rate × hours spent = real cost
- OLC: 0 upfront. % of closed deals only. You pay from money you've already received.

**3. Aggressive Price**
Performance-only IS the most aggressive price possible — zero until results. Define: % of gross revenue per closed deal (or per reservation deposit received). The ask is not "pay us" — it's "let us prove it first."

**4. Payment / Invoicing Terms**
No payment plans needed (no upfront). Define: invoiced monthly on confirmed closed deals. Payment due within 14 days of invoice. Triggers: signed SPA or reservation deposit cleared — not verbal commitment.

**5. Premiums (Bonuses — each named and valued)**
- Grand Slam Offer design session (normally a standalone $2,000 engagement)
- Sales system build: scripts, objection handlers, follow-up sequence (normally $1,500)
- Weekly performance report: pipeline, conversion rate, deal stage breakdown
- Access to buyer co-broker network (Matthieu's existing RE contacts = warm buyer pipeline)

**6. Power Guarantee**
The model IS the guarantee. "If we don't close, you owe us nothing." But add a secondary commitment: "If we don't book your first qualified buyer appointment within 14 days of launch, we'll give you a written breakdown of exactly what's not working and what to fix — at no charge."

**7. Scarcity**
"We take a maximum of 3 developer clients at a time. This is a real constraint — we're closing deals personally, not delegating to a junior team. Right now we have [X] slot available." Must be true. Do not fabricate.

## Application: ScaleRise — Godfather Offer (Post-STCC)

*To be applied after STCC case study is documented. Currently premature.*

**1. Rationale**
"We built this system for ourselves first — to run Scalrize's own content and lead follow-up without a full team. Once it worked, we productized it. You're getting something that's been running in production, not a prototype."

**2. Value Stack**
- Content manager: CHF 4,000–6,000/month
- Marketing automation setup: CHF 2,000–5,000 one-time
- Ongoing ops + reporting overhead: 5–10 hours/week of senior time
- ScaleRise: CHF 495/month, 3 workflows running in 30 days

**3. Aggressive Price**
CHF 495/month is already low. Front-end: consider "first workflow free" to demonstrate output quality before commitment. Backend: upsell to additional workflows, annual plan, or dedicated account management.

**4. Payment Plans**
- Monthly: CHF 495/month
- Annual: CHF 4,950 (2 months free) — higher LTV, lower churn, easier to manage

**5. Premiums**
- White-label interface at scalrize.com/app
- Onboarding SOP (set up in 5 days or less)
- 30-day support window post-launch
- First month performance report showing time saved and output volume

**6. Power Guarantee**
"If the first workflow doesn't save you at least 5 hours in the first 30 days, we'll refund your first month. No questions." Track the hours from day 1. Document it. Make refund threshold real and measurable.

**7. Scarcity**
"We onboard a maximum of 2 new clients per month to maintain quality. After that, you're on a waitlist." Must be enforced.

## Quick Reference: Godfather Offer Checklist

Before going to market with any offer:

- [ ] **Rationale** — can you explain clearly why this offer exists and is believable?
- [ ] **Value stack** — have you shown what this would normally cost before revealing your price?
- [ ] **Aggressive price** — is your front-end price as compelling as it can be?
- [ ] **Payment plan** — is there an installment option available?
- [ ] **Premiums** — do you have at least one bonus so valuable it could justify the price alone?
- [ ] **Guarantee** — is it specific, performance-based, and more aggressive than "we'll try our best"?
- [ ] **Scarcity** — is there a genuine and communicated reason to act now?
