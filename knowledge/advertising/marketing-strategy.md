# Marketing Strategy

*Sources: Sabri Suby*

---

## Core Philosophy

- **"What you say" beats "how you say it" every time.** Most marketers obsess over ad structure, platform hacks, funnel design. The ones who win obsess over the market itself — knowing the customer so well that the message strikes like lightning.
- **The best marketers don't guess what customers want.** They live inside the mind of their dream prospect and know exactly what it is.
- **Marketing is not static.** Markets move. New competitors enter. New technology shifts behavior. Research is a continuous, living process — not a one-time exercise.

---

## Pillar 1: Market Research

**Spend 80% of your time on this. Nothing else comes close in ROI.**

### How to do it
- Join the biggest Facebook groups in your niche and read everything
- Go deep on the relevant Reddit subreddits
- Study the comments — not the posts
- Sign up to competitor email lists, read their funnels, watch their VSLs
- Build intake forms that capture rich data when someone books a call (their words, their problems, their language)
- Use AI to analyze all of that data at scale

### What you're looking for
- The exact language your market uses to describe their problem
- What keeps them up at night (the emotional charge behind the problem)
- The 2–3 keywords that represent 80% of search volume in the market (their words, not yours)
- What they've already tried and why it failed

### The golden rule
**Never rewrite your market's language in your own words.** Copy their exact phrases — from comments, reviews, intake forms — directly into your ad copy, emails, and landing pages. The prospect reads it and thinks: "this person is inside my head."

### Staying current
Markets shift. Build a system (intake forms, ongoing community monitoring, AI analysis) that keeps feeding you fresh intel continuously. Reference this document every time you write copy.

## Pillar 2: The Market Pyramid

Most marketers only fight for the 3% ready to buy right now. The real money is in the other 97%.

```
         [3%]   Ready to buy NOW         → Red Ocean (everyone fights here)
        [7%]    Information gathering    → Orange Ocean (curious, interested)
      [12%]     Problem-aware            → Aware they have a problem, not searching for solutions
    [60%+]      Problem-unaware          → Don't even know they have a problem
```

**Your job as a marketer:** Move each section of the pyramid upward toward buying. Architect your funnel to serve the entire pyramid, not just the tip.

### Why the 97% matters
- The 3% converts easily — you don't need skill to sell to someone who already wants to buy
- The 97% is where fortunes are built — infinite market size, almost no competition
- By owning the 97%, you also capture the 3% because you dominate the entire buying journey
- The brand that educates and nurtures the 97% controls the market

### Sabri's story
He was running Google Ads for people searching "Facebook Ad agency" — competing in the red ocean. He switched to free value offers (reports, education) that attracted both information gatherers AND ready-to-buy prospects. His conversions went up because his offer was more compelling than a competitor's "book a call" for the same audience. He captured every layer of the pyramid.

## Pillar 3: The Three Oceans

| Ocean | Who's there | What they want | Competition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red | 3% ready to buy | Quick solution, fast close | Hyper-competitive, price-driven |
| Orange | 7% curious | Proof that solutions work | Moderate, attention-driven |
| Blue | 90% unaware/gathering | Education, storytelling | Almost none — but requires skill |

**Red Ocean:** Price competition. Everyone is here. Hard to differentiate.

**Orange Ocean:** These prospects have tried one or two solutions before. They're buyers — but not on trust alone. They need proof (case studies, testimonials, results). More skill required, but much bigger market.

**Blue Ocean:** Uncontested. No one is fighting for these people. Requires: storytelling, elaborate hooks, educating prospects about the problem and all available solutions, and what happens if they make the wrong choice. Much more skill — but infinite market size.

**Default strategy:** Lead with value. Educate first. Hard offers and sales messages are Red Ocean tactics. Value content, free reports, and education pull people from all three oceans into your funnel.

## Pillar 4: Dream Buyer (20/80 Rule)

- In every business, 20% of customers generate 80% of revenue
- Identify those 20% — what makes them different? What do they look like?
- Architect your entire funnel around that one dream buyer: ads, landing pages, VSLs, offers
- Forget everyone else in the market — you'll still get them, but your message will be magnetic to the one person who matters most
- When a prospect reads your copy and thinks "they're speaking directly to me, not a group," that's when you win

## The LIVO (Low Investment Value Offer)

A free piece of content that:
- Delivers incredibly high value on its own
- Answers the market's most pressing questions without requiring them to speak to a salesperson
- Captures leads from all levels of the pyramid (not just ready-to-buy)
- Establishes you as the educator and authority in the market

Example: Instead of "Book a call", offer "Download the free report: How to get to #1 on Google without an agency." This converts more than a straight sales CTA, attracts information gatherers AND ready-to-buy prospects, and puts you in control of the entire buying journey.

**The mechanism:** You capture their email → you educate and nurture → they move up the pyramid → they buy when ready. The competitor who only runs "Book a call" ads never even gets a chance with 97% of the market.

## Pattern Interrupt and Attention

*(These principles overlap with `facebook-ads.md` — confirmed by this source)*

- The human brain is a pattern recognition machine. It runs on autopilot through patterns all day.
- Anything new, unusual, or threatening to existing patterns forces attention — the brain flags it as "pay attention or my existence is threatened"
- If you don't disrupt the pattern, you will never get a stop-scroll
- **Make your ads not look like ads** — native content, human language, not polished corporate
- **Do the opposite of your competitors** — study what everyone else in your niche is doing and deliberately go the other direction. If everyone uses talking-head VSLs with ring lights and white backgrounds, do the opposite.

## Headlines (Confirmed from Facebook Ads — same source)

The headline is the single most important element. Everything else can be right and a bad headline will kill it.

A great headline must pass these criteria:
- **Budget** — is this information worth my time right now?
- **Unique** — is this something new, or have I seen it a thousand times?
- **Specific** — not vague generalities, hyper-specific to the exact problem
- **Useful** — how does my life get better if I read this?
- **Urgent** — why should I pay attention right now?

Inspiration source: same as Facebook Ads — tabloids, gossip mags, news sites, supermarket checkout aisle magazines.

## Competitor Research

- Pull all competitor ads (Facebook Ads Library), their VSLs, their funnels
- Study the language they use
- Then do the **opposite**
- Doing what everyone else does because "it must be working" is how you disappear into the noise
- Differentiation by definition means doing something different

## Business Metrics: CAC, LTV, Payback Period

Three numbers determine whether a business wins or loses. Engineer them in your favor.

### 1. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
Every dollar spent on acquiring a customer: ad spend, agency fees, sales team, tools. Total acquisition spend ÷ customers acquired.

### 2. Lifetime Value (LTV)
Total revenue ÷ number of customers over a 12-month period = average LTV per customer.

**Ideal LTV:CAC ratio: 3:1**
- 1:1 = losing money
- 3:1 = profitable and still scaling (sweet spot)
- 5:1 = under-spending; you could be acquiring far more customers

At 3:1, you're willing to spend 33% of LTV to acquire a customer. The goal is not the cheapest CAC — it's maximizing LTV so you can outspend every competitor.

⚠️ **Correction (source: Alex Hormozi, `paid-ads.md` § The Money):** LTV overstates the number that matters. Two businesses with identical LTV can have wildly different profit if their margins differ — a $500 LTV at 10% margin is only $50 of actual profit. Hormozi's LTGP (Lifetime Gross Profit, margin-adjusted) is the more accurate version of this same ratio. Use LTV:CAC here as a quick proxy when margin data isn't handy; use LTGP:CAC in `paid-ads.md` when it is.

### 3. Payback Period
How long does it take to recover your CAC as cash?

This is the hidden constraint most businesses ignore. You can have a healthy 3:1 LTV:CAC and still run out of cash if your payback period is 12 months — because you're funding acquisition costs months before revenue returns.

**Benchmarks:**
- 30 days = ideal (can run on a credit card cycle, no cash strain)
- 90 days = acceptable for service businesses
- 6+ months = cash trough — business needs working capital to scale

**The golden rule:** The business that can spend the most to acquire a customer wins. That means: maximize LTV, shorten payback period, outspend competitors into irrelevance.

### Manipulating Payback Period
- **One-click upsell** (e-commerce): immediately after purchase, offer more of what they bought or a complementary product at a discounted rate. Increases day-one revenue, shortens payback.
- **Setup fees / phase fees** (services): charge upfront for onboarding. Recovers acquisition cost faster.
- **Front-end loss leader + backend profit**: aggressive entry price brings volume in; upsells and renewals make the margin.

## Quick Reference: The Full System

```
1. Research the market obsessively (80% of your time)
      ↓
2. Identify the Dream Buyer (20% giving 80% of value)
      ↓
3. Map where the market sits (Pyramid: 3/7/12/60)
      ↓
4. Lead with value → capture all levels (LIVO)
      ↓
5. Educate and nurture toward buying (Blue Ocean play)
      ↓
6. Disrupt the pattern → stop the scroll → headline → click
      ↓
7. Convert with a Godfather Offer (see offer-creation.md)
      ↓
8. Maximize LTV, shorten payback, outspend competitors
```
