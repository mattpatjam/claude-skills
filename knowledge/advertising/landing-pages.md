# Landing Pages

*Sources: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads) + Sabri Suby*

---

## Source: Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads)

Once a prospect acts on your ad's CTA, capture their contact info. The simplest tool for this is a landing page — don't overthink it. The simpler the page, the easier it is to test. Focus on two things only: the words and the image.

**3 layouts that consistently work:**
- Headline + subhead + form
- Same, plus an image
- Same, plus 3 bullets

**Rules:**
- Match your ad exactly — same colors, same words, same person, same promise. If the ad promises X, the page has to deliver X. People forget this constantly and waste ad spend because of it.
- Keep it a continuous experience from click to close — no jarring shift in look or language between ad and page.
- More form fields = fewer but more qualified leads. Fewer fields = more volume, lower quality. Pick based on what you need right now.

**Action:** Build your first landing page now, matched word-for-word to your best-performing ad.

---

## Source: Sabri Suby (from `facebook-ads.md` Hack 6)

Most advertisers spend 80% of effort on ads and 10% on landing pages. If the ad and landing page feel disconnected, people bounce instantly.

**The rule:** Your winning ad headline should be mirrored on the landing page headline. Same language, same promise, same tone.

**Why the ad platform is the best split-testing tool for landing pages:**
- Your ad headline gets seen by thousands of people
- Only a fraction click
- You already know which headline won (it's the one getting clicks)
- Mirror that exact headline on the landing page — conversion rate will jump

Apply the same mirroring to sub-headlines and lead-in copy. Always run a minimum of 3 landing page variations.

---

## Confirmed (both sources agree)

Message-match between ad and landing page isn't optional — it's the single highest-leverage lever after the ad itself. Both sources independently land on the same rule from different angles: Hormozi via "match your ad exactly," Sabri via "mirror the winning headline." Treat this as confirmed truth, not a single-source opinion.
