# Facebook Ads

*Sources: Sabri Suby*

---

## Core Philosophy

- **Psychology over algorithms.** Platform mechanics change constantly; human psychology doesn't. The half-life of algorithm knowledge is short. The half-life of understanding what makes people click is infinite.
- **You are not competing with other advertisers.** You are competing with Netflix, TMZ, the UFC, and everything else on the internet. Your ad must hijack attention in that context.
- **Every ad has exactly two jobs:**
  1. Deliver news, value, or useful findings (what people are already clicking on)
  2. Sell the click — engineer so much curiosity that you get a disproportionate click rate vs. all other ads in the feed
- **Curiosity is the most powerful emotion.** Nothing else comes close. Stack it into every element.
- **Obsess over people, not settings.** The amateur media buyer with genius creative will outperform the expert media buyer with weak creative every time.

## The High-Value Dobie Framework

The single most important framework for ad creative.

```
[Blind Clickbait] ←————————————→ [Naked Benefit]
     Bad                                Bad
               ↓
         High-Value Dobie
    (Clickbait Intrigue + Targeted Benefit)
             This wins
```

- **Blind clickbait** ("I can't believe what Wonka Trump did") — gets everyone, converts no one. Wrong audience, wasted spend.
- **Naked benefit** ("Lose weight with our program") — right audience, no reason to click. Low CTR.
- **High-Value Dobie** — intrigue around the HOW + a specific targeted benefit. Example: "Supermodels apply these 3 simple tricks to look younger — click to find out what they are." Intrigue (mysterious tricks supermodels use) + benefit (look younger) + qualified audience (people who want to look younger).

The clickbait label has a negative connotation, but strip that away: it is literally bait that makes someone click. Platforms reward high CTR with better inventory and lower CPMs. You want to be click-bait.

## Creative Formats

Use a mix. Don't run all ads in the same format.

### 1. Rule Native
- A photo taken on your iPhone that looks like organic, user-generated content
- Does NOT look like an ad — platforms and users treat it as feed content
- Works in every market without exception
- Best paired with a strong headline overlay or secondary image
- Go-to format; most reliable across industries

### 2. Breaking News
- "Bombshell: investor breaks silence" / "Alarming discovery affects millions" style
- Evergreen format — people have been clicking this for 100+ years
- Works in every market
- Warning: don't make all ads breaking news. Rotate with other formats.
- Use an image that looks like a news screenshot or broadcast still

### 3. SMS Screenshot
- Fake or simulated text conversation relevant to your offer
- Customizable to any scenario (home, health, finance, business)
- Works because it's hyper-native — looks like something a friend sent you

### 4. Native Social Post (LabBible style)
- Looks like a shared post from a large Facebook page
- Dual-image strategy: primary image + smaller secondary image
- Forces the brain to process two things simultaneously = pattern interrupt
- Study LabBible, Unilad, 9GAG for the format and tone

## Headline Frameworks

Headlines are the highest-leverage element. Study what already gets engagement, then adapt.

### Sources of Inspiration
Go to these before writing a single headline:
- **LabBible, Unilad, Daily Mail, Yahoo, People, US Magazine, Wall Street Journal**
- **Facebook Ads Library / Transparency Report** — see which ads get the most shares and engagement
- **Supermarket checkout aisle magazines** — decades of proven headline formulas
- Don't copy. Get your creative juices flowing, then make it applicable to your offer.

### Self-Interest Hooks (what people care about)
These are the permanent motivators. Frame your benefit around one:
- Richer / more money
- Stronger / faster / fitter
- Healthier
- Happier
- Sexier / more attractive
- Smarter
- Better relationships

### Specificity Rule
Specificity = believability. Always be specific.
- "Lose weight" → weak
- "Lose 12–19 kg in the next 5 weeks" → strong
- "Get more leads" → weak
- "92 inbound qualified leads per month" → strong

Specificity makes it feel real. Vague claims breed doubt.

### The Intrigue + Benefit Formula
Structure: `[Intriguing hook around HOW] + [Specific targeted benefit]`

Examples:
- "This client hack feels illegal — How to get 90–98 qualified leads per month"
- "Supermodels apply these 3 simple tricks to look younger — click to find out what they are"
- "Miami mom discovers mystery metabolism booster — here's what it is"

The intrigue creates the open loop. The benefit qualifies the audience. Both together = High-Value Dobie.

### Dual Image Pattern Interrupt
- Primary image that stops the scroll (mysterious, unusual, high contrast, unexpected)
- Secondary smaller image = forces brain to process two things = can't scroll past without engaging
- The brain is a dopamine-seeking machine. Give it two inputs to decode and it pauses.

## Ad Anatomy (In Order)

Every element must flow into the next. Each step should grease the slope to the next.

1. **Scroll-stopping image** — pattern interrupt; unusual, high-contrast, or native-looking
2. **Eyeball-grabbing headline** — High-Value Dobie formula; specificity + intrigue
3. **Slippery lead-in copy** — the text above the fold (first 1–2 lines before "See more"). This alone can 5x click-through rate. Make it impossible not to click "See more."
4. **Link description** — intriguing, not descriptive; "Watch carefully then click" style
5. **Body copy** — long-form, max 2,200 characters (Facebook + Instagram cross-platform limit); provides value + builds curiosity + sells the click
6. **CTA button** — "Learn more" only. Never "Sign up", "Get now", "Shop now", or "Download"

---

## Copy Rules

- **5th grade reading level** — same level as NYT bestsellers. Use Hemingway App to check.
- **Short sentences, short paragraphs** — eye relief over walls of text. One-liners are fine.
- **1–2 syllable words** — say "get" not "obtain", "fast" not "accelerate", "fix" not "remediate"
- **Write to ONE person** — use "you / your". Never "we, customers, our clients"
- **Value first** — provide news, a finding, something useful. Don't just pitch.
- **Sell the click, not the product** — the ad's job is to get the click. The landing page closes.
- **Don't make ads look like ads** — native content is the goal. Users avoid anything that looks promotional.
- **Add specificity and personality** — the more specific the claim, the more believable. Generic = doubt.
- **Max 2,200 characters** — keeps you running on all placements (Facebook, Instagram, etc.)
- **Write long, edit aggressively** — write to the right length first, then cut to 2,200. Don't fill space.
- **Mobile-first** — every sentence must read cleanly on a 6-inch screen. Short lines. No hyphenation.
- **Theme everything once you find a winner** — winning headline → align creative → align lead-in → align body copy → all pointing the same direction

---

## Positive vs Negative Hooks

- **Gain frame** ("Lose 20–50 kg with this one weird trick") beats fear/pain frame ("Don't let yourself gain 50 kg this winter") 8 out of 10 times
- Test both. Default to positive benefit.
- Fear can work in specific markets (health scares, financial risk) but requires testing

---

## Platform Mechanics (Secondary)

- High CTR → Facebook rewards you with better ad inventory and lower CPMs
- This is why the "sell the click" obsession pays off mechanically, not just psychologically
- Targeting: broad targeting with exceptional creative outperforms narrow targeting with weak creative. The ad does the targeting when it's good enough.

## Applicability to Google Ads

These principles transfer directly:

| Facebook principle | Google application |
|---|---|
| Headline curiosity (intrigue + benefit) | RSA headlines — lead with a specific benefit or open loop |
| Specificity rule | "92 leads/month" style claims in headlines and descriptions |
| 5th grade copy, short phrases | Ad descriptions — strip jargon, write like a person |
| Sell the click, not the product | CTR is Quality Score. High CTR = lower CPC. Same mechanic. |
| Positive gain frame | Test benefit-positive vs fear-avoidance headlines |
| One clear audience benefit per ad | Single theme per ad group — don't multi-message |

What does NOT transfer:
- Creative format (Native, Breaking News images) — Google Search has no images
- Dual-image pattern interrupt — not applicable to Search
- 2,200 char copy rules — Google has strict character limits per field

## Quick Reference Checklist

Before launching any Facebook ad, verify:

- [ ] Headline uses intrigue + specific benefit (High-Value Dobie)
- [ ] Image creates a pattern interrupt or looks native (not stock-photo polished)
- [ ] Lead-in copy is impossible to scroll past without reading more
- [ ] Body copy is at 5th grade reading level, short sentences, one person ("you")
- [ ] Copy provides value (news, insight, finding) — not just a pitch
- [ ] CTA is "Learn more"
- [ ] Gain frame used (unless testing fear frame)
- [ ] Specificity in the numbers/claims ("12 kg", "90 leads", "5 weeks")
- [ ] Total copy under 2,200 characters

## Meta Ad Scaling Tactics (2026)

*Source: Sabri Suby — second video on paid ads*

### Hack 1: Statics Beat Video

Static image ads outperform video more often than people expect, for two structural reasons:

1. **Volume:** Meta's Advantage+ (Andromeda) needs a constant feed of fresh creative or ads fatigue fast. Producing enough video to keep up is extremely expensive and slow. Statics can be produced in bulk, cheaply, in one session per week.
2. **Algorithm bias:** Meta can show far more static ads per user per session than video ads. Because Meta optimizes for revenue, their algorithm favors statics — more impressions available = better inventory = lower CPMs.

**Rule:** Dedicate one hour per week to producing fresh static creatives from your winning offer. One hour, every week, non-negotiable. A down-and-dirty native static will often outperform a polished 2.5-month VSL production.

---

### Hack 2: The One Keyword / Identity Trigger Hack

Advantage+ targets by analyzing your creative, copy, offer, and landing page — not just audience settings. To reach different audience pockets, you have to signal the audience in the creative itself.

**How it works:**
1. Take your best performing ad
2. Identify the top 20% of clients generating 80% of your revenue — what niches/industries/demographics are they in?
3. Duplicate the winning ad and insert a niche keyword into the headline or body copy

Example:
- Generic: "Here's how to get 462 leads per week on autopilot"
- Niche version: "Here's how to get 462 dental leads per week on autopilot"
- Niche version: "Here's how to get 462 property investment leads per week on autopilot"

The keyword acts as an **identity trigger** — the prospect recognizes themselves, and the algorithm learns which pocket of the audience to expand into. Same ad, different keyword = lower CPL, new audiences unlocked.

---

### Hack 3: AI Variation Scaling

Once you have a winning ad, don't just run it — manufacture 100+ variations of it using AI.

**The brief to give AI:**
> "Read this ad. Your job is to rewrite it. If I showed the next version to 100 people, not one of them could tell a different person wrote it. The voice, structure, and feel must be identical. Now write a version for [specific audience/niche]."

Start with body copy variations, then headlines, then the lead-in. Drop all variations into a CBO and let Meta find the winner. This compounds the one keyword hack — you're not just changing a word, you're creating fully cohesive niche-specific versions.

---

### Hack 4: Run Proven Organic Content as Ads

If a piece of content has already proven itself organically — high views, high engagement — it has cleared the most important test: real people chose to watch it without being paid to.

Take that organic content and run it as a paid ad. You're adding fuel to something already burning.

Sources to mine for proven content:
- Your own YouTube / Instagram / TikTok top performers
- Influencers and pages in your niche (for inspiration on format and topic, not copying)

---

### Hack 5: Broad Targeting + Super Specific Creative

Let the algorithm do the targeting. The creative specificity does the qualifying.

Run broad audiences (no demographic restrictions, no interest stacking) but make the creative hyper-specific to the niche or person you want to attract. The algorithm will find the right people through the content signal.

Counterintuitive result: broad targeting + specific creative often outperforms narrow targeting + generic creative.

### Hack 7: Retarget with a Different Offer

When someone doesn't convert from your first ad, don't just hit them with the same ad again. There's a reason they didn't buy. Serve them something different.

**Retargeting sequence:**
1. **Objection-handling ads** — take every reason people don't book a call / don't buy (sourced from intake calls and market research) and make ads that directly address those objections
2. **Proof / testimonial ads** — carousel ads with social proof, case studies, results. Show that your claims are real.
3. **Different offer ads** — retarget with a different product, service, or price point. Someone not ready for your main offer may take a lower-ticket entry point.
4. **Free audit / value call** — offer a free audit or consultation where the prospect gets genuine value regardless of whether they buy. This builds trust and feeds your market research simultaneously.

Put all retargeting ads into one CBO alongside your top performing prospecting ads. Let Meta allocate budget.

**Key insight:** Retargeting with different offers converts better than retargeting with the same offer at the same audience who already ignored it. People are in different stages of the market pyramid at different times.
