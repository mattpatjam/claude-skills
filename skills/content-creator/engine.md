# Content Creator — Engine Rules

*Fixed rules. Identical for every business this tool is ever configured for. Nothing in this file is edited per client — client-specific facts live in `clients/[business-name]-config.md` instead. If you find yourself wanting to change something here for one business, it belongs in their config, not here.*

Read this file in full alongside the business's config file before generating anything.

---

## CORE PURPOSE & PHILOSOPHY

Every piece of content exists for one reason: to genuinely help the reader. Not to game a platform's algorithm, not to flood a feed for visibility, not to sell — at least not directly.

Every post must serve at least one of these three purposes:

1. **Educate** — teach the reader something specific and actionable they didn't know before.
2. **Spark conversation** — ask a real question, share a real observation, or take a clear stance. The goal is comments and replies from people who found it relevant, not passive likes from people who didn't read it.
3. **Build trust over time** — each post is a small deposit in a credibility account. Consistency across weeks compounds; one clever post does not.

**Teach what, not how.** Show the insight and the outcome. Never hand over the complete tactical process a competitor could copy wholesale. The insight is the hook; the process is what the business's actual product or service delivers. "What separates a congress that works from one that just fills a room" is a post. "Our full 12-step congress planning checklist" is a giveaway that removes the reason to hire the business writing it.

**"How I/we" beats "how to."** Frame posts from the business's own real experience, not generic advice. "How we handled a 90-minute reconfiguration between sessions" beats "How to plan flexible event spaces." Personal or institutional framing, grounded in something that actually happened, is unchallengeable — no reader can argue with what a business actually did. Generic framing invites comparison and skepticism.

**Benchmark question, run before every post:** if a smart, experienced person in the target audience read this, would they stop scrolling — or scroll past? If the honest answer is "scroll past," rewrite it. Every time.

What this is NOT: a content calendar for vanity metrics, a place to recycle generic tips found on any blog, a megaphone for self-promotion, or a free manual that replaces the reason to hire the business. If a post doesn't pass the benchmark question, it doesn't go out.

## HOOK FRAMEWORK

Every post opens with a hook (first 1–2 lines, format rules below). The hook must be one of these three types:

1. A surprising or counterintuitive statistic
2. A bold, specific claim that challenges a common assumption
3. A question the reader immediately recognizes from their own experience

The hook must create a genuine curiosity gap — the reason someone clicks "see more" or keeps reading past the first line. No filler openers ("We are pleased to share," "Exciting news," "In today's fast-paced world"). No emojis in the first line.

**Seven attention drivers — combine 2–3 in a strong hook:**

1. **Recency** — "just happened" beats "happened five years ago," every time
2. **Relevancy** — speaks directly to the specific target audience's situation
3. **Celebrity** — names a known person, client, or entity where relevant and true
4. **Proximity** — close to home geographically or professionally
5. **Conflict** — opposing ideas, opposing people, tension between two forces
6. **Unusual** — rare, bizarre, one-in-a-million, counterintuitive
7. **Ongoing** — a story still in progress, "what happens next" energy

*(Source: `Content.method.md` — Hormozi's headline-characteristics framework.)*

**Deeper hook sources** — once a business has enough brand material (a stated framework, an offer, a clear position against how things are usually done, a documented milestone), hooks can also pull from:

- The business's own frameworks or methodology
- The "old broken way" they're positioned against
- A specific transformation or milestone they've delivered
- An identity shift for the reader ("you're now someone who…")
- Client stories (anonymized) with a real "before/after"

*(Source: `expert-secrets-reference.md` §15, "Where Hooks Come From.")*

## BODY STRUCTURE

**Default: the 3-beat story arc.** Every body moves the reader through three beats:

1. **Open on tension or a gap** — something the reader believes that's wrong, a situation they recognize, a cost they didn't know they were paying
2. **Move through the insight or turning point** — what's actually true, what changed, what the business learned
3. **Close on a resolution or lesson** — what to do with this now, feeding directly into the CTA

For list-style posts, anchor the list inside this same arc: open with the problem that makes the list necessary, deliver the list, close with the lesson. Never lead with the list itself.

**Retention mechanics — three tools, mix them:**

- **Lists** — things, facts, tips, opinions presented in order. Flexible, works for almost any pillar.
- **Steps** — actions in a specific sequence toward a goal. Stronger completion pull ("I need to know all the steps").
- **Stories** — real or anonymized events with a lesson. Highest emotional pull, "what happens next" tension.

*(Source: `Content.method.md` — Hook → Retain → Reward, the Retain stage.)*

**Deeper story structure — for narrative-heavy pillars** (anonymized client stories, behind-the-scenes, in-progress content), the 3-beat arc can expand into the fuller 5-phase Epiphany Bridge structure:

1. **Backstory** — where things stood before (the pain, the failed attempt, the frustration)
2. **Journey** — what was tried that didn't work; the "villain" is a false belief or a broken approach, never a named person
3. **New opportunity** — the moment the old path was abandoned for something different
4. **Framework** — the decision or mechanism that made the shift possible
5. **Achievement** — where things stand now, stated plainly, no hype

Two techniques that make this land:

- **Oversimplify.** Write at a level a smart generalist can follow without industry jargon. If a concept requires specialized vocabulary, stop and translate it.
- **"Kinda like" bridge.** When explaining something abstract or technical, translate it into something the reader already understands via analogy, rather than explaining the mechanism directly.

*(Source: `expert-secrets-reference.md` §7 and §15, "The Epiphany Bridge.")*

**Formatting, regardless of which structure is used:**
- No paragraph longer than 3 lines
- One idea per paragraph
- Bullet points when presenting more than two items
- Active voice throughout
- Exactly one specific data point, credential, or concrete example per post — not more

## PLATFORM FORMAT RULES

Length, hook cutoff, and hashtag count are set automatically from the business's configured platform(s) and content goal (setup interview items 3 and 4). **Length depends on platform AND content goal together, not platform alone** — a promotional email and a thought-leadership newsletter on the same platform need very different lengths.

| Platform / format | Length target | First-line cutoff | Hashtags | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn text post | 1,300–1,900 characters | ~200–210 characters before "see more" | Max 3 | Comments weigh far more than likes in the ranking signal; dwell time matters more than any single reaction type. Posts under 500 characters read as low-effort. |
| LinkedIn article | 1,900–2,200 words | — | — | Longer-form, same voice and rules as posts |
| Instagram caption | 125–220 characters typical; mix ~60% short (<150 chars) / 30% medium (150–300 words) / 10% long (700–2,200 chars) across a content run | ~125 characters before "more" cutoff | 3–5 | Hook → Value → CTA structure outperforms |
| Facebook organic post | 40–80 characters | — | minimal/none | Text-only posts underperform video now — keep any text post brief |
| Promotional/marketing email | 50–125 words (75–100 word sweet spot) | Subject line under 44 characters | — | Short and single-CTA; longer promotional copy underperforms |
| Informational/thought-leadership newsletter | 800–1,200 words | — | — | Audience-dependent — executive audiences prefer shorter (~3-minute) reads even within this range |

**Verify every fact rule.** Only use facts traceable to the business's configured trusted-sources list (setup item 9). If no list was provided, general web search is acceptable but treat it as lower-confidence and say so internally before using a stat. Never fabricate a statistic, citation, or data point. If a fact can't be verified, write from the business's own expertise and credentials instead of forcing in an unverified number.

**Zero external links in the post body.** If a source needs citing, it goes in a comment after posting, not in the body.

**Emojis:** match the business's configured tone. Institutional/formal tones: zero, or max 1 and never in the first two lines. Personal/casual tones: up to 3, never in the first line, only when they add clarity, not decoration.

## CTA / CLOSE RULES

**Default close:** a genuine, low-friction question that invites real response. Peer-to-peer tone, curiosity not command. Never "Like and share," "Follow for more," "Link in bio," or "Check out our website."

**PS statements.** A PS line at the end of a post or email is the second most-read part of any piece of written content, after the hook. Use it for a secondary thought, a light callback to the hook, or (if the business's purpose includes an ask) the one place the ask can sit without disrupting the body.

*(Source: `Content.method.md` — Give:Ask Ratio section.)*

**How often an ask is allowed — the Give:Ask Ratio.** Calibrated by the business's configured purpose (setup item 2):

| Mode | Ratio | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 100% ask | 0:1 | No audience built |
| Mature/monetizing | ~4:1 | Sustains an existing audience, doesn't grow it |
| Growth | 10:1 or higher | Grows fast, undermonetizes short-term |
| Pure give | ∞:1 | Maximum growth, hardest to sustain a business on alone |

If the business's configured purpose is pure brand-awareness/thought-leadership, default to pure-give (no asks at all, ever, in the body). If purpose includes lead-gen, promotion, or event-driving, use one of two methods:

- **Integrated** — a small CTA embedded inside an otherwise valuable piece (e.g. a PS line), keeping the give:ask ratio high within that single piece.
- **Intermittent** — dedicate a full post to an ask only every Nth cycle (N derived from the business's configured cadence), formatted with the same hook/body/CTA structure as every other post, never a bare pitch.

*(Source: `Content.method.md` — Give:Ask Ratio and the two ask methods.)*

## TWO-OPTIONS RULE

Every content type produces **exactly two versions per occurrence** — not just social posts. A LinkedIn post gets 2 options. A newsletter issue gets 2 draft versions. Any other content type a business's config defines gets 2 versions too. Each version uses a different content pillar or a meaningfully different angle — never two versions of the same idea with different wording. This is a hard output-format rule, not a suggestion, and it does not vary by content type. If two drafted versions are too similar, regenerate the weaker one before showing them.

## CONTENT CALENDAR / BATCH GENERATION

A business's config can define more than one content type on more than one cadence (see `config-template.md` section 9) — e.g. two LinkedIn posts a week and one weekly newsletter. When more than one content type is due in the same session:

- The per-generation interview (the 3 session questions) is asked **once per session, not once per content type.** The same answers feed every deliverable due that session.
- Research (Step 3 in `SKILL.md`) also runs once per session and gets reused across all deliverables, rather than re-researched per content type.
- Generation still runs separately per content type, since each one has its own format rules from the platform benchmark table — but all deliverables in a session draw from the same session input and research.
- Each deliverable still gets its own two versions, checked against its own pillar rotation and its own post-log history (a newsletter shouldn't repeat a newsletter angle from last month; a LinkedIn post checks against LinkedIn post history — they don't cross-contaminate each other's rotation).

## OPTIONAL MODULE: EMAIL / NEWSLETTER SEQUENCES

Activated only when the business's configured platform includes an email nurture sequence or multi-part newsletter series — not for single social posts.

**SOAP Opera 9-email structure** for a new-subscriber sequence:

| Emails | Focus | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Emotion | One core story per email, building toward the offer. Email 5 introduces the full offer/stack. |
| 6–7 | Logic | Facts, data, results, proof — the rational case. |
| 8–9 | Fear/urgency | What's lost by not moving. Real cost of inaction, not manufactured pressure. |

Each email ends by teasing the next one, so the reader wants to open the following email — this is the "soap opera" hook mechanic. Use for onboarding/welcome sequences specifically, not for regular newsletter issues (those follow the standard informational-newsletter length/format rules above).

*(Source: `expert-secrets-reference.md` §15, "SOAP Opera Email Sequences.")*

## MULTI-PLATFORM SEQUENCING

If a business's setup selects more than one platform, default to mastering one platform before generating content for a second (Depth-then-Width), rather than running all platforms simultaneously from day one — unless the business's setup explicitly requests parallel rollout across platforms.

*(Source: `Content.method.md` — Depth then Width vs. Width then Depth.)*

---

## NICHING

Keep the business's configured target audience narrow at first rather than defaulting to the broadest plausible description of who the content is for. Own a specific, narrow audience segment credibly before the config is later broadened. This shapes how the audience field in the business's config gets applied during generation, not something to re-ask about each session.

*(Source: `Content.method.md` — Puddles → Ponds → Lakes → Oceans.)*

## QUALITY CHECKLIST — RUN BEFORE FINALIZING EVERY POST

Voice and identity:
- [ ] Has the business's own recent post history (post log) been checked to avoid repeating a topic, pillar, or angle?
- [ ] Has the research step run (business's own history, industry scan, trusted sources)?
- [ ] Does the post sound like it was written by someone who actually does this work, not a generic content team?
- [ ] Free of promotional language and category clichés?

Content and purpose:
- [ ] Does this post educate, spark genuine conversation, or build trust? (At least one — if none, rewrite.)
- [ ] Would the target audience stop scrolling for this, or scroll past?
- [ ] Is there exactly one specific, verifiable fact or concrete example?
- [ ] Is every fact traceable to a verified source, or clearly written from the business's own expertise instead?
- [ ] Does it teach what, not how — insight and outcome shown, full process withheld?

Format:
- [ ] Is the hook within the platform's cutoff length?
- [ ] Does the hook create a genuine curiosity gap?
- [ ] Is total length within the platform/content-goal target from the table above?
- [ ] Are all paragraphs 3 lines or fewer?
- [ ] Is the CTA a genuine low-friction question, not a command?
- [ ] Zero external links in the body?
- [ ] Emojis within the tone-appropriate limit and absent from the first line(s)?
- [ ] Hashtags within the platform's limit and genuinely relevant?

Two-options check:
- [ ] Do both options use different pillars or meaningfully different angles?
- [ ] Would the business have a genuine choice between two distinct approaches, not two rewordings?

If any item fails, rewrite before outputting. No exceptions.

## HUMANIZER PASS — MANDATORY, NO EXCEPTIONS

Before any post is shown to the business for approval, run it through the `humanizer` skill. This is not optional and never needs to be asked for — it runs automatically on every chosen or drafted post, every time. Matches the standing rule that humanizer runs on all client-facing content in this workspace.

**Portability note:** "run it through the humanizer skill" only means something inside Claude Code, where skills are a native concept. Anywhere else this system runs (Renner or otherwise), the actual rule set at `.claude/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md` — the full pattern list plus its before/after examples — must be pulled in and embedded directly as its own pass (a separate system prompt on a second generation call, not folded into the main one). ## ROTATION / NO-REPEAT LOGGING

Before generating, read the business's post log (last 12 entries **for the content type currently being generated**) from `clients/[business-name]-post-log.md`. Each entry: date / content type / pillar / target reader / one-line hook summary.

Filter to the content type being generated before building the "do not repeat" block — a newsletter's rotation check only looks at past newsletter entries, a LinkedIn post's rotation check only looks at past LinkedIn entries. Different content types don't share a rotation history even if they're for the same business and the same week.

Inject the filtered last 12 entries as a "do not repeat these angles" block before generation. If a planned angle, hook, or target reader closely resembles a recent entry, find a meaningfully different one before proceeding.

After the business picks which version(s) to use, append a new entry per chosen piece to the post log immediately — a session covering 2 LinkedIn posts + 1 newsletter appends 3 entries, not 1.
