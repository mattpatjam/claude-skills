## CORE PURPOSE & PHILOSOPHY

These posts exist for one reason: to genuinely help people.

Not to game the LinkedIn algorithm. Not to flood a feed with content for the sake of
visibility. Not to sell — at least not directly.

Every post must serve at least ONE of these three purposes:

1. Educate — Teach the reader something specific and actionable they didn't know before.
   If someone reads the post and thinks "I never thought about it that way" — it worked.

2. Spark conversation — Ask a real question, share a real observation, or take a clear
   stance. The goal is comments, debate, and DMs from people who found it relevant —
   not passive likes from people who didn't read it.

3. Build trust over time — Each post is a small deposit in a credibility account.
   The reader who sees 20 posts over 10 weeks and thinks "this person really knows what
   they're talking about" is worth more than 1,000 empty impressions.

**The teach what, not how rule:**
Posts show the insight and the outcome. They never walk through the complete tactical process.
The insight is the hook. The process is what people pay for.

**Give-ask ratio:**
This account is in pure-give mode. Every post is a deposit — no sales asks, no "DM me to work together", no "check out my services." CTAs are conversation invitations only: genuine questions, requests for opinions, peer-to-peer dialogue. An ask post is always premature on a growing account. If it feels like a soft pitch, rewrite it.

"What makes a buyer ready to sign" is a post. "Here are the 7 steps to qualify a buyer" is a manual.
"Why I never send a price before asking two questions" is a post. The two questions stay private.
"This type of buyer closes in under 3 weeks — here's how I spot them early" is a post.
The identification method is what clients hire Matthieu to do.

The desire for the how is the reason people reach out, DM, or book a call.
If a post teaches the full playbook, it removes the reason to hire the person who built it.
Teach the principle. Name the outcome. Hold the execution.

What this is NOT:
- A content calendar for vanity metrics
- A place to recycle generic real estate tips found on any blog
- A megaphone for self-promotion
- Filler — if a post doesn't pass the "would I actually stop scrolling for this?" test,
  rewrite it
- A free manual that replaces the need to hire Matthieu

The benchmark question before every post (see "Who This Is For" above):
"If a foreign buyer or investor seriously evaluating property in Bali or Lombok read
this, would they find it worth their time — or would they scroll past?"

If the honest answer is "scroll past" — rewrite it. Every time.

## POST FORMAT & STRUCTURE — FOLLOW THIS PRECISELY

### CHARACTER COUNT
Target: 1,200 to 1,600 characters
This length maximizes LinkedIn dwell time and triggers the "See More" button
which signals high-value content to the algorithm.
Never exceed 1,600 characters.
Never go below 1,000 characters.

### THE HOOK (Lines 1—2)
- Must be under 200 characters total
- Must work as a "Scroll-Stopper" — one of these three types:
  a) A surprising or counterintuitive statistic
  b) A bold, slightly provocative claim
  c) A painful question the reader immediately recognizes
- Must create an open loop — a curiosity gap that can only be closed by reading more
- The first 3 lines together must make it impossible NOT to click "See More"
- No emojis in the first line
- No "I am thrilled", no "exciting news", no throat-clearing
- Must feel consistent with how Matthieu naturally opens his posts

**Headline components — use one or more to make the hook land:**
A meta-analysis of what drives the most attention identified seven components. The best hooks combine two or three:
1. **Recency** — as recent as possible ("this week", "last month", "just changed")
2. **Relevancy** — personally meaningful to the reader's situation
3. **Celebrity** — references a known person, entity, or event
4. **Proximity** — close to home geographically or professionally
5. **Conflict** — opposing ideas, opposing people, tension between two forces
6. **Unusual** — odd, rare, counterintuitive, or surprising
7. **Ongoing** — a story still in progress, with a plot twist possible

Strong hook examples:
"Most foreign buyers in Bali lose money before they even sign anything."
"A leasehold villa is not an investment. Here is what it actually is."
"The most dangerous word in Bali real estate is 'freehold'."

Weak hook examples (never do these):
"Today I want to talk about something important."
"As a real estate professional in Bali, I often get asked..."
"In today's market, it is more important than ever to..."

### THE BODY
- No paragraph longer than 3 lines — white space is not optional, it is strategy
- One idea per paragraph — never stack two points in the same block
- Use bullet points or numbered lists when presenting more than two items
- Maximum 5 bullet points per list — if you have more, cut ruthlessly
- Include exactly one specific, verified data point or concrete example
  to anchor credibility
- Write as if speaking to a smart peer over coffee — direct, clear,
  occasionally witty
- Active voice throughout — "buyers overlook this" not
  "this is often overlooked by buyers"
- Reference Matthieu's professional experience or perspective naturally
  where it adds credibility — never force it

**Retention mechanics — keep curiosity unresolved until you're ready to close:**
The body works by keeping an unresolved question in the reader's mind. Three tools to do this, each with a different strength:
- **Lists** — things, facts, tips, opinions presented one after another under a theme. Flexible order. Works for almost any pillar. Best for Educator and Perspective posts.
- **Steps** — actions in a specific sequence that accomplish a goal. More explicit reward ("I need to know all the steps"). Best for Educator posts with a process.
- **Stories** — events (real or anonymized) with a lesson. Highest emotional pull. Reader wants to know what happens next. Best for Human and Journey posts.
Mix them: stories that reinforce a step, lists with a story anchor, steps inside a narrative. More layers = more curiosity held.

**Story arc (applies to all pillars):**
The body must move the reader through three beats: open with a tension or gap (something they believe that's wrong, a situation they recognize, a cost they didn't know they were paying), move through the insight or turning point (what's actually true, what changed, what Matthieu learned), and close on a resolution or lesson (what to do with this now). The resolution feeds directly into the CTA. For Educator posts structured as lists, anchor the list inside a story — open with the problem that makes the list necessary, then deliver the list, then close with the lesson. Never lead with the list itself.

### THE CLOSE & CTA
- End with a low-friction question that invites genuine response
- The question must feel natural, not like a marketing tactic
- Use equal-to-equal language — curiosity, not command. Talk with the reader,
  never down to them, whether they turn out to be a buyer or a peer

Strong CTA examples — favor ones that let a buyer/investor self-identify in the
comments, not just a peer:
"If you're mid-search right now, what's the part nobody's warned you about?"
"What's stopping you from pulling the trigger here, price, trust, or something else?"
"Curious what's confusing you most if you're looking at Bali right now, drop it below."
"Have you seen this play out differently? I'd genuinely like to know." (works for either
audience — fine as an occasional variation, not the default)

Weak CTA examples (never do these):
"Like and share if you found this useful!"
"Follow me for more content like this."
"Link in bio."
"Check out my website."

No external links in the post body — ever. Links go in comments only.
