## 3. 2026-10-03 · 1.60% engagement · 500 impressions

Branded residential communities in Bali can sell for close to double the price/m2 of an identical unbranded villa down the road.

New BCG data explains exactly why.
BCG's global real estate research found that branded developments, the kind partnered with a recognizable hospitality or lifestyle brand, rent for 20 to 30% more than comparable unbranded properties. Sales premiums can run as high as double.

The brand name itself is not what buyers are paying for. It is the guarantee sitting behind it, quality standards enforced from day one, construction kept on schedule, professional management already locked in instead of figured out later.

I have watched two nearly identical land plots in the same area of Bali sell six months apart. Same size, same view, same road access. The one inside a managed, branded development closed faster and at a meaningfully higher price per are than the standalone plot next to it.

The lesson here is not "only buy branded." It is that the infrastructure and guarantees wrapped around a property are worth real money, and most independent villas quietly offer none of it unless the buyer builds those guarantees in themselves.

Before you compare two properties on price alone, what guarantees is each one actually backing up?

## 4. 2026-10-26 · 1.34% engagement · 747 impressions

Most developers in Bali do not need another brokerage, all sending the same listing to the same buyers. They need a reel Partner, someone that works with them for the entire process, from Sales to Marketing to Management.

Three weeks into building one from the inside, here is what I think the job actually is.

Sales starts before the price is set. Right now pricing gets decided, then agencies are handed units and told to go. The read on who is actually buying in that area, at that size, at that price, arrives a year late in the form of unsold inventory. That read belongs at the start.

Marketing is not a listing with better photos/renders. It is Google, Meta, SEO and increasingly GEO, built to put qualified buyers in front of a sales team who know the project properly. Most of what passes for developer marketing here is a brochure and hope.

Management is the part everyone treats as someone else's problem. It decides whether the first ten owners recommend the project or warn people off it. Which decides how phase two sells.

Then the developer wonders why unit 14 sat for a year.
One partner across all three, fewer projects, paid on how the project performs rather than on units moved. That is slower, harder to scale, and it means turning work down.

It is also the only version where the people selling a project carry any consequence for how it turns out.
Developers, genuine question: which of those three is costing you the most right now?
