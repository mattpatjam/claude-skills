# Offer Creation Framework
## Source: acquisition.com Offer Creation Course + $100M Offers

---

## The Core Problem Hormozi Solves
Most people compete on price because their offer looks the same as everyone else's.
A Grand Slam Offer is so differentiated, price comparison becomes impossible.

---

## 1. Market Selection (Before the Offer)
Pick a market with these three traits:
- **Massive pain** — the problem is urgent and deeply felt
- **Purchasing power** — they have money or access to it
- **Easy to target** — you can find them cheaply (FB groups, LinkedIn, associations, etc.)

The four primary markets: Health, Wealth, Relationships, Happiness.
Niching down = less competition + higher perceived expertise + better margins.

**Hormozi's market scoring criteria:**
- Does the market have a burning problem? (not a vitamin, a painkiller)
- Are they already spending money to solve it?
- Can you reach them without massive ad spend?

---

## 2. Charge What It's Worth
- Most people underprice because they're afraid of rejection
- Price anchors perceived value — low price = low quality in buyer's mind
- The goal is NOT to be the cheapest. The goal is to be the obvious best value.
- **Price to the value delivered, not your cost or effort**

Premium pricing framework:
- What's the dream outcome worth to them? (in $ or life impact)
- Price at a fraction of that value
- Then justify with the offer stack

---

## 3. The Value Equation
```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

To increase value:
- Increase Dream Outcome (make the result bigger/clearer)
- Increase Perceived Likelihood (proof, testimonials, guarantees, credentials)
- Decrease Time Delay (faster results = more valuable)
- Decrease Effort & Sacrifice (do it for them, simplify, remove friction)

**Application rule:** Every element of your offer should move at least one of these four levers.

---

## 4. Offer Creation — The Stack

A Grand Slam Offer is NOT just a product. It's a stack of:

### Core Product/Service
What you actually deliver. Name the Dream Outcome, not the features.
- Bad: "12 coaching sessions"
- Good: "Get your first 3 clients in 30 days or we keep working until you do"

### Bonuses
Each bonus should:
- Solve a specific objection or friction point
- Have a standalone perceived value (name the value)
- Be logical stepping stones toward the core result

Bonus naming: "How to [achieve X] without [feared thing] even if [limiting belief]"

### Guarantee
See guarantees section below.

### Scarcity & Urgency
- Scarcity = limited supply (spots, units, access)
- Urgency = limited time
- Must be REAL or it destroys trust
- Soft urgency: cohort start dates, price increases, bonus deadlines

### Name
See MAGIC formula below.

---

## 5. Offer Creation — Part 2 (Specificity)
- Get hyper-specific on the outcome: not "more leads" but "14 qualified appointments per month"
- Specificity = believability
- Vague promises feel like hype. Specific promises feel real.
- Use numbers, timeframes, conditions wherever possible

---

## 6. Bonuses — How to Build Them
- Identify the top 3-5 reasons people don't buy (objections)
- Create a bonus that demolishes each objection
- Give each bonus a dollar value ("Valued at $X")
- Stack them AFTER revealing the core offer, BEFORE the price

Formula: "And you also get [bonus name] — which alone is worth $X — because [specific reason it solves their friction]"

---

## 7. Guarantees — Risk Reversal
Types of guarantees (strongest to weakest):
1. **Unconditional money-back** — full refund, no questions asked
2. **Conditional money-back** — refund IF you do X and don't get Y
3. **Outcome-based** — "we work until you get the result, no extra charge"
4. **Service guarantee** — "if you're not happy with X, we'll redo it free"
5. **Anti-guarantee** — explain why you don't offer one (builds trust through honesty)

**Key principle:** The guarantee should make the buyer feel stupid NOT to try it.
Transfer the risk from the buyer to you. The better your product, the more aggressive your guarantee can be.

---

## 8. Scarcity & Urgency
**Scarcity (supply-based):**
- Limited spots in cohort or program
- Only X units available
- Only working with X clients per month

**Urgency (time-based):**
- Bonus expires at [date]
- Price increases after [date]
- Enrollment closes [date]

Rules:
- Never fake it — fake urgency destroys long-term trust
- Always give a REASON for the scarcity/urgency (makes it believable)
- Restate the urgency at the END of your pitch, not just the middle

---

## 9. Naming — The MAGIC Formula
**M** — Magnet (what draws them in, the dream outcome)
**A** — Avatar (who it's for, stated or implied)
**G** — Goal (specific result/timeframe)
**I** — Interval (how long / how fast)
**C** — Container (what it is — program, system, blueprint, bootcamp, etc.)

Example: "The 6-Figure Agency Blueprint: Get Your First 3 Clients in 30 Days — For Coaches Who Hate Cold Calling"

Naming rules:
- Address the dream outcome AND the avatar
- Use numbers (specific = credible)
- Avoid jargon your buyer doesn't use
- Test multiple names — the name IS part of the offer

## 10. Real-World Examples & Case Studies

### Agency Revshare — Commoditized vs Grand Slam (real numbers)
Same $10k ad spend, same audience of 300k impressions.

**Old (commoditized) — $1k down + $1k/month retainer:**
- Response rate: 0.013% → 40 appointments → 30 show → 16% close → **5 clients**
- Revenue: $5,000 upfront. ROAS: 0.5:1 (lose money getting customers)

**New (Grand Slam) — pay once, no retainer, only pay per show, 20-client guarantee:**
- Response rate: 0.033% (2.5x) → 100 appointments → 75 show → 37% close (2.3x) → **28 clients**
- Price: $3,997 one-time (4x higher)
- Revenue: $112,000 upfront. ROAS: 11.2:1
- **Net result: 22.4x more cash from the same ad spend**

The math: 2.5x responses × 2.5x close rate × 4x price = 22.4x revenue.

### The Value Equation in Practice

**Meditation vs Xanax** — same dream outcome (relaxation, decreased anxiety), different value scores:

| Driver | Meditation | Xanax |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | 1/1 | 1/1 |
| Likelihood of achievement | 0/1 (hard to stick to) | 1/1 (take pill = works) |
| Time delay | 0.5/1 (10-20 min, if not frustrated) | 1/1 (15 min) |
| Effort & sacrifice | 0/1 (daily commitment, discomfort) | 1/1 (swallow a pill) |
| **Total** | **1.5/4** | **4/4** |

This is why Xanax is a multi-billion dollar business and meditation companies rarely are. Supplements ($123B industry) are 2x the size of gyms ($62B) for the same reason — pill is easier than showing up.

**Fitness vs Liposuction** — same dream outcome (look thin), different perceived effort:
- Fitness: 12+ months, daily sacrifice, soreness, hunger, embarrassment, impermanence
- Liposuction: fall asleep, wake up thin, 2-4 weeks soreness
- That's why people pay $25,000 for lipo and resist $100/month gym memberships

**London Tube** — psychological vs logical solution:
- Logical: make trains faster (cost: billions)
- Psychological: add a dotted map showing when the next train arrives (cost: a few million)
- Result: bigger satisfaction increase from the map. Perceived time delay dropped more than actual time saved.

Same entrepreneur pivoted to automated mask manufacturing during COVID. Within 5 months: millions per month. Same skills, different market.

### Demand Curve Math — Why Limiting Supply Makes More Money
10 people willing to pay $500 for a workshop = $5,000.
Of those 10, typically 2 are willing to pay $5,000 = $10,000.

By selling to the 2 instead of the 10:
- 2x the revenue
- Lower costs (fewer people to serve)
- 8 people left with unmet demand — they want it more next time
- Price resistance drops at next launch

The 80/20 of demand: 1 in 5 prospects will pay 5x the price. Always.
