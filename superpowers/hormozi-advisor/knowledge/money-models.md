# Money Models Framework
## Source: acquisition.com Money Models Course

---

## Core Principle
How you charge is as important as what you charge.
Wrong money model = leaky bucket even with a great product.

---

## The 6 Ways to Make Money (Hormozi's Model Types)

### 1. One-Time Purchase
- Customer pays once, gets product/service
- Simple, predictable per transaction
- Problem: no recurring revenue, must keep acquiring new customers
- Best for: high-ticket one-time services, products, transactions
- **Real estate application:** villa sales commission

### 2. Subscription / Retainer
- Customer pays recurring fee (monthly/annual)
- Best model for building enterprise value
- Requires ongoing value delivery to prevent churn
- Key metric: MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue) and churn rate
- **CRM application:** monthly SaaS subscription
- **Scalrize application:** monthly content retainer

### 3. Revenue Share / Performance
- You earn % of results you generate for client
- High trust barrier to entry, but highest alignment
- Works when you can directly attribute revenue to your work
- Risk: client attributes less to you than you do
- **Real estate:** commission on deal close

### 4. Licensing
- Sell rights to use your system/brand/IP
- High margin once built, scales without proportional effort
- **CRM opportunity:** white-label licensing to agencies

### 5. Consulting / Done-With-You
- Sell access to your expertise + client does the work
- Lower margin than DFY but more scalable than 1:1
- Works well as an upsell to a course or lower-ticket product

### 6. Done-For-You (DFY)
- You do the work entirely
- Highest price, hardest to scale, highest client satisfaction
- **Scalrize current model:** DFY content creation

## Pricing Architecture — The Ladder

The goal is to have multiple products at different price points so you can:
- Capture buyers who can't afford the premium yet
- Upsell buyers naturally over time
- Increase LTV of each customer

Example ladder for Scalrize:
```
Free: LinkedIn content checklist (lead magnet)
      ↓
$97/mo: Content templates + prompt library (self-serve)
      ↓
$497/mo: Done-with-you (you get the prompts + monthly review)
      ↓
$2,000/mo: Done-for-you (full content calendar + posts written)
      ↓
$10,000+ setup: Full AI workflow implementation
```

---

## Pricing Psychology Rules

1. **Anchor high, then offer a "deal"** — always show the value/price before the actual price
2. **Odd numbers feel more precise** — $497 > $500; $1,997 > $2,000
3. **Annual = more committed customer** — offer discount for annual upfront (10-20%)
4. **Tiered pricing = self-segmentation** — let buyers choose their tier, it reveals their budget
5. **Price anchoring with bonuses** — stack bonus values before revealing price

## Unit Economics — What to Track

### CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
Total spend to acquire one customer (ads + time + tools)
Rule: CAC should be recovered within 3 months at most

### LTV (Lifetime Value)
Total revenue from one customer over their full relationship
Rule: LTV:CAC ratio should be 3:1 minimum, 5:1+ is healthy

### Payback Period
How long to recoup the cost of acquiring a customer
Rule: < 12 months for most businesses; < 3 months for cash-flow health

### Gross Margin
Revenue minus direct cost of delivery
Rule: SaaS/digital = 70%+; Services = 50-70%; Physical = 40-60%

## Upfront Cash — How to Collect Fast

### Order Bumps
Small offers presented immediately after the main purchase. Customer is already in buying mode — low friction, high take rate.
- "Fries with your burger" — they already said yes, offer something useful and cheap
- Works in every niche: fitness, coaching, SaaS, services

**Real example:** Gym owner selling $999 for 12 weeks. Added order bumps at checkout (meal plans, supplements, private coaching) at $50-200 each. Total cart value jumped without touching the core offer.

### Payment Plans — Charge More for Flexibility
People who want to pay in installments pay extra for that option. Cover risk and protect cash flow.
- Rule: if the offer is $1,000 upfront, payment plan = $1,200 total
- This is "price for pain" — flexibility costs something

**Real example:** $5,000 coaching program with low conversions. Restructured as $1,750/month × 3 = $5,250. Added $97 order bump, $297 upsell, $997 back-end. Result: conversions doubled, cash flow tripled.

### The Upfront Cash Stack (Gym Owner Case)
- Main offer: $999 for 12 weeks (vs $99/month)
- Anchored at $2,000, dropped to $999 (felt like a steal)
- Order bumps: $50-200 per buyer
- Payment plan: $399/month × 3 for those who needed it
- Result: big cash upfront + predictable installment flow

## LTV Compounding Math — Real Numbers

**Same 1,000 customers, different monetization:**

| Layer | Action | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Front end only | 1,000 × $100 | $100,000 |
| + Back-end offer | 30% take $500 upgrade | +$150,000 |
| + Subscription | 50% stay 6 months × $50/mo | +$150,000 |
| **Total** | Same 1,000 customers | **$400,000** |

No new customers required. 4x revenue from monetizing what you already have.

**Value ascension ladder example:**
Free ebook → $97 course → $997 program → $5,000 mastermind → $50,000 done-for-you

Each step: deliver results first, then offer the next level. Never pitch before the win.

## Unit Economics — Real Cases

### SaaS: Fixing a Broken CAC/LTV Ratio
- Before: CAC $300, LTV $400 — not enough margin to scale
- Fix: changed funnel, added order bumps, improved onboarding
- After: LTV jumped to $800, CAC dropped to $200 → 4:1 ratio → scalable

### Coaching: Collapsing a 6-Month Payback Period
- Before: 6-month payback period (too slow, too risky)
- Fix: added upfront offers + fast action bonuses
- After: payback dropped to 30 days → could reinvest in ads immediately

### Gym Chain: CAC by Location
- Tracked CAC per city. Found range: $50 to $150 depending on location
- Shifted budget to low-CAC locations
- Result: same total spend, significantly more profit

**Core rule:** The business that can spend the most to acquire a customer wins. Higher LTV = more you can spend = competitors can't keep up.

## 8 Money Model Mistakes (Common Killers)

1. **Misaligned incentives** — paying for effort not outcomes. Pay sales reps for revenue, not calls. Pay support for satisfaction, not speed. Pay marketers for leads, not likes.

2. **No tracking** — flying blind. Daily metrics: new leads, sales, refunds, ad spend. Weekly: funnel performance, email, support. Monthly: P&L, feedback, team.

3. **Overcomplicated offers** — 5 offers, 10 funnels, 20 upsells = customers confused, team stuck. Fix: one offer, one funnel, one message. Simple scales. Complex breaks.

4. **Ignoring customer feedback** — "feedback is free gold, most people throw it away." Surveys, exit interviews, support tickets. Find the patterns. Fix what's broken.

5. **Weak foundations** — no SOPs, no onboarding, no training, no clear roles. Scaled too early, broke everything. Fix the base first. "Don't scale chaos."

6. **Ignoring churn** — "most businesses lose more money from churn than from ads." Track it. Find exit reasons. Fight it with better onboarding, loyalty programs, exit surveys.

7. **No profit focus** — "Revenue is vanity. Profit is sanity. Cash is reality." Track net profit, not just revenue. Know your margins, cut waste.

8. **No direction** — drifting, reacting, copying. Set measurable goals: 30% revenue growth in 90 days. Reduce churn 10% this quarter. Share goals with the team.

## Scaling Checklist — Before You Spend More

Do not scale until you can check every box:

- [ ] Offer converts consistently (not just once)
- [ ] Delivery is smooth and repeatable
- [ ] Churn is low and understood
- [ ] Profit margin is real (not just revenue)
- [ ] Team has clear roles, goals, scorecards
- [ ] Systems (SOPs) are documented and tested
- [ ] You know your CAC, LTV, and payback period daily
- [ ] Cash runway to fund the growth

**If you can't check these: fix first, scale later. Scaling a broken system makes it more broken, faster.**

## Money Model Audit for Matt's Businesses

### Real Estate (Bali Exception Estate)
- Model: transaction commission (one-time)
- Problem: no recurring revenue, boom/bust cash flow
- Opportunity: retainer for ongoing property management referrals, or a buyer advisory retainer ("pay me $X/month, I find and screen deals for you")

### Bali Exception CRM
- Model: SaaS subscription (correct model)
- Key metric to target: < 5% monthly churn
- Pricing decision: per-seat vs. flat monthly vs. tiered by contacts/deals
- Recommendation: start with flat monthly (simple to sell), move to per-seat at scale

### Scalrize
- Current: DFY retainer (monthly)
- Gap: no lower-ticket entry point → losing prospects who can't afford DFY
- Opportunity: build the ladder (see above)
- Best margin move: productize the DFY into a system that can be partially automated
