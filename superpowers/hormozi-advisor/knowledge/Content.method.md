## Why Content Is the Multiplier

Brand is not a vanity play. It is a force multiplier on every other form of advertising (cold outreach, paid ads, warm outreach). Hormozi went from "content is a waste of time" to "brand is more important than everything" after watching The Rock, Conor McGregor, Kylie Jenner, and Logan Paul build billion-dollar businesses on audience alone.

- 78% of his gym launch customers had consumed 2+ pieces of long-form content before buying
- He had attributed that revenue to paid ads — the content was the real closer
- Content grows your outreach list (followers = warm leads you can DM)

**The rule:** Content makes everything else work better.

## The Content Unit: Hook → Retain → Reward

Every piece of content — from a tweet to a 3-hour podcast — is made of one or many content units. Each unit has three jobs:

### 1. Hook
Get them to stop and redirect attention toward you.

**Three levers:**

**A. Topics** (5 types)
1. **Far past** — life lessons, big mindset shifts, defining moments
2. **Recent past** — review your calendar or last week; what happened that others would find interesting
3. **Present** — real-time capture. Email yourself ideas as they happen. Use Twitter/X to test in real time
4. **Trends + your expertise** — only comment on trends you're actually qualified on
5. **Manufactured** — engineer the experience ("I lived on $100/month for a year"). Hormozi skips this one personally

**Favorite shortcut:** Do things → talk about what you did → repeat.

**B. Headlines** (7 characteristics of high-performing headlines)
1. **Recency** — "just happened" beats "happened 5 years ago" every time
2. **Relevancy** — nurses click nurse content; address your specific audience in the headline
3. **Celebrity** — name-drop relevant authorities, clients, or well-known figures
4. **Proximity** — make it close to home geographically or emotionally
5. **Conflict** — opposing ideas, opposing people, values in tension (good vs evil, freedom vs security)
6. **Unusual** — rare, bizarre, one-in-a-million. The circus freak show principle
7. **Ongoing** — live coverage, evolving story, "what happens next" dynamic

Stack as many of these as possible in one headline.

**C. Format**
Match what has rewarded your audience on that platform before. Study top-performing content on the platform and make yours look like it — same style, energy, structure. Same video reformatted differently for Reels vs TikTok vs YouTube Shorts.

---

### 2. Retain
Keep them consuming. Use curiosity. Three structures:

- **Lists** — things, tips, facts, opinions in order. Flexible; partial consumption still gives value. "7 ways I invested $1,000 in my 20s"
- **Steps** — ordered actions to reach a goal. Less flexible but stronger completion incentive. "3 steps to creating a great hook"
- **Stories** — describe real or imagined events. Creates "what happens next" tension. Can be past, hypothetical, or fiction

Use all three together in one piece. Stories hook attention, steps or lists give structure, both keep people through.

---

### 3. Reward
Satisfy the reason they clicked. Keep the promise the hook made.

- Value per second is the metric — not length
- No such thing as too long, only too boring
- People don't have shorter attention spans — they have higher standards. The competition for their attention has increased. Netflix proves people binge for hours when content is good
- If you reward people consistently, your brand starts doing the work: they stop for your content automatically

If you open loops and never close them, they'll watch once and never return.

## Give:Ask Ratio

The most studied ratio in media. Television runs **3.6:1** (47 min content / 13 min ads per hour). Facebook runs **4:1**. These are mature, maximally-monetizing platforms — they're not growing fast.

**For growth:** Give until they ask. Give publicly, ask privately.

| Mode | Ratio | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 100% ask | 0:1 | No audience |
| 4:1 | Mature ratio | Monetize but don't grow |
| 10:1+ | Growth ratio | Grow fast, monetize less |
| 100% give | ∞:1 | Maximum growth, hard to survive |

- TikTok grew by running near-zero ads for years — pure give, compound sharing
- Give in public, ask in private = reputation stays pure; only buyers see the ask
- The moment you ask publicly is the moment you slow growth
- If your product is exceptional, an ask can still be a deposit — the buyer gets more than they paid and your brand grows

**Warning:** If you make money from an ask, do NOT ask more. Go back to giving. The vicious cycle is: ask → less response → ask more → audience shrinks → desperate asks → death.

## How to Ask (Two Methods)

### Integrated
Embed a CTA inside a valuable piece of content. Keep the give:ask ratio high within the piece itself.
- 60-min video with 30-sec plug = 59:1 ratio, fine
- After a valuable moment, or at the very end
- **PS statements** are the second most-read part of any written content (people read the top and skim to the bottom)

### Intermittent
Dedicate occasional full pieces to an ask. Every 10th or 50th post is an offer post — still formatted as hook/retain/reward but with an embedded CTA.
- Better for short-form where you can't maintain the ratio within a single piece
- Audience accepts it: "he's got to pay his bills, I get it"

**Lead magnet format:**
"I have 11 more tips on this — grab the visual at [link]"
Thank-you page shows paid offer.
Make the lead magnet match the content topic exactly.

**Direct offer format:**
"I'm looking for 5 people trying to achieve [outcome] in [timeframe] without [effort/sacrifice]. If you don't get [result], I'll [make it right]. DM me / book a call / comment below."

## Scaling Content

### Depth then Width (recommended for beginners)
1. Pick one platform → post content
2. Post regularly on that platform
3. Maximize quality and quantity on that platform
4. Add a second platform → repeat

**Pro:** Compounds faster, more efficient with limited resources
**Con:** No omnipresence, dependent on one channel

### Width then Depth (for more experienced creators)
1. Pick one platform → post
2. Post regularly
3. Immediately add the closest adjacent platform (e.g., Reels → TikTok → YouTube Shorts)
4. Continue across relevant platforms
5. Maximize all simultaneously

**Pro:** Broader audience faster, repurpose easily
**Con:** More skill, time, labor. Lots of bad content everywhere at first

**Rule:** Master one platform before touching a second.

## How Hormozi Actually Does It

- Sits down **twice a month**
- Records **~30 short clips** from reviewing calendar + tweets
- Records **2–4 long videos** that day, unpacking tweets with stories or depth
- Posts manually (not pre-scheduled) — the moment before publish forces one last quality check
- Posts reached **5–6M followers** with just this system in 2 years

## 7 Lessons for Making Content

1. **"How I" beats "How to"** — "How I built my agency" vs "How to build an agency." Less defensible, more relatable. People listen instead of trying to disprove you.

2. **People need to be reminded more than taught** — 1 in 6 of Hormozi's followers didn't know he had a book after 2 years of daily posts. Henry Ford thought his ad was boring before it even launched. Repeat yourself far more than feels comfortable.

3. **Puddles → Ponds → Lakes → Oceans** — Narrow your scope to dominate a small pond first. A 20-year-old life coach shouldn't make general life coaching content — they should talk about the specific people they've helped and the specific struggles they know. Expand credibility radius as you grow.

4. **Content creates sales tools** — Track which posts generate inquiries. Turn those into a "greatest hits" library for your sales team: one piece per common objection. Salespeople send specific content between calls to warm leads and overcome resistance.

5. **Free content retains paying customers** — Customers don't separate where they got value. If your free content is great, paying customers stay loyal longer because they feel the relationship delivering more than they paid for.

6. **No shorter attention spans — higher standards** — Don't blame algorithms. Don't optimize for short. Make genuinely good content and the platform rewards you.

7. **Avoid pre-scheduling posts** — Manually posting forces a last-second quality check. Hormozi's team finds manually posted content consistently outperforms scheduled content.

## Benchmarks

Measure **monthly**:
- **Total followers** across all platforms
- **Reach / impressions** — how many people see your content
- **Rate of growth** — % increase month over month

If audience is growing: good.
If growing fast: great.
If flat or shrinking: the content isn't good enough yet. Not the algorithm — the content.

**Mindset:** Compare your growth to your own last month. Not to someone 10 years ahead of you. Everyone started at zero.
